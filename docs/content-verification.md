# Product-sheet items to verify (dermocosmetics courses)

The courses use **only** what the Lotus product sheet states. The items below were unclear, ambiguous, possibly mis-typed, or very strong claims in the sheet, so the lessons use cautious wording. Please confirm each against current leaflets/brand documents, then correct the sheet or the lesson text (admin → lesson, or edit `content/courses-src` and re-run `npm run pack:courses && npm run import:courses`).

## 15-hair-scalp-care

### Author notes for the Lotus team (not part of the course)

Items in the product sheets ("Foltent", "Asepta", "ducray") that were unclear, duplicated or look like typos. The course uses cautious wording for each; please confirm.

- Sheet name "Foltent" and item names "Folten Conditionr", "Nail strenghther", "Nail Fortfying cream": spelling looks like typos. The course uses Foltène, Conditioner, Nail strengthener, Nail Fortifying cream. Please confirm the official item names.
- Foltène Shampoo Men/Women and Treatment Men/Women: the sheet gives the same usage for both versions and does not say how they differ. The course does not describe any difference.
- Foltène Eyelash Treatment: the sheet says "mascara to strengthen and treat the eyelashes". "Treat" is vague; the course avoids any growth claim.
- Asepta Ecrinal Ampouls and Lotion: "for all cases of hair loss" is very broad. The course attributes it to the notes and adds a doctor-referral caution.
- Asepta Ecrinal Shampoo: "free from chemical substances" is an absolute wording that may not be accurate; the course attributes it to the notes and adds a sensitivity caution. Please confirm intended meaning.
- Asepta Ecrinal Cream is for patchy loss, which is a doctor-referral red flag; the course tells staff to refer first and treat the cream as supportive care only. Please confirm this is acceptable.
- Asepta Nail Fortifying cream: "skin growths" (الزوائد الجلدية) is unclear (cuticle, hangnails, or something else). The course repeats the sheet wording and flags it.
- Asepta ANP statements: the sheet says ANP "prevents hair loss completely", "restores growth within only two months" and is "safe in pregnancy, breastfeeding and for children". These are strong claims and also differ from the 3-month treatment note. The course attributes them to the Lotus notes, does not turn them into guarantees, and asks staff to point to the leaflet and doctor. Please verify against the product leaflet and the Egyptian Drug Authority-approved wording. The "90% of the hair protein is keratin" figure is also restated only as the sheet's statement.
- Asepta sheet: the ANP note is split over two rows, and it is not stated which products contain ANP; the course does not say.
- Ducray Anaphase and Kelual: the sheet gives no product form (shampoo, ampoule, lotion, capsule) and no directions. The course says the form is not stated.
- Ducray Neoptide: the dosing text is garbled in places ("1 مل = 12 رشة لمدة شهريرش مساءا"; the word "شهريرش" looks like two words run together) and the row is duplicated (first as a short line, then as a long line). The course restates only the readable parts (3 sprays of 30 ml for 3 months, once daily, 1 ml = 12 sprays, sprayed in the evening on dry hair, washed two hours later) and points to the leaflet. Please confirm "for a month" and the number of sprays per day.
- Ducray Neoptide: the sheet does not state use in pregnancy, breastfeeding, men or children; the course says so.
- Ducray Elution shampoo: "the 3 months summer period" is ambiguous (3 months of continuous summer use, or use as a seasonal preventive?). No frequency is given. The course restates the sheet and tells staff to follow the label.
- Ducray Kertoyl "shampoo for psoriasis": the course tells staff not to diagnose and to refer undiagnosed thick, silvery scalps to a dermatologist.
- No prices or dosing frequencies are given in the sheets, so the course contains none. Please add the current Lotus price list separately if wanted.
