## Learning objectives

After completing this lesson, you will be able to:

1. Describe the customer journey in a Lotus pharmacy from first contact to follow-up.
2. Identify the touchpoints where the customer forms an opinion about the branch.
3. Recognise the "moments that matter" that decide trust or disappointment.
4. Explain what customers in Egypt expect from a trusted pharmacy.
5. Map the journey of a real customer visit and spot where service can improve.

---

## 1. What is the customer journey?

The customer journey is the full path a customer takes with us: from the moment they think of the pharmacy, through the visit or the order, to what happens afterwards. The customer does not judge us by one thing. They judge us by the **whole experience**: the entrance, the greeting, the wait, the advice, the price, the packing, the goodbye, and what we do if something goes wrong.

In Egypt the pharmacy is often the first place a family goes for a health question. People come worried, in a hurry, or carrying a prescription they do not fully understand. The experience we give is therefore part of care, not only part of selling.

## 2. The stages and touchpoints

A touchpoint is any moment when the customer sees, hears or deals with Lotus. Most journeys follow these stages.

| Stage | Typical touchpoints | What the customer is thinking |
|---|---|---|
| Before the visit | Sign, opening hours, phone, WhatsApp, a recommendation from a neighbour | "Is it open? Will they have it? Can I trust them?" |
| Arrival | Entrance, cleanliness, lighting, the greeting | "Do they notice me?" |
| Waiting | Queue, seating, acknowledgement | "Will this take long? Am I being ignored?" |
| Need discovery | Questions, listening, privacy at the counter | "Do they understand my problem?" |
| Advice and product | Explanation, alternatives, availability | "Is this right for me? Is the price fair?" |
| Payment | Price clarity, receipt, insurance or corporate card | "Am I being charged correctly?" |
| Leaving | Packing, instructions, goodbye | "Do I know how to use it?" |
| After the visit | Follow-up call, delivery, a reminder to refill, complaint handling | "Do they still care once I have paid?" |

## 3. Moments that matter

Not all touchpoints weigh the same. A few moments decide whether the customer trusts us.

1. **The first ten seconds.** A greeting and eye contact tell the customer they are welcome before anyone speaks.
2. **The moment of need.** When a customer describes a symptom or asks for a medicine, they want to feel heard. Interrupting or answering a phone call mid-conversation is damaging.
3. **The moment of "we do not have it".** How we handle unavailable stock decides whether the customer returns.
4. **The moment of price.** Surprise is the enemy. Say the price clearly before the customer reaches the cashier.
5. **The moment of a problem.** A wrong item, a delay or a doubt about a product. How we respond often matters more than the problem itself.
6. **The goodbye.** The last impression is what the customer remembers on the way home.

> A customer who has a problem solved well often trusts the pharmacy more than a customer who never had a problem.

## 4. What customers in Egypt expect from a trusted pharmacy

Expectations are shaped by culture and by experience. In most Lotus branches customers expect:

- **Respect and warmth.** A polite greeting, a calm tone, and addressing the customer respectfully in Arabic.
- **Professional advice.** A pharmacist who listens, explains clearly and refers to a doctor when needed.
- **Availability and honesty.** The right product, or an honest answer and a safe alternative with the customer's consent.
- **Speed without rushing.** Short waits, but no feeling of being pushed away.
- **Privacy.** Health matters are personal. Speak quietly and never discuss one customer's medicines in front of others.
- **Fair, clear prices** and correct change and receipts.
- **Quality and safety.** Clean shelves, products in date, medicines stored correctly.
- **Reliability after the sale.** Orders arrive as promised and complaints are taken seriously.

## 5. Mapping a journey

Journey mapping is a simple team exercise. Pick one common visit, for example a mother buying a child's fever medicine at an evening peak. Walk through every stage, and for each one write what the customer sees, feels and needs, what we do now, and one improvement. Do this in the team meeting with real branch examples.

**Checklist for a journey review**

- Can the customer find the branch and see that it is open?
- Is the entrance clean and the counter tidy?
- Was the customer greeted within a few seconds?
- Was the customer's need understood before products were offered?
- Was the price stated clearly?
- Did the customer leave knowing how to use the product?
- Is there a follow-up for orders, deliveries or ongoing treatment?

## 6. Common mistakes

- Treating the visit as only "the sale" and ignoring waiting and goodbye.
- Looking at the screen or phone instead of the customer.
- Discussing a customer's medicine loudly.
- Saying "no" or "not available" and stopping there.
- Assuming a quiet customer is a satisfied customer. Many simply do not come back.
- Forgetting that WhatsApp, phone and delivery are also part of the journey.

## Scenario

On a busy evening, a customer enters with a prescription and looks hesitant. Three people are ahead. One technician is on the phone, and the pharmacist is counselling another customer.

**Model approach.** A team member makes eye contact and says a short welcome. The pharmacist then says: "Welcome, I will be with you in a few minutes. You can give me the prescription now so we can check availability." The prescription is checked while the customer waits, so time is used well. At the counter the pharmacist lowers their voice, explains each medicine, says the total before payment, and offers a short written note of the doses. If one item is missing, the pharmacist offers a suitable alternative only after confirming the prescriber's rules and the customer's consent. At the door the customer hears a goodbye and an invitation to call if they have questions. Each of these small actions improves one stage of the journey.

## Key takeaways

- The customer judges the whole journey, not only the product.
- Every touchpoint, including phone, WhatsApp and delivery, builds or weakens trust.
- The first ten seconds, the moment of need, unavailable stock, price, problems and the goodbye are the moments that matter.
- Egyptian customers expect warmth, professional advice, honesty, privacy, fair prices and reliability.
- Mapping a journey as a team shows where small changes give big improvements.
- A problem handled well can strengthen loyalty.
