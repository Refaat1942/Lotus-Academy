## Learning objectives

After completing this lesson, you will be able to:

1. Answer phone calls and WhatsApp messages to the Lotus standard.
2. Confirm an order clearly so that nothing is misunderstood.
3. Offer substitutions only with the customer's consent and within the rules.
4. Show courtesy and professionalism in delivery.
5. Handle missed or failed deliveries calmly.
6. Protect the privacy of customers' orders and health information.

---

## 1. Distance service is real service

In Egyptian cities many customers order by phone or WhatsApp and receive their medicines at home, especially older customers, parents with young children and people who are unwell. They cannot see our counter, our uniform or our smile. They judge us by **how fast, how clearly and how respectfully** we respond. The same standards of the branch apply here: welcome, listening, honesty and follow-up.

## 2. Phone standards

1. **Answer quickly,** ideally within a few rings.
2. **Greet and identify:** "Lotus Pharmacy, [branch], good evening. How can I help you?"
3. **Listen fully** and take notes. Do not eat, chew or talk to colleagues during a call.
4. **Ask for permission before a hold:** "May I put you on hold for one minute while I check?" Return within the time promised and thank the customer.
5. **Repeat the key details** such as product, strength, quantity, price and delivery address.
6. **Close politely:** "Is there anything else I can help you with? Thank you for calling Lotus."

If a customer is on the phone and one is at the counter, the customer physically present is greeted first, but a short, polite request to the caller to wait is better than leaving the phone unanswered for long. Where possible, one team member covers the phone during busy hours.

Medical questions: the pharmacist answers questions about medicines. Give general information only, do not diagnose, and say "please consult your doctor" when appropriate. Never give clinical advice by guessing.

## 3. WhatsApp standards

- **First response** within the time set by the branch (for example within a few minutes during opening hours). If you cannot reply fully, send a short message: "We received your message and will reply shortly."
- **Use a professional tone:** polite greeting, clear Arabic, no slang that could be misunderstood, and emojis kept minimal or avoided. No voice notes with background noise.
- **Use the official branch account only,** never a personal phone, for customer messages.
- **Structure messages:** short sentences, one question at a time, a list for products and prices.
- **Prescription photos:** accept them only as the SOP allows, check them carefully, and delete them from personal devices where the SOP requires. Never forward them.
- **State working hours** and what happens outside them.

Example message:

Pharmacist: "Good evening, welcome to Lotus. We have the product you asked for. The price is [amount] EGP. Delivery takes about [time]. Would you like us to proceed?"

## 4. Order confirmation

Always confirm before dispatch. A confirmation message contains:

| Item | Example |
|---|---|
| Products and quantities | Product name, strength, number of boxes |
| Prices and total | Each price, delivery fee, final total in EGP |
| Payment method | Cash on delivery or other options offered |
| Address and contact | Full address, floor, landmark, and who will receive |
| Delivery time | A realistic time window |
| Notes | Prescription required on delivery, if applicable |

Ask the customer to reply "confirmed". Controlled or prescription-only medicines follow the current rules of the Egyptian Drug Authority / Ministry of Health and the Lotus SOP. Never dispatch an order that does not meet these rules.

## 5. Substitutions with consent

When something ordered is unavailable:

1. Tell the customer promptly, not at the door.
2. Offer a suitable alternative that the pharmacist approves, explain what is the same and what is different, and state any price difference.
3. Wait for a **clear yes** in writing or by call. Silence is not consent.
4. If the order is based on a prescription, do not change the medicine without the required approval.
5. Record the agreed change on the order.

Script: "The brand you requested is out of stock. We have an equivalent with the same active ingredient at [price]. Would you like this instead, or should I remove it from the order?"

## 6. Delivery courtesy

The courier is the face of Lotus at the customer's door.

- Clean, neat appearance, a name badge or Lotus identification, a polite greeting.
- Call or message when close, and respect the agreed time.
- Hand over the order in sealed, clean packaging, and keep medicines protected from heat and damage.
- Check the products with the customer when appropriate, give the receipt, and collect payment accurately.
- Never discuss the contents of the order with neighbours or other people at the door, and never leave medicines unattended.
- Speak respectfully even if the customer is upset. Escalate to the branch if there is a problem.
- Thank the customer on leaving.

## 7. Missed or failed deliveries

A missed delivery is frustrating for everyone. Follow these steps.

1. Call the customer **before** declaring a failed delivery. Try the agreed number once more and wait a reasonable time.
2. If there is no answer, send a message with the branch's next step. Do not leave medicines with a neighbour or doorman unless the customer clearly authorised it in advance and the SOP allows.
3. Return the order to the branch safely, with medicines kept in correct storage. Items needing special storage conditions are the priority.
4. Contact the customer later to arrange a new time, and apologise if the delay was on our side.
5. If the delay is ours, offer the agreed courtesy such as free delivery on the rescheduled order, if the SOP allows.

Script: "We tried to deliver your order and could not reach you. We have kept it safely. When would suit you for a new delivery today or tomorrow?"

## 8. Privacy of orders

Orders reveal health conditions. Treat them as confidential.

- Share customer details only with staff who need them to fulfil the order.
- Do not read out an order, name or address where others can hear.
- Do not post, forward or screenshot customer chats or prescriptions outside the official process.
- Confirm that the person receiving a sensitive order is the right person.
- Never discuss a customer's orders with relatives or neighbours who ask.

## Common mistakes

- Leaving the phone ringing or WhatsApp unanswered for hours.
- Dispatching without confirming the total and address.
- Substituting a product silently.
- Sending customer photos or chats to personal groups.
- A courier announcing the medicine's name at the door.
- Declaring a failed delivery without trying to call.

## Scenario

On a rainy evening a customer sends a WhatsApp message with a photo of a prescription for her father. The branch is busy.

**Model approach.** A team member replies within minutes: "Good evening, we received the prescription and the pharmacist is checking availability. We will reply shortly." The pharmacist checks the items and finds one is out of stock. The customer receives a clear message with prices, the delivery fee and total, and a question about the out-of-stock item: an equivalent is offered, only if appropriate and within the rules, and the pharmacist explains the difference. The customer replies "confirmed". The courier calls when close, hands over the sealed bag at the door, and does not mention the medicines aloud. If the customer had not answered the door, the courier would call again, report to the branch, and the branch would arrange a new time. The prescription photo is handled as the SOP requires.

## Key takeaways

- Phone, WhatsApp and delivery are part of the customer experience and must meet the same standards as the counter.
- Answer quickly, greet clearly, listen and repeat the key details.
- Always confirm products, price, address, payment and time before dispatch.
- Substitute only with a clear yes and within the rules, never silently.
- The courier represents Lotus: courteous, punctual and discreet.
- Missed deliveries need a call, safe storage and a new arranged time.
- Orders are confidential health information and must be protected.
