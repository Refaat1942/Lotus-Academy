## Learning objectives

After completing this lesson, you will be able to:

1. Welcome customers within a few seconds using a warm, professional greeting.
2. Apply Lotus standards for appearance, hygiene and a tidy counter.
3. Use good counter etiquette, including listening, privacy and phone discipline.
4. Manage queues and busy hours fairly and acknowledge waiting customers.
5. Handle unavailable stock honestly and offer a useful next step.

---

## 1. The welcome

The customer decides within seconds whether the branch feels welcoming. The welcome is simple and must become a habit for the whole team.

**The welcome routine**

1. Look up and make eye contact.
2. Smile and greet in Arabic, matching the customer's tone.
3. Offer help or give a short time estimate.
4. Use a respectful form of address, never a nickname the customer did not choose.

| Situation | What to say (Arabic, short) | English meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Customer enters | "أهلًا وسهلًا، اتفضل" | Welcome, please come in |
| You are with another customer | "أهلًا بحضرتك، دقيقة واحدة وهكون معاك" | Welcome, one minute and I will be with you |
| Elderly customer | "حمدلله على السلامة، أقدر أساعد حضرتك إزاي؟" | Good to see you, how can I help you? |

Pharmacist: "Welcome. How can I help you today?"
Customer: "I need something for my son's cough."
Pharmacist: "Of course. How old is he, and how long has he been coughing?"

Notice that the welcome leads directly into listening.

## 2. Appearance and the branch

Customers judge safety and quality from what they see.

- **Staff:** clean, ironed uniform or coat, name badge worn visibly, hair and hands tidy, no strong perfume, no chewing gum.
- **Hygiene:** wash hands regularly, especially after handling money.
- **Counter:** clear of personal items, food, drinks and loose papers. Only the current customer's items are on it.
- **Shelves and floor:** products faced forward, in date, dusted, price tags correct and visible, floor clean and dry.
- **Storage:** medicines needing special conditions are kept as the Lotus SOP requires, never displayed in direct sunlight.
- **Entrance:** door and window clear, opening hours visible, lighting working.

Do a **five-minute opening check** every morning and a **short tidy** before each peak period.

## 3. Counter etiquette

- Give one customer your full attention at a time. If the phone rings during a conversation, say "Excuse me, one moment" only if it is truly necessary, or let a colleague answer.
- Put your phone away. Personal calls and messages are never taken in front of customers.
- Speak clearly and at a calm pace. Avoid medical jargon, or explain it simply.
- Protect privacy: lower your voice, and for sensitive topics invite the customer to a quieter corner if the branch layout allows.
- Say the **price before** packing the items, and repeat the total before payment.
- Check that the customer understood how to use each product. Ask: "Would you like me to repeat the instructions?"
- Never argue about a customer's tone. Stay calm and polite.
- Close with a goodbye: "Thank you for visiting Lotus, we wish you good health."

## 4. Queues and busy hours

Evening hours and the start of the month are busy in many Egyptian branches, when people collect regular medicines and prescriptions. A busy branch is not an excuse for poor service.

**Principles of fair queue management**

1. **Acknowledge everyone.** A look, a nod or "I will be with you shortly" is enough.
2. **Serve in order of arrival,** except for urgent situations, such as a customer who appears unwell or an elderly or disabled customer, handled respectfully and quietly.
3. **Use waiting time.** Ask the next customer to prepare the prescription, or start checking availability.
4. **Divide the work.** One team member answers simple questions and handles quick purchases while the pharmacist counsels.
5. **Give honest time estimates.** "About five minutes" is better than "just a moment" if it will be five minutes.
6. **Do not take a phone call before a customer standing in front of you,** unless it is urgent for patient safety.
7. **Call for help.** If the queue grows, the manager redistributes tasks or opens a second counter position.

Busy-hour script:

Pharmacist: "Thank you for waiting. I apologise for the delay. How can I help?"

If a customer is clearly frustrated by the wait:

Pharmacist: "I understand that waiting is not pleasant. Thank you for your patience. Let me take care of you now."

## 5. When stock is unavailable

"Not available" is the beginning of service, not the end. Follow these steps.

1. **Check carefully:** shelf, storage and system, including other Lotus branches if the SOP allows.
2. **Tell the truth** and apologise briefly.
3. **Offer options:** order from another branch or supplier with a clear time, offer a suitable alternative (same active ingredient or an equivalent product) only within the rules and after the customer agrees, or suggest the customer ask the prescriber when a prescription is involved.
4. **Never push** a more expensive alternative without explaining the reason. Explain the price difference.
5. **Record** the request so purchasing can see the demand.
6. **Follow up** when the product arrives, if the customer agreed.

Pharmacist: "I am sorry, this item is not available today. I can order it from another branch and it should arrive tomorrow, or I can suggest an equivalent with the same active ingredient. Which would you prefer?"

Substitution rules follow the current rules of the Egyptian Drug Authority / Ministry of Health and the Lotus SOP. Never change a prescribed medicine without the required approval.

## 6. Common mistakes

- Greeting after the customer has been standing for minutes.
- Chatting with a colleague, or looking at the phone, while customers wait.
- Saying "no" without any alternative.
- Eating or drinking at the counter.
- Letting a messy counter or empty shelves give the impression of poor care.
- Raising the voice to compete with a busy branch.

## Scenario

It is 8 pm and the branch has eight customers. A customer asks for a specific cream that is out of stock and says: "I came all the way here for this."

**Model approach.** The pharmacist apologises sincerely, without excuses: "I am really sorry, I understand you made a special trip." Then the pharmacist checks the system, finds the item in another branch, and offers delivery the next day or collection when it arrives. The pharmacist also describes, within the rules, a similar product if the customer is interested, and explains the price difference. The customer chooses delivery. The pharmacist writes down the details, confirms the time, and tells the customer how they will be contacted. A team member, meanwhile, has acknowledged the other waiting customers. The customer leaves informed and not abandoned.

## Key takeaways

- The first few seconds decide whether a customer feels welcome. Use eye contact, a smile and a clear greeting.
- Appearance, hygiene and a tidy counter signal safety and professionalism.
- Give each customer full attention, protect privacy and keep personal phones away.
- In busy hours acknowledge everyone, serve fairly, use waiting time and give honest estimates.
- "Not available" must always be followed by an honest explanation and a useful option.
- Substitutions need customer consent and must follow the rules and the Lotus SOP.
