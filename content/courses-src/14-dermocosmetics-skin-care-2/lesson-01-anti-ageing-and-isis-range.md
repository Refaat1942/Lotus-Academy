## Learning objectives

After completing this lesson, you will be able to:

1. Explain the main causes of fine lines and wrinkles in simple words
2. Distinguish prevention from correction and set realistic expectations by age group
3. Separate product facts from general skin-care advice when speaking to a customer

5. Recommend a safe starting routine and recognise when to refer to a dermatologist

## 1. How and why skin shows age

Lines and wrinkles are a normal part of life. Pharmacists do not promise to remove them; they help customers slow down visible ageing and look after their skin. This section is general skin-care knowledge, not product information.

Common contributors to visible lines:

- **Sun exposure over the years.** In a sunny country like Egypt, daily sun is the biggest avoidable factor. Unprotected exposure over time is linked to lines, uneven colour and rough texture.
- **Natural slowing of skin renewal and of the skin's support structure.** Skin becomes thinner and drier with age.
- **Repeated facial movement.** Frowning, squinting and smiling create *expression lines*, especially on the forehead, around the eyes and the mouth.
- **Dryness and dehydration.** Dry skin shows fine lines more clearly.
- **Lifestyle.** Smoking, poor sleep and very irregular skin care can make skin look tired.

> Key idea: most of what a customer can control is daily sun protection, gentle cleansing, moisturising and consistency.

## 2. Prevention versus correction

| | Prevention | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Aim | Keep skin healthy and slow visible change | Soften lines that are already visible |
| Typical customer | Younger customer with no lines yet | Customer who already sees lines |
| Core habits | Daily sun protection, gentle cleansing, moisturiser | The same habits, plus a targeted anti-wrinkle product used regularly |
| Honest message | "The earlier the habit, the better" | "Improvement is gradual; lines will not disappear" |

### Realistic expectations by age

These are general guidelines for conversation, not rules:

- **Twenties:** focus on sun protection, cleansing and light moisturising. Specialist anti-ageing products are usually not the priority.
- **Thirties:** first expression lines and early dryness may appear. Prevention habits plus a light anti-ageing product can be discussed.
- **Forties and beyond:** lines are more visible and skin is drier. Richer care and consistent use matter more.

Always ask about the customer's goal, current routine and skin sensitivity before recommending anything.

## 3. Product facts versus general advice

When you counsel, keep two voices clearly apart:

- **Product facts:** what the Lotus product sheet says the product is for. Say exactly this and nothing more. Do not add ingredients, percentages or promises.
- **General advice:** daily sun protection, patience, patch testing, avoiding too many new products at once.

If a customer asks something the sheet does not answer, say: "Let me check the leaflet for you" and read the leaflet, or ask the pharmacy manager.

## 5. Counselling examples

**Example: a customer in her thirties with early lines.**

> Customer: "I see small lines near my eyes. What cream removes them?"
> Pharmacist: "No cream removes lines completely, but a good routine can soften their look and slow new ones. Do you use sunscreen every day?"
> Customer: "Only at the beach."
> Pharmacist: "In Egypt the sun affects us every day, even in the city. Daily sunscreen is the first step. Then we can add a gentle care step. For the eye area we have a product listed for puffiness and dark circles, and for general lines there is one listed to help collagen formation. Let us start simply."

**Example: a customer with rough elbows and heels.**

> Pharmacist: "For very dry, flaking skin on the elbows, knees and heels we have a moisturiser listed for exactly this. For general body dryness, including children, there is a balm that can also be used on the lips."

## 6. Safe starting rules

1. Introduce **one new active product at a time** so a reaction can be traced.
2. Suggest a **patch test** on a small area (for example the inner forearm) before first full use.
3. Exfoliating products should be used gradually and with sun protection.
4. **Pregnancy or breastfeeding:** do not decide yourself. Advise the customer to ask her doctor and read the leaflet.
5. **Refer to a dermatologist** if there is severe or painful acne, scarring, sudden skin changes, a mole that changes, white patches, or no improvement after a reasonable trial.

## Common mistakes

- Promising that wrinkles will disappear
- Selling an anti-ageing product without daily sun protection
- Recommending several new active products on the same day
- Describing a product whose usage is not on the sheet
- Treating acne in teenagers as simply "oily skin" when it may need medical care

## Scenario

A customer in her forties walks into a busy Lotus branch and asks for "the strongest anti-wrinkle product". She uses a basic cream and no sunscreen. The pharmacist asks about her skin type, how long she is willing to follow a routine, and whether she is pregnant or breastfeeding. She is not. The pharmacist explains that results are gradual, proposes daily sunscreen first, a moisturiser, and then one targeted product listed for collagen support, introduced after a patch test. She is told to come back in a few weeks to review the skin and is warned to stop and ask if redness or irritation appears. The pharmacist does not promise removal of wrinkles and does not add extra products she did not need.

## Key takeaways

- Wrinkles are normal; the pharmacist's role is prevention and gradual improvement, never promises
- Daily sun protection is the foundation of any anti-ageing routine in Egypt
- Use only the sheet for product facts; keep general advice clearly separate
- ISIS covers exfoliation, acne, dry body skin, sun protection, eye care, vitamin C and anti-wrinkle needs
- Where the sheet has no usage written, check the leaflet and do not guess
- Introduce one product at a time, patch test, and ask a doctor about pregnancy or breastfeeding
- Refer severe acne, vitiligo concerns and changing moles to a dermatologist
