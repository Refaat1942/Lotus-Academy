## Learning objectives

After completing this lesson, you will be able to:

1. Run a short consultation that finds the customer's concern, skin type, routine and budget
2. Build simple morning and evening routines using products from the Lotus sheet
3. Explain regimen length and realistic expectations honestly
4. Respond to premium-price objections with honest value explanation and without pressure
5. Offer ethical add-ons and recognise when to refer to a doctor or dermatologist

---

## 1. The consultation: five questions before any product

A premium product is only valuable when it fits the customer. Use a short, friendly consultation, even in a busy branch.

1. **Concern:** "What bothers you most about your skin?" (lines, spots, dryness, tired skin, acne marks)
2. **Skin type and sensitivity:** "Is your skin dry, oily or mixed? Does it react to new products?"
3. **Current routine:** "What do you use morning and evening? Do you use sunscreen?"
4. **Health and safety:** "Are you pregnant or breastfeeding? Do you have a skin condition or take medicines?"
5. **Expectations and budget:** "How much time can you give, and what monthly budget feels comfortable?"

> Customer: "I want something for my face."
> Pharmacist: "Happy to help. Can I ask three quick things so I choose properly? What is your main concern, what do you use now, and is your skin sensitive?"

If the answer to question 4 suggests pregnancy, breastfeeding or a medical condition, do not decide yourself. Ask the doctor and read the leaflet.

## 2. Simple premium routines

Keep routines short. A routine the customer follows is better than a long routine she abandons. The examples below use only products from the Lotus sheet, and the prices are not discussed here.

### Example A: early lines, thirties, combination skin

| Time | Steps |
|---|---|
| Morning | Nuxe cleansing water or foam, Nuxe toning lotion, Merveillane fluid (combination and oily), ISIS Uveblock fluid (SPF 50+) |
| Evening | Nuxe cleansing step to remove sunscreen, toning lotion, Merveillane fluid or serum |
| Weekly | Nuxe exfoliant as listed, introduced gradually |

### Example B: deep lines, fifties, dry skin

| Time | Steps |
|---|---|
| Morning | Nuxe cleansing milk, Nuxuriance day cream (normal and dry skin), sunscreen |
| Evening | Nuxe cleansing milk, Nuxuriance night cream, Eye And Lips serum |

### Example C: dark spots after acne, sunscreen as the base

| Time | Steps |
|---|---|
| Morning | Gentle cleanser, moisturiser, ISIS Uveblock sunscreen, or A.C.M. Depiwhite SPF50 as written in the leaflet |
| Evening | The brightening product listed for night use (for example Unitone 4 (30 ml) as the leaflet advises, Neotone, or Depiwhite advanced on the spots), after a patch test |

Rules of thumb (general advice):

- Introduce **one new active product at a time**.
- Always include **daily sunscreen**, especially with brightening, exfoliating or anti-wrinkle products.
- For brightening products, **follow the leaflet** for how often and how long to apply them, and never mix several brightening creams unless the leaflet or the doctor says so.
- Keep to two to four steps at first. Add more only if needed.

## 3. Regimen length and expectations

Customers who understand the timeline are more satisfied and less likely to blame the product.

- **Anti-ageing:** improvement is gradual. Weeks of regular use are needed before the skin looks fresher or smoother. Deep lines soften but do not vanish.
- **Brightening:** weeks to months of regular use with daily sunscreen. Stopping early or skipping sunscreen can bring spots back.
- **Cleansing, toning and moisturising:** comfort and a fresher look are usually felt sooner than changes in lines or spots.

Phrase it plainly:

> "Please give it a few weeks of regular use. If you see no change, or the skin becomes red or sore, come back and tell us before you buy anything else."

Offer a follow-up: "Come and see us in a few weeks and we will review your skin together."

## 4. Handling premium-price objections

Customers compare the price to other brands, to what they paid in the past, or to a pharmacy in another street. Never argue, never criticise another product, and never invent claims to justify the price.

### A framework: Listen, Clarify, Explain value, Offer options

1. **Listen** without interrupting. Acknowledge: "I understand, price matters."
2. **Clarify** the real concern: "Is it the total, or are you not sure it will work for you?"
3. **Explain value honestly:** what the product is listed for, how it fits the routine, how long it is meant to last, and what to expect.
4. **Offer options:** a smaller size, a simpler routine, a single key product first, or a more affordable alternative that fits the need.

### Honest value statements (examples)

- "This cream is listed for expression lines in all skin types. If you use it every day with sunscreen, a jar is meant to cover a good period of daily use. The leaflet will tell you how much to use."
- "You do not need the whole line today. Let us start with the cleanser and one care product, and add only if you see a need."
- "I cannot promise a result. What I can promise is that I will not sell you things you do not need."

### Customer objections and responses

| Customer says | Pharmacist responds |
|---|---|
| "It is too expensive." | "I understand. Let us look at what you really need today. If I choose only two steps, what budget feels comfortable?" |
| "The other pharmacy sells something cheaper." | "There may be several choices. I can explain what this product is listed for, and you can decide. I would not suggest you pay more than the plan you need." |
| "Will it definitely work?" | "Skin responds differently from person to person, and results take weeks. If you follow the routine and use sunscreen, you give it a fair chance." |
| "I will think about it." | "Of course. Take the leaflet information in your head, and come back anytime. I can also note the products for you." |

### What not to do

- Do not exaggerate results or say "guaranteed".
- Do not say a cheaper product is "fake" or "useless".
- Do not push the largest set because it earns a higher bill.
- Do not offer discounts or promotions that are not approved by the Lotus SOP.

## 5. Ethical add-ons

An add-on is ethical when it makes the main product work better or keeps the skin safe and the customer would reasonably want it.

| Main product | Reasonable add-on | Why it is reasonable |
|---|---|---|
| Brightening cream | Daily sunscreen | Sun can darken spots again |
| Exfoliant | Moisturiser and sunscreen | Exfoliated skin needs comfort and protection |
| Anti-wrinkle night cream | Daytime sunscreen | Sun is a major factor in lines |
| Cleansing step | Toning lotion | The next step in the listed routine |
| Eye cream | None unless the customer wants one | Do not add for the sake of adding |

Ask yourself: "Would I suggest this add-on if the customer were my own family member?" If not, do not suggest it.

## 6. When to refer

Stop selling and refer to a doctor or dermatologist when:

- a spot is new, growing, raised, bleeding, itchy or changing colour
- there is severe or painful acne, scarring or sudden skin changes
- there are white patches (the sheet lists a product for vitiligo, which still needs a medical opinion)
- the customer is pregnant or breastfeeding and wants an active treatment, so the doctor decides
- there is persistent redness, burning, swelling or blistering after using a product
- there is no improvement after a reasonable trial

## Scenario

A customer in her forties comes to a Lotus branch for "an anti-wrinkle cream and something for dark spots". She is shocked by the cost of the products the pharmacist first mentions. The pharmacist listens, then asks about her routine: she uses no sunscreen and no night product. The pharmacist explains that a sunscreen every morning would help both goals, builds a simple three-step plan (cleanser, a single listed anti-wrinkle product at night, sunscreen in the morning), and suggests adding a brightening product later if the spots remain after a few weeks and a patch test. She is told that results take weeks. The pharmacist notes the plan on a card with the product names and offers a follow-up visit. The final bill is lower than she feared, she understands what each product is for, and she leaves feeling respected.

## Key takeaways

- Consult first: concern, skin type, current routine, health and budget
- Keep routines to two to four steps and always include daily sunscreen
- Give honest timelines: weeks for anti-ageing and weeks to months for brightening
- Handle price objections with listening, clarifying, honest value and options
- Never promise results, criticise other products or use unapproved promotions
- Add-ons must serve the customer's skin, not the bill
- Refer changing spots, irritation, white patches, pregnancy or breastfeeding questions and non-responding cases
