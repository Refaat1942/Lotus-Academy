## 1. Why liver products need careful counselling

Liver disease is a major health concern in Egypt. Viral hepatitis (B and C), fatty liver linked to excess weight and diabetes, alcohol, and medicine-related liver injury are all seen in daily practice. At the same time, "liver tonics" are among the most requested OTC products. Many customers buy them hoping to "clean" or "protect" the liver while the real cause of their liver disease goes untreated. The pharmacist's job is to give honest information about what these products can and cannot do, and to make sure the cause is being treated by a doctor.

## 2. Hepatoprotectants (variable evidence)

| Product | Active | Claim |
|---|---|---|
| Legalon | Silymarin | Milk thistle — adjunct |
| Essentiale | Phospholipids | Fatty liver adjunct |
| Hepaticum | Mixed herbal | Popular Egypt OTC |

Evidence is **modest** — these products are **not a substitute for treating the cause** (HBV, NASH, alcohol).

- **Silymarin (Legalon)** is extracted from milk thistle. It has antioxidant effects in laboratory studies, but clinical benefit in patients is limited. It is generally well tolerated; mild stomach upset can occur.
- **Essential phospholipids (Essentiale)** are marketed for fatty liver as an adjunct. Evidence of benefit on important outcomes is limited.
- **Mixed herbal products (Hepaticum)** are popular OTC choices. Herbal does not always mean safe: some herbal products can themselves injure the liver, so always ask what the customer is already taking.

**What actually treats the liver:** antiviral treatment for hepatitis B or C prescribed by a doctor; stopping alcohol; for fatty liver (now often called metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease, MASLD, with the inflamed form previously called NASH), **gradual weight loss**, healthy diet, physical activity and control of diabetes and lipids; and avoiding medicines and herbs that harm the liver.

## 3. Gallstone dissolution and PBC

**Ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA, Ursofalk)** is a bile acid that reduces the cholesterol content of bile. It can slowly dissolve **small cholesterol gallstones** in a gallbladder that still works, over many months, and stones may return after stopping. It does not dissolve calcified or pigment stones and does not replace surgery when a surgeon advises it. UDCA is also a main treatment for **primary biliary cholangitis (PBC)**, usually long term under a specialist. Counsel the patient to take it regularly as prescribed, often with food, and not to stop without advice.

**Rowachol** is a mixture of plant terpenes used as an **adjunct** for gallstones; evidence is limited and it does not replace medical assessment.

**Refer urgently:** severe pain in the right upper abdomen, especially with fever, yellow eyes or skin (jaundice), dark urine, pale stools, persistent vomiting or confusion.

## 4. Paracetamol: safe dose and overdose awareness

Paracetamol is safe at the correct dose but **toxic to the liver in overdose**. For most adults the maximum is 4 g a day (for example, no more than eight 500 mg tablets in 24 hours, with at least four hours between doses); the doctor may advise a lower maximum for people with liver disease, regular alcohol use or low body weight. Many cold and flu combinations also contain paracetamol, so "double dosing" is common: always check every product the customer takes.

An overdose may cause **no symptoms at first**, while liver damage develops over the next one to three days. An effective antidote exists but works best when given early. **Any suspected overdose, even if the person feels well, must go to the emergency department immediately.**

**Counseling (Arabic):** "حاميات الكبد مساعدة بس — الأهم وقف الكحول وعلاج السبب. باراسيتامول متعديش الجرعة — سام للكبد."

## Scenario

> Patient with hepatitis B on herbal hepatoprotectant only — urgent physician referral for antiviral assessment.

A customer buys a herbal liver product every month. In conversation the customer mentions a hepatitis B result from a blood test a year ago and says, "I take this instead of the expensive treatment."

**Model approach:** The pharmacist explains respectfully that herbal products cannot treat hepatitis B and that the virus can silently damage the liver. The pharmacist strongly advises an urgent visit to a doctor or hepatology clinic for assessment of whether antiviral treatment is needed, mentions that family members may need testing and vaccination as the doctor advises, and reviews any other medicines, especially paracetamol use. The herbal product may be continued only if the doctor agrees.

## Key takeaways

- Legalon (silymarin) is popular but has modest evidence; it is an adjunct only
- Always treat the underlying cause of liver disease
- Ursofalk (UDCA) is for small cholesterol gallstones and for PBC
- Paracetamol overdose is hepatotoxic; check combinations and refer any overdose immediately
- Alcohol cessation is critical in liver disease
- Herbal products are not a substitute for antiviral treatment
