## 1. How nausea and vomiting are controlled

Nausea and vomiting are coordinated by the brain's vomiting centre, which receives signals from several places: the **chemoreceptor trigger zone** (sensitive to drugs and toxins, mainly through **dopamine D2** and **serotonin 5-HT3** receptors), the **gut** (5-HT3 signals released when the gut lining is irritated, for example by chemotherapy), and the **inner ear** (motion, mainly through **histamine H1** and muscarinic receptors). Each antiemetic blocks one of these pathways, so choosing the right one depends on the cause.

## 2. Antiemetics

| Drug | Brand | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Metoclopramide | Primperan | D2 antagonist — EPS risk |
| Domperidone | Motilium | D2 — less CNS; QT risk |
| Ondansetron | Zofran | 5-HT3 — chemo/post-op |
| Dimenhydrinate | Dramamine | Antihistamine — motion sickness |

**Metoclopramide (Primperan)** blocks D2 receptors centrally and speeds up stomach emptying (a prokinetic effect). Because it crosses into the brain it can cause **extrapyramidal symptoms (EPS)**: abnormal movements of the face, neck or eyes, restlessness and, with long use, tardive dyskinesia. The risk is higher in **children and young adults**. It should be used at the lowest dose for short courses only, as prescribed; avoid long-term use.

**Domperidone (Motilium)** also blocks D2 receptors and has a prokinetic effect, but it crosses into the brain much less, so EPS are rare. Its key risk is **QT prolongation** and abnormal heart rhythm, especially in older adults, at higher doses, in people with heart disease, and with other medicines that prolong the QT interval or strongly inhibit its metabolism (for example some macrolide antibiotics and azole antifungals). European guidance restricts it to the lowest effective dose for the shortest time, usually no longer than one week. It is taken **15–30 minutes before meals**.

**Ondansetron (Zofran)** blocks 5-HT3 receptors and is used for severe nausea and vomiting after chemotherapy or surgery, as prescribed. Constipation and headache are common side effects; it can also affect the QT interval.

**Dimenhydrinate (Dramamine)** is a sedating antihistamine for motion sickness. It works best when taken 30–60 minutes before the journey. Warn about drowsiness: do not drive, and avoid alcohol.

## 3. Irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) in Egypt

IBS is a long-term functional condition with abdominal pain or discomfort linked to a change in bowel habit (diarrhoea, constipation or both), often with bloating. Stress and diet commonly trigger flares.

**Medicines often used:**

- **Mebeverine (Duspatalin)**: an antispasmodic that relaxes gut smooth muscle; usually taken before meals
- **Trimebutine (Polybalm)**: regulates gut motility and relieves spasm
- **Peppermint oil** (enteric-coated capsules): relaxes gut muscle; may worsen heartburn in some people

**Diet and lifestyle:** regular meals, adequate water, gradual changes in fibre (soluble fibre such as psyllium is often better tolerated), reducing caffeine, fizzy drinks and very fatty or spicy food, managing stress, and regular activity. A **low FODMAP** diet can help many patients; it is best done for a limited period with professional guidance because it is restrictive.

**Counseling (Arabic):** "موتيليوم قبل الأكل ب15-30 دقيقة. قلل الضغط والقهوة لمتلازمة القولون."

## 4. Red flags: refer to a doctor

IBS is diagnosed by a doctor. Refer when bowel symptoms start after age 50, or there is blood in the stool, unintended weight loss, anaemia, night-time symptoms that wake the patient, fever, or a family history of bowel cancer or inflammatory bowel disease. For vomiting, refer when there is blood or "coffee-ground" material, signs of dehydration, severe abdominal pain, head injury, vomiting lasting more than 24–48 hours (shorter in infants and older adults), or possible pregnancy with severe vomiting.

## Scenario

> Motilium requested long-term — counsel max 7 days per EMA guidance, QT monitoring.

An older customer asks for "a big box of Motilium" because they take it every day for bloating and fullness. They also take a heart medicine and recently finished a course of clarithromycin.

**Model approach:** The pharmacist explains that domperidone is meant for short courses, usually not more than seven days, because of the risk to heart rhythm, which is higher with age, heart disease and some antibiotics. The pharmacist does not sell a long-term supply, offers simple advice on meals and bloating, and refers the customer to the doctor to find the cause of the symptoms and review heart-rhythm risk before any further use.

## Key takeaways

- Primperan (metoclopramide) carries an EPS risk, especially in the young; avoid long-term use
- Motilium (domperidone) carries a QT risk; use short courses only, before meals
- Zofran (ondansetron) is for severe nausea such as after chemotherapy or surgery
- IBS management depends on dietary and lifestyle counselling, with low FODMAP as an option
- Mebeverine is a common antispasmodic for IBS
- Refer red flags such as blood in stool, weight loss or new symptoms after age 50
