## 1. Allergy or cold?

Customers often say "I have a cold" for very different problems. **Allergic rhinitis** causes sneezing, itchy nose and eyes, and clear watery discharge, often seasonal or triggered by dust, pets or pollen, without fever. A **common cold** is a viral infection with sore throat, blocked or runny nose, cough and sometimes mild fever, usually improving within 7–10 days. **Influenza** comes on suddenly with high fever, muscle aches and marked tiredness. Antihistamines are the core treatment for allergy; for colds, treatment is supportive and aimed at the most troublesome symptoms. Antibiotics do not treat viral colds or flu.

## 2. Antihistamines

Antihistamines block histamine at H1 receptors, reducing sneezing, itching and runny nose. **First-generation** antihistamines cross into the brain and cause drowsiness, slowed reactions and dry mouth; **second-generation** antihistamines cross much less and are the usual first choice for daytime allergy.

| Gen | Brand | Sedation |
|---|---|---|
| 2nd | Telfast (fexofenadine) | Non-sedating |
| 2nd | Zyrtec (cetirizine) | Mild sedation |
| 1st | Allergex (chlorpheniramine) | Sedating |
| 2nd | Claritin (loratadine) | Non-sedating |

**Sedation and driving:** first-generation antihistamines can impair driving and machine use even when the person does not feel sleepy, and the effect adds to alcohol and other sedating medicines. Cetirizine causes drowsiness in some people. For drivers, students and workers who need to stay alert, a non-sedating option such as fexofenadine or loratadine is usually preferred. Fexofenadine should be taken with water rather than fruit juice, which can reduce its absorption. Older adults, children, pregnancy and breastfeeding need extra care; check the label and refer when in doubt.

## 3. Cold/flu products in Egypt

**Otrivin (xylometazoline)** nasal spray or drops relieve a blocked nose quickly — **max 7 days**. Longer use leads to **rebound congestion** (rhinitis medicamentosa), where the nose becomes more blocked each time the spray wears off. Use the strength made for the child's age as stated on the label.

**Pseudoephedrine products raise BP** and heart rate. Avoid or use only on medical advice in people with high blood pressure, heart disease, overactive thyroid, glaucoma, prostate enlargement, or those taking certain antidepressants (MAO inhibitors).

**Paracetamol + phenylephrine combos are common.** The effectiveness of oral phenylephrine as a decongestant has been questioned by regulators in some countries. Many combinations also contain an antihistamine or caffeine.

**Reading combination labels:** check every active ingredient against what the customer actually needs, what else they are taking (to avoid double paracetamol, as covered in Lesson 5.4), and their medical conditions. A single-ingredient product is often the better choice. Cough and cold combinations should not be sold for young children without a doctor's advice.

**Supportive care** is the foundation: rest, fluids, warm drinks, saline nasal spray or drops, steam, honey for cough in adults and children over one year, and paracetamol or ibuprofen for fever and aches when suitable.

**Counseling (Arabic):** "أوتريفين مايتستخدمش أكتر من 7 أيام — ممكن يسبب احتقان مزمن. مضادات الهيستامين الأولى بتنعس — متسوقش. لو عندك ضغط، احذر مزيلات الاحتقان."

## 4. Red flags: refer to a doctor

Refer when there is difficulty breathing or wheeze, high fever lasting more than three days, severe sore throat with difficulty swallowing, ear pain, facial pain with fever lasting more than ten days, symptoms in an infant, a chronic condition such as asthma, COPD, heart disease or diabetes with worsening symptoms, or confusion.

## Scenario

> Hypertensive patient wants OTC cold remedy with pseudoephedrine — counsel BP risk, alternative.

An older customer on blood pressure tablets asks for a popular cold-and-flu tablet containing pseudoephedrine for a blocked nose and headache.

**Model approach:** The pharmacist explains that pseudoephedrine can raise blood pressure and interfere with the customer's treatment. The pharmacist suggests saline nasal spray, and a short course of a topical decongestant such as Otrivin for no more than seven days if suitable, plus paracetamol at the correct dose for the headache. The pharmacist advises the customer to check blood pressure if possible and to see the doctor if symptoms worsen or last more than ten days.

## Key takeaways

- Telfast (fexofenadine) is a non-sedating choice for allergy
- Otrivin is limited to a maximum of 7 days to avoid rebound congestion
- First-generation antihistamines are sedating; warn about driving
- Decongestants such as pseudoephedrine raise blood pressure
- The common cold needs mainly supportive care
- Read combination product labels and check every ingredient
