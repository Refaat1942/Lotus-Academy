## 1. Asthma and COPD in brief

In **asthma**, the airways are chronically inflamed and narrow in episodes, causing wheeze, cough, chest tightness and breathlessness, often worse at night or with triggers such as dust, smoke, cold air, infections and exercise. Treatment has two jobs: **relieve** symptoms quickly and **control** the underlying inflammation. In **COPD**, usually linked to smoking or long exposure to smoke and dust, airflow limitation is persistent; long-acting bronchodilators are the backbone of treatment and stopping smoking is essential.

## 2. Bronchodilators and controllers

| Class | Brand | Use |
|---|---|---|
| SABA | Ventolin (salbutamol) | Rescue |
| LABA | Serevent | With ICS only |
| ICS | Flixotide | Controller |
| ICS/LABA | Seretide, Symbicort | Asthma/COPD maintenance |
| Anticholinergic | Spiriva (tiotropium) | COPD |

- **SABA (salbutamol, Ventolin)** relaxes airway muscle within minutes and lasts about four to six hours. It relieves symptoms but does not treat inflammation. Common side effects are tremor and a fast heartbeat.
- **LABA (salmeterol, Serevent; formoterol)** lasts about 12 hours. In asthma a LABA must **never be used alone**; it is always combined with an inhaled corticosteroid, because LABA alone can hide worsening inflammation and has been linked to severe attacks.
- **ICS (fluticasone, Flixotide; budesonide)** reduces airway inflammation and prevents attacks. It works over days to weeks, so it must be used **every day** even when the patient feels well. Rinsing the mouth after use reduces the risk of oral thrush and hoarseness.
- **ICS/LABA combinations**: **Seretide** = fluticasone/salmeterol; **Symbicort** = budesonide/formoterol. Used daily as maintenance in asthma and in some COPD patients. Because formoterol acts quickly, some doctors prescribe Symbicort both as maintenance and as reliever; follow the prescription exactly.
- **Long-acting anticholinergic (tiotropium, Spiriva)**: once-daily bronchodilator, mainly for COPD maintenance.

Current international asthma guidance no longer recommends treating asthma in adults and adolescents with a reliever alone; every patient with asthma should have a controller plan from the doctor.

## 3. Inhaler technique

Poor technique is one of the most common reasons an inhaler "does not work". Ask the patient to show you, not just tell you.

**Pressurised metered-dose inhaler (MDI, for example Ventolin, Seretide Evohaler):**

1. Remove the cap and **shake** the inhaler.
2. Breathe out gently, away from the mouthpiece.
3. **Seal the lips** around the mouthpiece.
4. Start breathing in **slowly and deeply** and press the canister once at the same time.
5. Continue breathing in, then **hold the breath for about 10 seconds** (or as long as comfortable).
6. Wait about 30 seconds before a second puff if prescribed.

**Dry powder inhalers (for example Symbicort Turbuhaler, Seretide Diskus)** work differently: do not shake them, load the dose as the device instructs, breathe out away from the device, then breathe in **quickly and deeply** to pull the powder in, and hold the breath.

**Spacers** improve delivery from an MDI, especially in **children and older adults** who find it hard to coordinate pressing and breathing. Young children use a spacer with a face mask. After an ICS, **rinse the mouth** and spit.

**Counseling (Arabic):** "فنتولين للضيق السريع — لو بتستخدمه كتير كل يوم، راجع الدكتور. سيريتايد للوقاية يومياً حتى لو مفيش أعراض. اشطف فمك بعد البخاخ الكورتيزون."

## 4. Signs of poor control and red flags

Refer for review if the patient uses the reliever more than twice a week, wakes at night with symptoms, has limited activity, or has needed oral steroids or emergency care. **Urgent help** is needed if breathlessness is severe, the patient cannot speak in full sentences, the lips are blue, or the reliever gives little or no relief.

## Scenario

> Uses Seretide only when symptomatic — counsel daily controller use, Ventolin for rescue.

A young adult refilling Seretide says the inhaler "lasts for months" because it is only used when the chest feels tight. The patient also buys Ventolin often.

**Model approach:** The pharmacist explains that Seretide is a preventer that controls inflammation and must be used every day as prescribed, even without symptoms, while Ventolin is the rescue inhaler for quick relief. The pharmacist checks technique with a placebo or the patient's own device, reminds the patient to rinse the mouth, and advises a doctor's review because frequent Ventolin use is a sign of poor control.

## Key takeaways

- Ventolin (salbutamol) is a SABA rescue inhaler
- Seretide and Symbicort are daily controllers (ICS/LABA)
- Rinse the mouth after an ICS
- A spacer improves MDI technique, especially in children and older adults
- A LABA is never used alone in asthma
- Shake an MDI before use; dry powder inhalers need a fast, deep breath and are not shaken
