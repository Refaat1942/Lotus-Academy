## Learning objectives

2. Identify **Antinal, Imodium, Normix** and the other diarrhoea products common in Egypt
3. Counsel **when NOT to stop diarrhea** and when to refer
4. Explain **rifaximin** for IBS-D and hepatic encephalopathy (HE)
5. Address **pediatric ORS priority**, with zinc and continued feeding

## 1. The first question: is it safe to stop the diarrhoea?

Most acute diarrhoea in Egypt is caused by viral or bacterial gut infections and settles within a few days. The real danger is **dehydration**, not the diarrhoea itself, and the danger is greatest in infants, young children and older adults. Diarrhoea is also the body's way of expelling some infections: when there is **blood in the stool or a fever**, slowing the gut can keep the infection inside and make it worse. So before choosing any product, the pharmacist asks: who is the patient, how long has it lasted, is there blood, fever, severe pain or vomiting, and are there signs of dehydration?

## 2. Antidiarrheals

| Drug | Brand | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Loperamide | Imodium | Symptomatic non-bloody diarrhea |
| Nifuroxazide | Antinal | OTC concern Egypt — not first line |
| Rifaximin | Normix | IBS-D, hepatic encephalopathy |
| Racecadotril | Tiorfan | Enkephalinase inhibitor — pediatrics |

**Loperamide (Imodium)** acts on opioid receptors in the gut wall to slow movement and increase water absorption. It is for **adults with non-bloody diarrhoea** and short-term use only. It should **not** be given to young children, and not when there is blood, high fever or suspected bacterial dysentery. Never exceed the label dose: high doses can cause serious heart-rhythm problems.

**Nifuroxazide (Antinal)** is a poorly absorbed intestinal anti-infective. In Egypt it is very widely bought over the counter for every kind of diarrhoea, including viral diarrhoea where it does not help. This is the **OTC misuse concern**: it is not first line, it can delay correct rehydration and medical assessment, and it does not replace ORS.

**Rifaximin (Normix)** is a non-absorbed antibiotic that stays in the gut. It is prescribed for **IBS with diarrhoea (IBS-D)** and to reduce the recurrence of **hepatic encephalopathy (HE)** in patients with advanced liver disease, where it lowers the gut bacteria that produce ammonia. It is a prescription medicine; check the patient understands it is part of a doctor's plan.

**Racecadotril (Tiorfan)** is an enkephalinase inhibitor that reduces the excess secretion of water into the gut without slowing gut movement. It is used, as prescribed or per label, together with ORS in children and adults.

## 3. Probiotics

| Brand | Use |
|---|---|
| Bioflor, Lacteol | Antibiotic-associated diarrhea |
| Enterogermina | Bacillus clausii |

Probiotics can **help reduce the risk of antibiotic-associated diarrhoea (AAD)** in some patients and are often sold alongside an antibiotic course. Evidence varies by strain. Bacterial probiotics are usually taken a couple of hours apart from the antibiotic dose; follow each product's leaflet. Probiotics are a support, not a treatment for dehydration, and should be avoided in severely ill or immunocompromised patients unless the doctor advises them.

## 4. Children: ORS comes first

For a child with diarrhoea, **oral rehydration solution (ORS)** is the most important treatment, more important than stopping the diarrhoea. Counsel the caregiver to:

- prepare the sachet exactly as written, with clean water, and never add sugar or salt
- give small, frequent sips with a cup or spoon, even if the child vomits (wait ten minutes and continue slowly)
- continue breastfeeding and normal food
- give **zinc** as recommended for children with diarrhoea (WHO-recommended dosing is 10 mg daily under 6 months and 20 mg daily from 6 months, for 10–14 days)

**Refer urgently** if the child has blood in the stool, high fever, is very drowsy or irritable, has sunken eyes, passes little urine, cannot drink, keeps vomiting, or is under 6 months of age.

**Counseling (Arabic):** "الإسهال مع دم أو حرارة — لازم دكتور. ORS أهم من وقف الإسهال للأطفال. إيموديوم للبالغين بس بدون دم."

## Scenario

> Child 2 years diarrhea — ORS + zinc, NO loperamide, physician if bloody.

A mother comes to a Lotus branch at night: her two-year-old has had watery diarrhoea since the morning. She asks for "Antinal and something to stop it, like Imodium".

**Model approach:** The pharmacist asks about blood, fever, vomiting, urine output and how alert the child is. With no red flags, the pharmacist explains that loperamide is not for young children, recommends ORS with clear preparation instructions and zinc, and advises continued feeding. The pharmacist explains that if blood appears, the child becomes drowsy or stops drinking, or the diarrhoea is not improving, the child must see a doctor straight away.

## Key takeaways

- Imodium (loperamide) is for adults with non-bloody diarrhoea only, short term
- Antinal (nifuroxazide) is widely misused over the counter and is not first line
- Normix (rifaximin) is prescribed for IBS-D and hepatic encephalopathy
- ORS is first line for children, with zinc and continued feeding
- Probiotics given with antibiotics can help reduce antibiotic-associated diarrhoea
- Bloody diarrhoea or fever needs a physician
