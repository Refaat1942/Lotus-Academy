## Learning objectives

After completing this lesson, you will be able to:

1. Explain the benefits and risks of hormone replacement therapy (HRT) in plain language
2. Identify HRT products in the Egyptian market such as Premarin, Activelle, Femoston, estradiol patches and Ovestin cream
3. Counsel on the risk of venous thromboembolism (VTE) and breast cancer with HRT
4. Describe non-hormonal alternatives such as SSRIs and gabapentin and simple lifestyle measures
5. Screen for contraindications such as a history of breast cancer and recognise red flags that need referral

## 1. Menopause in brief

Menopause is the last menstrual period, confirmed after 12 months without periods; in most women it happens around the early fifties. The years before it (perimenopause) can bring irregular periods and symptoms. Common symptoms are **hot flushes and night sweats** (vasomotor symptoms), sleep problems, mood changes, joint aches, and **vaginal dryness, discomfort with intercourse and urinary symptoms** (urogenital atrophy). After menopause the risk of osteoporosis rises.

Many Egyptian women see these symptoms as something to bear in silence. A respectful conversation at the counter can be the first time she hears that effective, safe options exist and that her doctor can help.

## 2. HRT products in Egypt

| Type | Brand | Components |
|---|---|---|
| Oral CEE | Premarin | Conjugated estrogens |
| Combined | Activelle, Femoston | Estrogen + progestogen |
| Patch | Estraderm | Transdermal estradiol |
| Local | Ovestin cream | Vaginal estriol |

HRT is a prescription medicine started by a doctor after an assessment. Availability of brands changes, so check the current Lotus product list.

## 3. Indications and principles

HRT is used for **moderate to severe vasomotor symptoms** and for **urogenital atrophy**. Key principles:

- Use the **lowest effective dose for the shortest duration** needed to control symptoms, with a review by the doctor at least once a year.
- **If the uterus is present, a progestogen must be added** to estrogen to protect the lining of the womb (endometrium) from hyperplasia and cancer. This is why combined products like Activelle and Femoston exist.
- **After a hysterectomy**, estrogen-only HRT is usually possible.
- **Local vaginal estrogen** (for example Ovestin cream) treats vaginal dryness and urinary symptoms with very low absorption, and can be used alongside or instead of systemic HRT, as the doctor decides.

## 4. Risks and contraindications

**Risks:** VTE (blood clots) and stroke, mainly with oral estrogen; and a small increase in breast cancer risk with longer use of combined HRT. **Transdermal estrogen (patch or gel) carries a lower VTE risk than oral estrogen.**

Contraindications and situations needing specialist review include:

- Current or past **breast cancer**, or other estrogen-dependent cancer
- **Undiagnosed vaginal bleeding**
- Current or previous VTE, or a known clotting disorder
- Active liver disease
- Pregnancy
- Untreated endometrial hyperplasia, recent heart attack or stroke

**Counselling (Arabic):** "العلاج الهرموني يفيد الهبات الساخنة بس ليه مخاطر — أقصر مدة وأقل جرعة. اللصقة ممكن تكون أأمن من الحبوب للجلطات."

## 5. Non-hormonal options

For women who cannot or prefer not to use HRT, doctors may prescribe non-hormonal options: **SSRIs or SNRIs** (for example venlafaxine) or **gabapentin** for hot flushes. Some antidepressants such as paroxetine and fluoxetine interact with tamoxifen, so a woman on tamoxifen after breast cancer needs her specialist to choose.

Lifestyle advice the pharmacist can give: light layered clothing, a fan, avoiding triggers such as spicy food, caffeine and alcohol, regular exercise, stopping smoking, and maintaining a healthy weight. Vaginal moisturisers and lubricants are useful OTC options for dryness. Evidence for herbal products is limited; some, such as black cohosh, have been linked to liver problems, and products containing plant estrogens may not be suitable after breast cancer. Discuss any supplement with the doctor. Calcium and vitamin D, exercise and avoiding smoking support bone health.

## 6. Red flags and privacy

Refer promptly for **any vaginal bleeding after menopause** or unexpected bleeding on HRT, a new breast lump or nipple change, calf pain or swelling, chest pain or breathlessness, sudden severe headache, or weakness of the face or limbs. These are not issues to manage at the counter.

Menopause can be a sensitive subject. Use neutral words, offer a private space, and avoid jokes about age. Some women prefer to speak to a female member of staff; arrange this when possible.

## Scenario

> A woman aged 52, who had a hysterectomy some years ago, says hot flushes are ruining her sleep and asks for "the hormone pills her friend uses".

Model approach: The pharmacist listens, asks about her symptoms, medical history (breast cancer, clots, liver disease, migraine) and current medicines, and explains that HRT is a prescription decision. Because she has no uterus, estrogen-only HRT may be an option to discuss with her physician, and a patch may carry a lower clot risk than tablets. The pharmacist explains that her friend's combined product may not be the right one for her, offers lifestyle tips, and reminds her that HRT needs an annual review with her doctor.

## Key takeaways

- Use HRT at the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration, with at least annual review.
- Patches carry a lower VTE risk than oral estrogen.
- Add a progestogen if the uterus is present; estrogen-only is possible after hysterectomy.
- Premarin and Activelle are available in Egypt; Ovestin cream is local vaginal estriol.
- Non-hormonal options include SSRIs and gabapentin, prescribed by a doctor.
- A history of breast cancer is a contraindication.
- Postmenopausal bleeding always needs referral.
