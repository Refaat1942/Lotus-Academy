## 1. Contraceptive methods available in Egypt

Family planning products are among the most frequent women's health requests in a Lotus branch. Some are bought regularly by the same customer, some are asked for urgently, and some are prescribed by a doctor or a family-planning clinic. The pharmacist's role is to make sure the method is used correctly and safely, to spot interactions and contraindications, and to refer when a doctor's assessment is needed.

| Method | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| COC (combined oral contraceptive) | Microgynon, Yasmin, Marvelon | Daily pill; estrogen + progestogen |
| POP (progestogen-only pill) | Micronor | Progestin only; option when estrogen is not suitable |
| Emergency | Postinor-2 (levonorgestrel) | Within 72 hours; the sooner the better |
| IUD | Copper IUD, Mirena (limited availability) | LARC; inserted by a doctor |
| Implant | Implanon (limited availability) | LARC; 3 years |
| Injectable | Depo-Provera | Every 3 months |

Availability of some products (for example Mirena and Implanon) can be limited, so check stock and the current Lotus product list before promising a product. Always check the leaflet of the exact brand, because dosing and missed-pill instructions differ between products.

## 2. Choosing a method: what the pharmacist checks

Choosing a method is a decision between the woman and her doctor, but the pharmacist often sees her first and most often. Before supplying or advising on a hormonal method, check:

- **Current medicines**, especially enzyme inducers such as rifampicin, carbamazepine, phenytoin and the herbal remedy Hypericum perforatum.
- **Combined pill contraindications**: a history of thrombosis (blood clots), migraine with aura, smoking at age 35 or over, high or uncontrolled blood pressure, a history of breast cancer, or recent childbirth (especially while breastfeeding in the first weeks). If any of these apply, refer to the doctor; a progestogen-only or non-hormonal method may be more suitable.
- **Breastfeeding**: estrogen can reduce milk supply, so progestogen-only methods are usually preferred for breastfeeding mothers (see Lesson 8.3).
- **Possibility of pregnancy**: if a period is late, suggest a pregnancy test before starting.

## 3. Taking the pill and missed-pill rules

The pill must be taken at the **same time every day**. Linking it to a daily habit (after brushing teeth or with dinner) helps.

The original Lotus rule, which matches the leaflets of many combined pills: **less than 12 hours late — take the missed pill immediately** and continue as normal. **More than 12 hours late — follow the leaflet instructions, and use backup contraception (condoms) for 7 days.** If pills were missed in the first week of a pack after unprotected sex, or more than one pill was missed, emergency contraception may be needed: assess and refer as appropriate.

Important differences:

- **Traditional progestogen-only pills** such as Micronor have a much shorter window (about 3 hours). Late by more than this, follow the leaflet and use backup for 2 days.
- Different brands have different instructions, so always read the instructions on the pack with the customer.
- Vomiting within a few hours of a pill or severe diarrhoea can count as a missed pill.

**Counselling (Arabic):** "الحبوب لازم نفس الساعة يومياً. لو نسيتي قرص، شوفي التعليمات على العبوة. الريفامبيسين بيقلل فعالية منع الحمل."

## 4. Emergency contraception

Postinor-2 contains levonorgestrel and should be taken **within 72 hours** of unprotected sex; it works better the sooner it is taken. Explain that it is not meant for regular use, does not protect for the rest of the cycle, and that if the next period is more than about a week late, a pregnancy test is needed. If she vomits within about 3 hours, she needs advice about another dose. A copper IUD fitted by a doctor is another emergency option. Women using enzyme-inducing medicines need specialist advice because levonorgestrel may be less effective. Follow the current Egyptian Drug Authority and Ministry of Health rules and the Lotus SOP on supply.

## 5. The rifampicin interaction

Rifampicin is a strong liver-enzyme inducer. It **reduces the efficacy** of combined pills, progestogen-only pills, implants and levonorgestrel emergency contraception, and the effect continues for weeks after the course ends. Methods that are not affected include the **copper IUD**, the levonorgestrel IUD and the **Depo-Provera injection**. A woman on rifampicin, for example for tuberculosis, should be referred to her doctor to agree a suitable method and use condoms in the meantime.

## 6. LARC versus daily pills

| Feature | LARC (IUD, implant) | Daily pills |
|---|---|---|
| Daily action needed | No | Yes, same time daily |
| Typical effectiveness | Most effective reversible methods | Lower in real life because of missed pills |
| Fitted by | Doctor or trained clinician | Self-administered |
| Duration | Years (implant 3 years) | Each pack |
| Return of fertility | Soon after removal | Soon after stopping |

The injectable sits in between: one injection every 3 months, but return of fertility can be delayed after stopping.

## 7. Red flags and privacy

Refer urgently if a woman on hormonal contraception reports calf pain or swelling, chest pain or breathlessness, a severe or unusual headache, vision changes, yellowing of the skin, or, with an IUD, severe lower abdominal pain, fever or a possible pregnancy.

Contraception is personal. Lower your voice, offer the quieter end of the counter, avoid comments or judgement, and do not discuss the product in front of other customers. Bag products discreetly, and keep WhatsApp and delivery orders confidential.

## Scenario

> A woman with tuberculosis on rifampicin asks for her usual pack of Microgynon.

Model approach: The pharmacist checks her medicines and explains privately that rifampicin reduces the pill's effect, so there is a real risk of contraceptive failure. The pharmacist does not tell her to stop rifampicin. Instead, the pharmacist advises condoms now and refers her to her doctor or family-planning clinic to discuss a method that rifampicin does not affect, such as a copper IUD or Depo-Provera, and to plan how long to continue extra precautions after the TB treatment ends.

## Key takeaways

- Microgynon is widely available; Yasmin and Marvelon are other common combined pills, and Micronor is a progestogen-only pill.
- Postinor-2 is emergency contraception: within 72 hours, the sooner the better.
- Rifampicin reduces hormonal contraceptive efficacy; copper IUD and Depo-Provera are not affected.
- LARC methods are the most effective reversible methods.
- Missed pills may need backup contraception; follow the product leaflet.
- Combined pill contraindications include thrombosis and migraine with aura.
- Protect privacy and refer red flags immediately.
