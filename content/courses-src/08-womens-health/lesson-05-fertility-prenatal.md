After completing this lesson, you will be able to:

1. List the main fertility medicines, such as clomiphene and gonadotropins, and their role
2. Identify prenatal supplements available in Egypt, such as folic acid, Pregnacare, Materna and Feroglobin

4. Explain the pharmacist's supporting role with IVF medicines: storage, timing and warning signs

## 1. Fertility medicines

Fertility treatment is prescribed and monitored by a gynaecologist or fertility specialist. The pharmacist's role is to dispense correctly, protect the cold chain, teach injection technique and timing, and recognise warning signs.

| Drug | Brand | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Clomiphene | Clomid | Ovulation induction |
| Letrozole | Femara | Off-label ovulation induction |
| Gonadotropins | Gonal-F, Menogon | IVF and ovarian stimulation |
| hCG trigger | Pregnyl, Ovitrelle | Trigger ovulation |
| Progesterone | Utrogestan, Cyclogest | Luteal phase support |

Practical points for the counter:

- **Storage**: many injectable fertility medicines need refrigeration or have specific storage rules after opening. Follow the leaflet, keep the cold chain during delivery, and explain home storage.
- **Timing**: the hCG trigger injection is usually given at an exact time set by the clinic, because egg collection or intercourse timing depends on it. Make sure the patient understands the time and has the product in good time.
- **Technique**: show how to use pens or prepare injections, according to the leaflet and clinic instructions, and how to dispose of needles safely.
- **Ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS)**: severe abdominal pain or swelling, nausea and vomiting, rapid weight gain, breathlessness or passing little urine during or after stimulation need urgent medical contact.

These medicines are never for self-medication. A woman who asks for Clomid "because it worked for a relative" should be referred for proper assessment; using fertility medicines without monitoring can cause multiple pregnancy and OHSS.

## 2. Prenatal supplements in Egypt

| Product | Contents |
|---|---|
| Folic acid | 400–500 mcg — preconception |
| Pregnacare | Prenatal multivitamin with folic acid; some variants (for example Pregnacare Plus) add omega-3 DHA |
| Materna | Prenatal multivitamin |
| Feroglobin | Iron + folic acid |

**Folic acid:** 400 mcg daily, starting at least 1 month before conception and continuing through the first 12 weeks of pregnancy, reduces the risk of neural tube defects (such as spina bifida). Because many pregnancies are unplanned, any woman who may become pregnant benefits from taking it. **A higher dose of 5 mg** is prescribed for women at high risk, for example after a previous pregnancy affected by a neural tube defect, with a family history, with diabetes, with a high body weight, or on certain antiepileptic medicines. The doctor decides the dose.

**Iron** is often needed in pregnancy, especially if a blood test shows anaemia; the doctor guides the dose. Iron can cause constipation or dark stools; taking it with vitamin C-rich foods and away from tea, coffee, milk and calcium supplements improves absorption. **DHA** (an omega-3 fatty acid) supports the baby's brain and eye development. **Iodine** is important for the baby's brain development and is included in many prenatal products.

Avoid high-dose **vitamin A** (retinol) supplements and fish liver oil in pregnancy, and do not combine several multivitamins, because of the risk of excess vitamin A. Prenatal vitamins support but **do not replace a healthy diet**.

**Counselling (Arabic):** "حمض الفوليك يبدأ قبل الحمل بشهر على الأقل. فيتامينات الحمل مش بديل أكل صحي. أدوية الخصوبة محتاجة متابعة طبية دقيقة."

## 3. Preconception medicine review

A woman planning pregnancy is the best moment to review her medicines. Medicines such as **valproate**, isotretinoin, ACE inhibitors, warfarin and methotrexate need review by the prescriber before conception (see Lesson 8.2). The key rule: she should **not stop a prescribed medicine on her own**, especially an antiepileptic, because uncontrolled seizures are dangerous for mother and baby. Refer for a planned switch.

## 4. Privacy, sensitivity and red flags

Infertility can be painful and carries stigma in many families. Speak quietly, avoid questions like "why no children yet?", never comment on age or the number of attempts, and do not discuss treatment in front of others. Keep prescriptions and delivery details confidential, and use discreet bags.

Refer urgently for OHSS symptoms, bleeding or severe abdominal pain in early pregnancy (possible ectopic pregnancy or miscarriage), and for fainting or shoulder-tip pain with a positive pregnancy test.

## Scenario

> A woman with epilepsy who takes valproate tells the pharmacist that she and her husband are planning a pregnancy and asks which vitamins to start.

Model approach: The pharmacist thanks her for sharing the plan and explains, calmly, that valproate carries a serious risk to the baby, so her neurologist must review the treatment urgently, before she tries to conceive. The pharmacist stresses that she must not stop valproate on her own. Folic acid should start now, and her doctor may prescribe the higher 5 mg dose because she takes an antiepileptic. The pharmacist advises effective contraception until the switch is completed and documents the conversation according to the Lotus SOP.

## Key takeaways

- Folic acid 400 mcg daily from at least 1 month before conception to 12 weeks; 5 mg for high-risk women as prescribed.
- Clomid (clomiphene) is a common ovulation-induction medicine.
- IVF medicines are for specialist use only, with careful storage, timing and monitoring.
- Pregnacare is a popular prenatal multivitamin in Egypt; check which variant contains DHA.
- Valproate must be reviewed by the specialist before pregnancy; never stopped abruptly.
- DHA supports fetal brain development; iron is given according to need.
- Fertility medicines are never self-medicated.
