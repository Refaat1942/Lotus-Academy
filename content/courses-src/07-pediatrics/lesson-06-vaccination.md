## Learning objectives

After completing this lesson, you will be able to:

1. Describe the key ages of the **Egyptian MOH immunization schedule**
2. Identify the **key vaccines** given from birth to 18 months
3. Counsel on **vaccine myths** in Arabic with respect and evidence
4. Apply **cold chain** awareness for vaccines in the pharmacy
5. Support **referral to vaccination centres** and counsel on side effects

---

## 1. Egypt MOH schedule (key ages)

The Ministry of Health and Population provides compulsory childhood vaccines free of charge through health offices and vaccination centres, and every child has a vaccination card. The schedule is **updated from time to time**, so always check the child's card and the current MOH schedule before advising. The table below is a training summary of key ages.

| Age | Vaccines (verify against the current MOH card) |
|---|---|
| Birth (first days to weeks) | BCG, hepatitis B birth dose (HepB 0), oral polio zero dose |
| 2, 4, 6 months | Pentavalent (DPT + Hib + HepB), polio (OPV, with IPV as scheduled); PCV and rotavirus where included in the current MOH programme or offered privately |
| 12 months | MMR, first dose (older schedules gave measles at 9 months) |
| 18 months | DPT booster, MMR second dose, OPV |
| School age | Boosters per MOH updates |

Key vaccine names:

- **BCG** protects against severe forms of tuberculosis in young children.
- **Pentavalent** combines diphtheria, pertussis (whooping cough), tetanus, Haemophilus influenzae type b and hepatitis B.
- **PCV** is the **pneumococcal conjugate vaccine**, which protects against pneumococcal pneumonia, meningitis and blood infection.
- **Rotavirus vaccine** is an **oral** vaccine given in early infancy that protects against severe rotavirus diarrhoea. Its availability in the national programme should be confirmed against the current MOH schedule; it is also available privately.
- **MMR** protects against measles, mumps and rubella.

## 2. Pharmacy role

The pharmacy's role is to **counsel and refer**: explain the schedule, remind parents of missed doses, refer them to the health office or vaccination centre, and answer questions about side effects. Some pharmacies stock or participate in vaccination programmes following the current MOH rules and the Lotus SOP. Questions about whether a sick or immunocompromised child should be vaccinated, or about a catch-up schedule, are referred to the vaccination centre or the doctor.

**Cold chain (2–8°C).** Vaccines lose effect if they get too warm or freeze. In the pharmacy:

- Keep vaccines in a dedicated pharmacy fridge at **2–8°C**, never in the door and never with food or drinks.
- **Never freeze** vaccines unless the manufacturer specifies it; a frozen vaccine may be damaged.
- Record the fridge temperature as the SOP requires and act on any deviation.
- Check expiry dates, and when a parent buys a private vaccine, give it in a cool bag and advise taking it **straight** to the clinic.

## 3. Counselling on side effects

Common reactions are **mild and normal**: a little fever, redness, swelling or tenderness at the injection site, and irritability for a day or two. Paracetamol by weight can be used if the child is uncomfortable (see Lesson 7.2). Seek medical help urgently for difficulty breathing, swelling of the face or lips, a widespread rash, a seizure, a very high fever, or inconsolable crying for hours.

## 4. Vaccine myths and respectful counselling

Common myths heard at the counter: "vaccines cause autism", "too many vaccines overload the immune system", "natural infection is better", "my child is healthy so he does not need it". The claimed **link between vaccines and autism is scientifically false**: it came from a retracted study and has been disproven by large studies.

A respectful approach:

1. **Listen** to the concern without judgement and ask what the parent has heard.
2. **Share clear facts** in simple words: the diseases are serious, and the vaccines are carefully tested and monitored.
3. Explain **herd immunity**: when most children are vaccinated, the community is protected, including babies too young to be vaccinated and children who cannot be vaccinated.
4. **Refer** to the vaccination centre or pediatrician for further discussion.
5. **Document** the conversation as the Lotus SOP requires and keep the door open: respectful persistence, never pressure.

**Counselling (Arabic):** "التطعيمات بتحمي من أمراض خطيرة — الآثار الجانبية خفيفة زي حرارة وسخونة مكان الحقنة. الربط بين التطعيم والتوحد غلط علمياً."

*English meaning:* "Vaccines protect against serious diseases. Side effects are mild, like fever and warmth at the injection site. The link between vaccination and autism is scientifically wrong."

## Scenario

> A father says he will not give his daughter the MMR vaccine because he read on social media that it causes autism.

**Model approach:** The pharmacist thanks him for caring about his daughter's safety and asks what exactly worried him. The pharmacist explains calmly that the autism claim has been studied in very large numbers of children and is false, that measles can be a serious disease, and that vaccinating protects his daughter and others through herd immunity. The pharmacist offers to answer more questions, suggests discussing it with the pediatrician or the vaccination centre, documents the conversation as the SOP requires, and invites him to come back. The pharmacist does not argue, mock or pressure.

## Key takeaways

- Follow the current MOH schedule and the child's card; pentavalent is given at 2, 4 and 6 months
- MMR is given at 12 and 18 months in current MOH schedules; verify against the card
- BCG and the hepatitis B birth dose are given at birth
- The autism link is debunked; counsel with respect and evidence
- The cold chain (2–8°C, never frozen) is essential
- The pharmacy's role is to counsel, remind and refer to vaccination centres
- Mild local reactions and low fever are normal; allergy signs need urgent care
