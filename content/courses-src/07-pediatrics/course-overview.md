## Course description

This course is for pharmacists and pharmacy staff in Lotus pharmacies in Egypt who serve parents and caregivers every day: the mother asking about a fever at midnight, the father with a new antibiotic prescription for a toddler, the grandmother who wants "a strong cough syrup" for a three-year-old. Children are not small adults. Their doses depend on weight, their formulations differ, and some medicines that are routine for adults are unsafe for them. Small measuring mistakes can become real overdoses.

The course covers pediatric dosing and formulations, common pediatric antibiotics and antipyretics, cough and cold care and what to avoid in young children, gastrointestinal care including ORS, zinc and colic, vitamins and iron, and the pharmacy's role in supporting the Egyptian Ministry of Health vaccination schedule. Every lesson includes worked examples, counselling phrases in Arabic and English, clear red flags that require referral, and a realistic Egyptian-pharmacy scenario.

Each lesson takes about 90 minutes and ends with a 10-question quiz; a 20-question final exam covers the whole course. Doses in this course are training examples. In practice always follow the product leaflet, the prescriber's instructions, the current rules of the Egyptian Drug Authority and the Ministry of Health and the Lotus SOP. Pharmacists counsel and refer; they do not diagnose.

## Learning outcomes

1. Calculate **weight-based dosing** and select appropriate formulations and measuring devices
2. Identify **pediatric antibiotic** suspensions such as Augmentin and cephalosporin syrups, and counsel on antipyretics safely
3. Advise on **cough/cold medicine avoidance** in children under 6 years and recognise respiratory red flags
4. Counsel on **ORS, zinc, colic and probiotic** products and recognise dehydration that needs urgent care
5. Counsel on **vitamin D and iron** supplements, including safe storage and iron poisoning risk
6. Support **Egyptian vaccination schedule** counselling, myth correction and referral to vaccination centres
