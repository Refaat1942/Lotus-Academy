## 1. Why cough and cold medicines are different in children

Most coughs and colds in children are caused by viruses and get better on their own within one to two weeks. Over-the-counter cough and cold syrups have **little evidence of benefit** in young children, and some ingredients can cause serious side effects such as drowsiness, agitation, a fast heart rate or slowed breathing. Accidental overdose is also more likely when several combination products are given together.

## 2. Avoid in young children

| Medicine or group | Advice |
|---|---|
| **Codeine** cough syrups | Must not be used in children. International regulators contraindicate codeine for cough in children under 12, and it is not recommended up to 18 because it can slow breathing. Follow the current rules of the Egyptian Drug Authority |
| **Oral decongestants** (e.g. pseudoephedrine, phenylephrine) | Avoid under 6 years |
| **Sedating antihistamines** in cold combinations | Use with caution; avoid in young children and never use to make a child sleep |
| Combination "cough and cold" syrups | Avoid under 6; check every product for hidden paracetamol or antihistamine |
| Decongestant nasal sprays for adults | Not for young children unless prescribed |

## 3. Appropriate care

Supportive care is the first line for an uncomplicated cold:

- **Saline nasal drops or spray** loosen mucus and are safe from birth. They are first-line for a blocked nose.
- **Bulb (nasal) suction** for infants, gently, after saline drops, especially before feeds and sleep.
- **Honey for cough only in children over 1 year**, for example a small spoonful before bed. Never give honey to babies under 1 year because of the risk of infant botulism.
- **Fluids** and frequent small feeds are supportive and help keep mucus thin.
- **Humidified, comfortable air.** Do not leave bowls of boiling water near children because of burn risk.
- **Paracetamol by weight for fever** or discomfort, as in Lesson 7.2.

## 4. Medicines a doctor may prescribe

| Drug | Pediatric use |
|---|---|
| Salbutamol (Ventolin) nebuliser or MDI with spacer | Wheeze, only as directed by a physician |
| Montelukast | Asthma prevention (prophylaxis), prescription only; granules are licensed from 6 months |
| Desloratadine | Allergy symptoms, from the minimum age stated on the product leaflet |

**Ventolin with a spacer:** Children cannot coordinate pressing an inhaler and breathing in. A spacer, with a face mask for young children, delivers the medicine much better. Teach the parent to shake the inhaler, attach it to the spacer, fit the mask over nose and mouth, press once and let the child take several normal breaths, then repeat as prescribed. If the child needs the reliever more often than the doctor said, or it does not help, they need urgent medical care.

**Montelukast** is a preventer, not a reliever for an attack. Parents should report any change in sleep, mood or behaviour to the doctor.

## 5. Red flags: refer to a doctor or emergency care

- **Wheeze** or a whistling sound when breathing: needs a physician
- Fast breathing, the skin between the ribs pulling in, nostrils flaring, grunting
- Blue or grey lips, pauses in breathing, or the child too breathless to feed or speak
- A harsh noise on breathing in (stridor), or drooling and unable to swallow
- Any baby under 3 months with cough or breathing difficulty, or fever with a cough in an infant
- Cough lasting more than three weeks, coughing fits followed by vomiting or a "whoop", or a possibly inhaled object
- The child is very drowsy, not drinking or shows dehydration

**Counselling (Arabic):** "شراب السعال بالكوديين ممنوع للأطفال. المحلول الملحي للأنف آمن. العسل للسعال بعد سنة."

*English meaning:* "Codeine cough syrup is forbidden for children. Saline for the nose is safe. Honey for cough after one year."

## Scenario

> A mother asks for "a strong cough syrup" for her 3-year-old, who has had a runny nose and cough for three days.

**Model approach:** The pharmacist asks about fever, breathing, feeding, wheeze, sleep and how long the cough has lasted, and watches the child's breathing if present. With no red flags, the pharmacist explains that cough syrups are not recommended under 6 years and may cause side effects, and recommends saline nasal drops, plenty of fluids, a small spoon of honey before bed (the child is over 1 year), and paracetamol by weight if the child is uncomfortable with fever. The pharmacist explains that if the child wheezes, breathes fast, struggles to breathe, stops drinking, or the cough lasts beyond three weeks, a physician must examine the child.

## Key takeaways

- No codeine cough syrups for children
- Saline nasal drops are safe and first-line for a blocked nose
- Honey for cough only in children over 1 year
- Avoid oral decongestants and cough/cold combinations under 6 years
- Ventolin is used only as prescribed, with a spacer (and mask for young children)
- Wheeze or difficult breathing always needs a physician
- Avoid sedating cold combinations in young children
