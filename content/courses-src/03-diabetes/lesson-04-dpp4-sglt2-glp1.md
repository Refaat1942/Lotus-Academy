## 1. Drug class comparison

| Class | Examples (Egypt) | Key benefit | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| DPP-4i | Januvia (sitagliptin), Galvus (vildagliptin) | Weight neutral; oral | Pancreatitis (rare) |
| SGLT2i | Forxiga (dapagliflozin), Jardiance (empagliflozin) | HF, CKD, CV benefit | Genital mycotic infections, DKA |
| GLP-1 RA | Ozempic (semaglutide), Victoza (liraglutide) | Weight loss, CV benefit | GI upset, injections |

**DPP-4 inhibitors** block the enzyme dipeptidyl peptidase-4, which normally breaks down the body's own incretin hormones (GLP-1 and GIP). Higher incretin levels increase insulin release and reduce glucagon **only when glucose is raised**, so DPP-4 inhibitors alone rarely cause hypoglycaemia. They are oral, once or twice daily, weight neutral and well tolerated, and are often used as an add-on to metformin, including in fixed-dose combinations. Advise the patient to seek care for severe persistent abdominal pain (possible pancreatitis). Vildagliptin may require liver function monitoring as stated in its leaflet, and doses of some DPP-4 inhibitors are reduced in kidney impairment.

**SGLT2 inhibitors** block the sodium-glucose co-transporter 2 in the **proximal tubule** of the kidney, so glucose is lost in the urine (this is expected, and urine tests will show glucose). Beyond glucose lowering, large trials such as DAPA-HF (dapagliflozin) and EMPEROR-Reduced (empagliflozin) showed reduced hospitalisation and cardiovascular death in heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF), **with or without diabetes**, and other trials showed slowing of chronic kidney disease. That is why cardiologists and nephrologists prescribe them to people who may not even have diabetes.

**GLP-1 receptor agonists** mimic GLP-1: they increase glucose-dependent insulin release, reduce glucagon, slow stomach emptying and reduce appetite. The result is good glucose lowering, significant **weight loss**, and proven cardiovascular benefit for some agents. They are injected — semaglutide (Ozempic) **once weekly**, liraglutide (Victoza) once daily.

## 3. Counselling on SGLT2 inhibitors

- **Genital mycotic infections** (thrush) are the most common side effect: keep the genital area clean and dry, and seek advice if itching or discharge occurs. Refer urgently for severe pain, swelling or fever in the genital/perineal area.
- **Volume depletion**: increased urination can cause dehydration, dizziness or low blood pressure, especially in the elderly, in hot Egyptian summers and in people on diuretics. Drink enough water unless the doctor has restricted fluids.
- **Ketoacidosis (DKA)**, sometimes with near-normal glucose, is rare but serious. Red flags: nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, unusual tiredness, fast breathing — seek urgent care.
- **Sick-day rules**: during acute illness with vomiting, diarrhoea, poor intake, or before major surgery, the doctor may advise temporarily stopping the SGLT2 inhibitor. Patients should know to ask.
- Do not stop the medicine just because glucose improves; in heart failure or CKD it is given for organ protection.

## 4. Counselling on GLP-1 receptor agonists

- Nausea is common at the start and usually improves; the dose is increased gradually. Eat smaller meals and stop eating when full.
- Inject subcutaneously into the abdomen, thigh or upper arm, rotating sites. Use a new needle each time and never share pens.
- Store unopened pens in the refrigerator (2–8 °C), never frozen; in-use storage follows the leaflet.
- Seek care for severe persistent abdominal pain (possible pancreatitis) or signs of dehydration from vomiting.
- These products are contraindicated in people with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN2 — ask before first supply and refer if relevant.
- Dispense only against a valid prescription; do not supply for cosmetic weight loss on request.

## Scenario

> *T2DM patient with heart failure EF 35%. On metformin only. Physician adds Forxiga.*

**Model approach.** This is an excellent guideline-directed choice: an ejection fraction of 35% means HFrEF, where dapagliflozin reduces hospitalisation and cardiovascular death regardless of glucose effect. The pharmacist explains the heart benefit in simple words, so the patient understands why the medicine matters even if glucose becomes normal. Counselling covers hydration (within any fluid limit the cardiologist has set), genital hygiene and warning signs of infection, dizziness on standing, DKA red flags, and sick-day rules — contact the doctor if vomiting or unable to eat or drink. The pharmacist checks for diuretics in the regimen and advises: "Do not stop Forxiga if your sugar becomes normal; it is protecting your heart."

## Key takeaways

- SGLT2i give HF and CKD benefit regardless of diabetes status (DAPA-HF and EMPEROR-Reduced evidence in HF).
- Forxiga and Jardiance are increasingly prescribed in Egypt; counsel on genital hygiene, hydration and sick-day rules.
- Ozempic is a once-weekly GLP-1 RA with powerful weight and CV effects, but cost is a barrier.
- DPP-4i are a well-tolerated, weight-neutral oral add-on with low hypoglycaemia risk alone.
- Genital candidiasis counselling is essential for SGLT2i.
- Do not stop cardiorenal-protective therapy just because glucose normalises.
