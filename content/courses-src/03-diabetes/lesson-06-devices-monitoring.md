## 1. Blood glucose monitoring

Monitoring tells the patient and the doctor whether treatment is working and warns early about hypoglycaemia. Lotus pharmacies sell meters, strips, lancets and sensors every day, so the pharmacist is often the person who teaches patients how to use them.

| Device type | Examples Egypt | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Glucometers | Accu-Chek, OneTouch, Contour | Strips 1–3 EGP each |
| CGM | FreeStyle Libre (limited) | Sensor ~800–1200 EGP/month |
| HbA1c | Lab test every 3 months | Target <7% most adults |

Prices are approximate and change frequently; always check the current price. **Self-monitoring of blood glucose (SMBG)** with a meter gives a reading at one moment. Strips are an ongoing cost, so when choosing a meter, discuss the price and availability of its strips, not only the price of the meter, and make sure the strips match the meter model.

**HbA1c** reflects average glucose over about three months. It is usually measured every 3 months when treatment is being changed or the target is not met; the doctor may test less often (for example every 6 months) once the patient is stable at target. It is the main measure of long-term control.

**Continuous glucose monitoring (CGM)** uses a small sensor on the skin to measure glucose continuously and show trends and lows, including at night. It is especially useful for people on intensive insulin or with frequent or unrecognised hypoglycaemia. In Egypt, access is limited mainly by cost. Patients should follow the product instructions for sensor application, and confirm with a finger-prick reading when symptoms do not match the sensor value.

## 2. Monitoring schedule

| Therapy | SMBG frequency |
|---|---|
| Lifestyle/metformin only | Fasting few times/week |
| Sulfonylurea/insulin | Pre-meal + bedtime; PRN hypoglycemia |
| Intensive insulin | 4+ times daily |

The exact schedule is set by the doctor. Medicines that can cause hypoglycaemia (sulfonylureas and insulin) need **more frequent** checking, and any time symptoms of hypoglycaemia occur, the patient should test if possible. Encourage patients to keep a logbook or app record with date, time, reading and notes (meal, exercise, illness) and to bring it to every visit.

## 3. Lancet technique and strip storage

- Wash hands with soap and warm water and dry them; sugar on the fingers can give a falsely high reading. Avoid alcohol wipes unless the area is fully dried.
- Use the **side of the fingertip**, not the pad, and rotate fingers to reduce pain.
- Use a new lancet each time and never share lancing devices.
- Apply the drop to the strip as the meter instructs; do not squeeze the finger hard.
- Store strips in their original **dry, capped vial**, close the cap immediately, keep away from heat, sun and humidity (not in the bathroom or a hot car), and do not use them after the expiry date or the period after opening stated on the vial.
- Use control solution or ask the pharmacy to check the meter when readings do not match how the patient feels.
- Dispose of lancets safely in a hard container.

## 4. Foot care & complications screening

Diabetes can reduce sensation in the feet (neuropathy) and blood flow, so a small injury may go unnoticed and become infected or develop into an ulcer.

**Daily foot care:**
- Check both feet every day, including between the toes and the soles (use a mirror or ask a family member).
- Wash daily in lukewarm water (test the temperature with the elbow), dry well between the toes, and moisturise but not between the toes.
- Avoid walking barefoot, including at home, at the mosque or on hot surfaces in summer; wear comfortable closed shoes and check inside them.
- Cut nails straight across; do not cut corns or calluses at home or use corn plasters without medical advice.
- **Refer promptly** for any wound, blister, colour change, swelling, warmth, pain or infection on the foot.

**Annual screening:** eye exam, foot exam, urine albumin, lipids, renal function. Blood pressure should be checked at every visit, and HbA1c as above.

**Counseling script (Arabic):**
> "افحص رجليك كل يوم — أي جرح صغير ممكن يكبر مع السكري. قيس السكر وسجل الأرقام في دفتر. التحليل التراكمي كل 3 شهور يقولنا التحكم عامل إزاي."

## 5. Integrated counselling

A complete diabetes consultation combines medicines, monitoring and lifestyle in one clear conversation. Use a simple checklist: what each medicine is for and when to take it; side effects and red flags; hypoglycaemia recognition and the rule of 15 if on a sulfonylurea or insulin; how and when to test glucose; diet, walking and weight; foot care; and the next HbA1c and annual checks. Check understanding by asking the patient to repeat the key points ("teach-back").

## Scenario

> *Newly diagnosed T2DM. Physician starts metformin + Diamicron MR. Patient asks pharmacy for complete counseling.*

**Model approach.** The pharmacist uses the integrated checklist:

- Metformin with food, starting at the prescribed dose; mild stomach upset usually settles.
- Diamicron MR once daily before or with breakfast; never skip breakfast after taking it.
- Hypoglycaemia signs (sweating, shaking, hunger, confusion) and the rule of 15; carry a source of sugar.
- SMBG: how to use the meter, when to test as the doctor advises, and keeping a log.
- Lifestyle: regular meals, fewer sweets and sugary drinks, smaller bread and rice portions, daily walking if the doctor agrees.
- Daily foot check and no barefoot walking.
- HbA1c follow-up in 3 months and annual eye, kidney and foot checks.

The pharmacist checks understanding with teach-back and invites the patient to return with questions.

## Key takeaways

- Glucometer strips are an ongoing cost; counsel on meter and strip choice together.
- HbA1c every 3 months (or as the doctor advises once stable) is the gold standard of long-term control.
- Sulfonylureas and insulin need more frequent SMBG.
- Daily foot self-examination is critical; avoid walking barefoot.
- CGM is limited by cost in Egypt but valuable for intensive insulin and frequent hypoglycaemia.
- Integrated counselling combines medicines, monitoring and lifestyle; this completes Course 3.
