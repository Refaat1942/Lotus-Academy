## 1. Principles of a good regimen

A regimen is a short list of steps the customer can really follow. In a busy Lotus branch, the best regimen usually has:

- **One core product** for the main problem (for example, a hair-loss line, a dandruff shampoo or a damaged-ends shampoo).
- **One or two supportive steps** only if they make sense (a conditioner, a mask).
- **A clear time frame**: how long to continue and when to come back.
- **A safety net**: the red flags that mean "see a doctor".

All product facts below come from the **Lotus product notes**. General advice on adherence and referral is general hair-and-scalp care knowledge and is not a product claim.

## 3. Explaining treatment duration honestly

The Lotus notes for Asepta state that hair-loss cases need 3 months of treatment and that the patient should not stop before this period. Neoptide is listed with a 3-month box. Use these facts to set a realistic plan, in three steps:

1. **Say the period out loud before the sale.** "The notes say hair loss needs about 3 months of regular use before you decide."
2. **Explain what it is not.** It is the minimum time to continue, not a promise of results at the end.
3. **Add a safety net.** "If the loss is sudden, in patches, increasing, or you have other symptoms, see a doctor, and we continue to support your hair care."

Wording to avoid: "guaranteed", "stops completely", "regrows in two months" as your own promise. If the customer has heard the ANP statements, you may say what the Lotus notes state, and then return to the 3-month plan.

### Seasonal use as stated in the notes

For the Elution shampoo, the notes suggest using it in the 3 months of the summer period, to protect against winter dandruff in people who suffer heavy winter dandruff. Say exactly that, and refer the customer to the label for how often to use it. Do not invent a schedule.

## 4. Model consultation dialogues

### Dialogue A: Shedding after childbirth

> Customer: "My hair is falling a lot since the baby."
> Pharmacist: "Congratulations. Shedding after childbirth is common. Is it all over or in patches? Any tiredness or other symptoms?"
> Customer: "All over. I feel tired like any new mother."
> Pharmacist: "Thank you. I suggest one core product from the hair-loss line. The Lotus notes list the Asepta ampoules and lotion for hair loss and say it needs about 3 months. Please read the leaflet, and if the loss gets worse or you have other symptoms, see your doctor."

### Dialogue B: Dandruff every winter

> Customer: "Every winter I get heavy dandruff."
> Pharmacist: "Is the scalp red or painful, or only flaky and itchy?"
> Customer: "Flaky and itchy."
> Pharmacist: "The Ducray notes list the Elution shampoo for people with heavy winter dandruff and suggest using it in the 3 summer months as protection. For flakes now, there are dandruff shampoos like Kelual in the notes. Follow the label for each."

### Dialogue C: A doctor-diagnosed scalp condition

> Customer: "My dermatologist said I have psoriasis on the scalp. Do you have a shampoo?"
> Pharmacist: "Yes, the Lotus notes list Kertoyl as a shampoo for psoriasis. Please keep following your doctor's plan, and come back if you have any questions."

## 5. Ethical add-ons

An add-on is ethical when it meets a real need that the customer mentioned, and unethical when it is only about the basket size.

| Situation | Reasonable add-on | Not reasonable |
|---|---|---|
| Customer buys a hair-loss shampoo and mentions dry ends | Conditioner or Mask, once | Three extra products |
| Customer with split ends buying Nutriceret shampoo | Nutriceret Mask or emulsion | A hair-loss ampoule |
| Customer with flakes only | The dandruff shampoo; maybe the ampoules if the flakes keep returning | A hair-loss line |
| Customer who asks about lashes or nails | The matching item once, with honest wording | Any promise of growth |

Rule: **offer once, explain why, and accept "no"**. Never push a product to a customer in obvious financial difficulty. Prefer a sensible core product and follow-up over a full basket.

## 6. Objections

| Objection | Suggested reply |
|---|---|
| "It is expensive for 3 months." | "I understand. Let's choose the one core product that matches your main need, and we can review after the first box. The notes say stopping early is the usual reason it does not work." |
| "I will use it for a month and stop." | "That is your choice. The notes say hair loss needs 3 months, so a month is too short to judge. If you must stop, please see a doctor if the loss continues." |
| "My friend's hair grew in two weeks." | "Everyone's hair is different and the cause matters. I cannot promise that. Let's plan an honest timeline." |
| "Is it natural, so safe?" | "The notes describe the ingredient as natural, and also state safety in pregnancy and breastfeeding. Any new product can still irritate, so stop and ask if you get burning or redness." |
| "Can I use two brands together?" | "It is better to use one routine. Combining several can confuse the picture. Choose one line, and ask your doctor if you want to combine." |
| "Do I need the whole line?" | "No. The core product first. Add steps only if a real need appears." |

## 7. When to refer, and closing the consultation

Refer to a doctor or dermatologist for: sudden or rapid loss, patchy loss, a child with patches, painful or very red, thick, silvery or oozing scalp, loss with general symptoms or after a new medicine, and no improvement after correct use for a reasonable time. A pregnant or breastfeeding customer should also check with her doctor about any product she uses, in addition to what the notes state.

End every consultation with three things:

1. **The plan**: product, order of steps, how long.
2. **The date** to come back or call (for example, after the first box).
3. **The safety net**: the red flags.

Adherence tips to share: use the product on fixed days or a fixed time, set a phone reminder, take a photo of the parting once a month in the same light, and keep the box near the shampoo.

## Scenario

A man in his thirties, a delivery customer ordering over WhatsApp, asks for "the best hair-loss treatment" and adds a nail strengthener "while you are at it". His message says he has had slow thinning at the crown for years and that he will "try it for two weeks".

**Model approach:**

1. The pharmacist replies with two or three questions (pattern, scalp symptoms, medicines, duration) before sending anything.
2. He has gradual thinning, no pain, no patches. The pharmacist offers one core item from a hair-loss line (for example the Foltène Men shampoo, with the ampoules as optional).
3. The pharmacist explains that the notes describe hair needing months of regular use, that two weeks is too short to judge, and that a doctor check is wise for long-standing thinning.
4. The pharmacist adds the nail strengthener he requested, with honest wording, and nothing more.
5. The pharmacist writes the plan, sets a follow-up message in a few weeks, and shares the red flags.

## Key takeaways

- A good regimen has one core product, one or two supportive steps, a time frame and a safety net
- The brands are alternatives for the same need; do not stack three hair-loss lines
- State the 3-month period from the Lotus notes before the sale, as a time to continue and not as a guarantee
- Elution shampoo is seasonal per the notes: preferably in the 3 summer months to protect from winter dandruff
- Add-ons must answer a real need; offer once and accept "no"
- Meet objections with honest, calm answers and a follow-up plan
- Refer for sudden, patchy, painful, very red or persistent problems
