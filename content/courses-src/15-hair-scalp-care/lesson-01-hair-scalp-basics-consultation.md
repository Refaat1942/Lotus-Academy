## Learning objectives

After completing this lesson, you will be able to:

1. Describe the hair growth cycle in simple words and explain why results take months
2. Distinguish between shedding, thinning and breakage using a few questions
3. Tell dandruff, dry scalp and oily scalp apart and match each to the right type of product
4. Run a short, structured consultation for a customer who asks about hair or scalp
5. Recognise red flags that require referral to a doctor or dermatologist
6. Set honest expectations about timelines before recommending any product

---

## 1. How hair grows: the cycle in simple words

Every hair on the scalp follows its own cycle, and the hairs are not all in the same phase at the same time.

| Phase | What happens | Simple message for the customer |
|---|---|---|
| Growth phase (anagen) | The hair is actively growing from the root. This is the longest phase and lasts for years. | "Most of your hair is growing at any moment." |
| Transition phase (catagen) | Growth stops and the root shrinks. It is short. | "A small number of hairs are preparing to rest." |
| Resting and shedding phase (telogen) | The hair rests, then falls and a new hair starts under it. | "A hair that falls is normally replaced by a new one." |

Three practical points follow from this cycle:

- **Some daily shedding is normal.** Customers often panic when they see hair in the brush or the shower. A few hairs every day is part of the cycle. A clear and lasting increase, or visibly less volume, is what deserves attention.
- **A trigger today shows up later.** A high fever, an illness, childbirth, a very strict diet or a period of heavy stress can push many hairs into the resting phase together. The extra shedding often appears two to three months after the trigger. So the customer may not link the shedding to the event.
- **Hair grows slowly.** Because the cycle is slow, any product needs months, not days, before a change can be judged. This is the single most important expectation to set.

## 2. Shedding, thinning or breakage?

The customer usually says "my hair is falling". Your first job is to find out what is really happening.

| | Shedding | Thinning | Breakage |
|---|---|---|---|
| What the customer sees | Whole hairs fall, often with a small white bulb at the root end | Wider parting, less volume, scalp more visible, thinner ponytail | Short broken pieces, uneven length, frizz, split ends |
| Where | Whole scalp or particular areas | Often top or parting line | Mostly mid-length and ends |
| Common contributors (general) | Stress, illness, childbirth, diet changes, some medicines, hormonal and thyroid conditions, low iron | Gradual change over a long time, family tendency, hormones | Heat styling, bleaching, colouring, tight hairstyles, rough brushing, dryness |
| Product direction | Hair-loss line, with a doctor check if persistent | Hair-loss line plus long adherence, doctor check if progressing | Mask, conditioner, leave-in products, gentle habits |

Ask the customer to hold a hair that fell: is the whole hair long, or is it a short broken piece? This simple question separates shedding from breakage in seconds.

## 3. Dandruff, dry scalp and oily scalp

These three are often confused, and the wrong product can make the customer unhappy.

| | Dandruff | Dry scalp | Oily scalp |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flakes | Often larger, may look yellowish or greasy | Small, white, powdery | Flakes may be present with greasy hair |
| Scalp feel | Itchy, sometimes slightly red | Tight, itchy, may feel dry all over the body | Greasy within a day or two of washing |
| Hair | Flakes on shoulders | Often dry elsewhere too | Flat, heavy, oily roots |
| Direction | Anti-dandruff shampoo or treatment used regularly | Gentle moisturising care, not too frequent hot washes | Regulating shampoo, regular washing |

A flaking scalp that is very red, thick, silvery, painful, oozing or spreading beyond the hairline should not be handled as simple dandruff. See the red flags below.

## 4. The five-minute consultation

A good consultation is short and structured. Ask, listen, then recommend.

1. **What is the main problem?** Shedding, thin hair, dandruff, itching, oily roots, dry ends, nails or lashes.
2. **Since when, and how fast?** Weeks, months or years. Sudden change needs more caution.
3. **Is it all over or in patches?**
4. **What changed recently?** Illness, fever, childbirth, stress, weight loss or diet, new medicine, new product, colouring or straightening.
5. **Who is it for?** An adult man, a woman, a pregnant or breastfeeding woman, a child. Check the product notes and the label for each.
6. **What is the current routine?** How often they wash, which products, heat tools, covered hair (hijab) and how it is fastened.
7. **Any scalp symptoms?** Itching, burning, redness, pain, wounds.
8. **What have they already tried and for how long?** Many customers stopped too early.
9. **Any allergies or known skin conditions?**

Useful phrases:

> Pharmacist: "Is the hair that falls long and whole, or short and broken?"

> Pharmacist: "Did anything change two or three months ago, like an illness, childbirth or a strict diet?"

> Pharmacist: "Are you losing hair all over, or in a particular spot?"

## 5. Red flags: refer, do not just sell

Refer the customer to a doctor or dermatologist when you notice any of these:

- Sudden or rapid hair loss, or clumps coming out
- Round or patchy bald areas, especially with smooth skin
- A painful, very red, scaly, thick or oozing scalp, or scalp wounds, pus or crusts
- Thick silvery plaques, or similar patches on elbows, knees or nails (possible psoriasis-type picture)
- Hair loss with tiredness, weight change, feeling cold or hot, menstrual irregularity, or other general symptoms
- Hair loss that started after a new medicine (do not advise stopping the medicine; send to the prescriber)
- A child with patchy loss or a scaly scalp
- No improvement after a reasonable, correct use of an appropriate product

Pharmacists counsel, they do not diagnose. Saying "I think you need a doctor to check the cause, and we can support your hair care meanwhile" protects the customer and builds trust.

## 6. Expectations and timelines

- Shedding usually needs patience: judge a hair-loss routine after consistent use over months, not after the first bottle.
- Dandruff and itching may calm sooner than shedding, but they can return if the customer stops completely.
- Damaged ends do not "repair" in the sense of growing back healthy. Products improve softness and appearance, and haircuts and gentle habits do the rest.
- Never promise "stops completely" or "regrows hair". Say what the product is for, how to use it, and how long to give it.
- Encourage a simple reminder, for example using the product on the same days each week, and a photo of the parting once a month to see progress honestly.

## 7. Common mistakes

- Selling a hair-loss product to a customer whose real problem is breakage
- Selling an anti-dandruff shampoo for a dry scalp without asking
- Ignoring red flags because the customer only wants "a shampoo"
- Promising results in two weeks
- Forgetting to ask who the product is for and whether they are pregnant or breastfeeding
- Skipping a patch or sensitivity caution: for any new product, advise using as directed on the label and stopping and asking for advice if burning, redness or rash appear

## Scenario

A woman in her thirties comes to a busy Lotus branch and asks for "the strongest hair-loss ampoules". She says her hair has been falling heavily for about six weeks.

**Model approach:**

1. Pharmacist asks: what changed in the last three months? She mentions a high fever about three months ago.
2. Pharmacist asks whether the hair is falling all over or in patches, and whether the scalp itches or hurts. It is all over, with no pain.
3. The pharmacist explains that shedding after a fever is common and often settles, but that it follows a slow cycle.
4. The pharmacist recommends an appropriate hair-loss line, explains it needs months of regular use, and suggests a doctor check if the shedding continues, increases or if she has other symptoms.
5. The pharmacist gives a simple plan (what to use, when, and a date to come back) instead of a one-time sale.

## Key takeaways

- Hair follows a slow cycle, so results are judged after months, not days
- Some daily shedding is normal; sudden, patchy or persistent loss needs a doctor
- Separate shedding, thinning and breakage before recommending anything
- Dandruff, dry scalp and oily scalp need different products
- A structured nine-question consultation takes about five minutes
- Red flags mean referral first; the pharmacist counsels and does not diagnose
- Honest timelines build long-term trust and better adherence
