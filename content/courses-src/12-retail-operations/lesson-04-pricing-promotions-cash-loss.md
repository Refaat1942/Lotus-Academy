## Learning objectives

After completing this lesson, you will be able to:

1. Keep prices accurate between the shelf, the system and the receipt
2. Apply promotions correctly and communicate their conditions honestly
3. Follow cash discipline at the till, including opening, handover and closing counts
4. Identify the common causes of shrinkage and apply simple prevention steps
5. Handle returns in general terms according to the Lotus policy and the rules on medicines

---

## 1. Why this lesson matters

In a busy Egyptian branch, hundreds of small transactions happen every day: a pack of tablets, a moisturiser, a thermometer, a delivery order by phone. Each transaction is a chance to build trust or to lose it. A wrong price, an unclear promotion, a cash difference or a missing product all damage the customer's confidence and the branch's results. Most of these problems are not dramatic. They come from small habits, repeated every day. This lesson turns those habits into a clear routine.

## 2. Price accuracy

A customer should pay the same price that they saw on the shelf, that the system shows and that appears on the receipt.

**The three-point price check**

| Point | What to check | Who and when |
|---|---|---|
| Shelf tag | Correct product, size and price | Team, after delivery, after any price change, and during shelf care |
| System | Price loaded correctly, promotion dates correct | Manager or authorised person, whenever a price file changes |
| Receipt | What the customer paid matches the tag | Cashier, at every sale |

Rules to remember:

- **Prices of registered medicines follow the official rules.** Never charge above the officially approved price, and follow the current Egyptian Drug Authority / Ministry of Health rules and the Lotus SOP for any pricing question. If you are unsure, ask the manager and do not guess.
- Non-medicine products (for example, dermocosmetics and accessories) follow Lotus pricing policy; only authorised staff change prices.
- A price change is made in the system first or together with the tag, never only on the shelf.
- When the tag and the till differ, fix it at once and follow the Lotus price-difference policy toward the customer.
- Never "round up" or add informal charges.

## 3. Promotions: rules and communication

A promotion is a promise to the customer. Keep it simple and honest.

**Before a promotion starts**
1. The promotion is approved by management in writing, with the product, dates and conditions.
2. The system is loaded and tested with a trial sale.
3. The shelf tag shows the promotional price, the dates and the condition (for example "buy two, get a discount").
4. The whole team is briefed on what the promotion is and what it is not.
5. Stock is checked: do not advertise something you cannot supply.

**During a promotion**
- Apply it to every eligible customer in the same way.
- Do not combine promotions unless the policy permits it.
- Explain the condition clearly before payment, so the customer is not surprised.
- Promotions on medicines must follow the rules of the Egyptian Drug Authority / Ministry of Health and the Lotus SOP. Do not encourage a customer to buy more medicine than needed because of an offer.
- Do not use promotion to push near-expiry products without telling the manager. Near-expiry decisions follow the SOP.

**After a promotion**
- Remove tags and reset prices on the end date.
- Review results with the manager: units sold, stock left.

**Script**

> Customer: "الخصم ده على كل الأحجام؟"
> Pharmacist: "الخصم على الحجم الكبير فقط، وده مكتوب على الرف. تحب أوضح لحضرتك الفرق بين الحجمين؟"

## 4. Receipts and records

- Give a receipt for every sale, printed or electronic according to Lotus policy.
- Check the receipt lines with the customer when the basket is large or when the customer asks.
- Prescription sales are recorded according to the current rules and the SOP.
- Never ring a sale under a wrong product to "make it easier".
- Keep voids, cancellations and discounts to a minimum, and record the reason each time. Voids and discounts need approval by the manager when the SOP says so.

## 5. Cash discipline

Cash is easy to mishandle when the branch is crowded. A simple discipline protects both the pharmacy and the cashier.

**Opening**
1. Count the opening float with a second person or the manager.
2. Record the amount in the cash sheet.
3. Check the card terminal, the printer and the connection.

**During the shift**
- One cashier, one drawer, one login. Never share a login.
- Close the drawer after each sale.
- Count change aloud and place the customer's banknote on the counter until the change is handed over.
- Check banknotes for obvious problems according to the Lotus guidance.
- Move excess cash to the safe at the times set by the SOP.
- Keep the cash drawer and safe out of customer reach, and never leave them open and unattended.

**Handover and closing**
1. Count the drawer in front of the manager or the incoming cashier.
2. Compare the count with the system's expected cash, card and other payment totals.
3. Record any difference, however small, and investigate causes: wrong change, wrong product, unrecorded refund, a mistake in the float.
4. Never "cover" a shortage from your own pocket and never hide an overage; both hide the real cause.
5. Deposit cash and reports as the SOP says.

| Cash difference | What it often means | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Small shortage | Wrong change or unrecorded item | Recount, check receipts, record |
| Repeated shortages with the same cashier | Training need or process issue | Manager reviews, retrains |
| Overage | Under-charged customer or unrecorded refund | Record; do not keep silently |
| Large difference | Possible serious error | Stop, inform the manager, follow the SOP |

## 6. Shrinkage: causes and prevention

**Shrinkage** is the loss of stock or value that cannot be explained by sales. It includes theft, errors, damage and expiry.

| Cause | Examples | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| Customer theft | Small, high-value items, cosmetics, baby products | Clear sight lines, staffed zones, small valuable items near the counter, greeting every customer |
| Staff or supplier error | Wrong receiving, wrong scanning, delivery shortages | Receiving checks, two-person counts, regular cycle counts |
| Administrative errors | Wrong unit, duplicate entry, wrong price | Training, system checks, correction of master data |
| Damage | Dropped boxes, leaks, heat damage | Safe handling, correct storage, step ladders |
| Expiry | Overstock, poor FEFO | Ordering discipline, expiry checks, early action |
| Internal theft | Cash or stock taken by staff | Clear controls, access limits, audits, reporting channel; no accusations without evidence |

Prevention habits for everyone: greet each customer who enters, keep the aisles clear, keep high-value items in view, do not leave the counter unattended, lock the stockroom, and report anything unusual to the manager. Treat customers with respect: suspicion without evidence is never a reason to accuse a person. The manager decides how to handle a suspected incident, according to the SOP and the law.

## 7. Returns in general terms

Returns are sensitive in a pharmacy because a medicine that has left the pharmacy cannot be guaranteed to have been stored correctly.

- **Medicines** are generally not taken back for resale once they have left the pharmacy. Follow the current rules of the Egyptian Drug Authority / Ministry of Health and the Lotus SOP. If a customer reports a wrong item given by the pharmacy, a damaged pack or a quality complaint, treat it as an incident: take the pack, note the batch and expiry, tell the pharmacist and the manager and follow the SOP, including reporting channels.
- **Non-medicine products** (for example, a sealed accessory, a device or a cosmetic) follow the Lotus return policy: condition, packaging, receipt and time limit as the policy states.
- Always be courteous. Listen first, explain the policy, and offer the available options.
- Record every return and exchange with the reason, and have the manager approve it if the SOP requires.
- Never put a returned medicine back on the shelf.

**Script**

> Customer: "اشتريت الكريم ده امبارح ومش مناسب، ممكن أرجّعه؟"
> Pharmacist: "أكيد أراجع معاك. هشوف حالة العبوة والإيصال وسياسة الاسترجاع، ولو فيه تهيج للبشرة فهوجّه حضرتك لطبيب جلدية."

## Scenario

At the end of a busy Friday evening shift in a branch in Nasr City, the cashier's drawer is short by an amount of EGP. The cashier is tempted to put in the missing amount from a personal wallet so that the report shows zero difference. The manager is at the counter.

**Model approach**
1. The cashier does not add personal money and does not hide the difference.
2. The cashier and the manager recount the drawer together.
3. They review receipts, voids and refunds in the system for the shift.
4. They look for common causes, such as wrong change on a busy period, an item sold under a different code or a refund not recorded.
5. They record the difference and the findings in the cash report.
6. If a pattern repeats, the manager provides coaching and checks procedures, such as keeping the customer's banknote visible until change is given.

## Key takeaways

- The customer should pay the same price on the shelf, in the system and on the receipt.
- Medicine prices follow the official rules; never charge outside them, and ask the manager if unsure.
- A promotion is a promise: approved, loaded, tagged, briefed and ended on time.
- Cash discipline means counts with a witness, one login per person, safe use of the drawer and honest records of every difference.
- Shrinkage comes from theft, errors, damage and expiry; prevention is mostly daily habit.
- Returned medicines are handled as incidents under the rules and the SOP, and are never put back on the shelf.
