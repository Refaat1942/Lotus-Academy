## Learning objectives

After completing this lesson, you will be able to:

1. Describe the main zones of a Lotus pharmacy and the purpose of each zone
2. Place products on shelves using eye-level, impulse and category-adjacency principles
3. Arrange OTC and dermocosmetics displays that help customers choose safely
4. Keep shelves clean, faced and correctly priced throughout the day
5. Plan and maintain seasonal displays without breaking storage or compliance rules

---

## 1. Why merchandising matters in a pharmacy

A pharmacy is a healthcare place first and a shop second. Merchandising in a pharmacy therefore has two jobs at the same time: it must help customers find what they need quickly and safely, and it must present the range in a way that supports the business. A customer who walks in with a child who has a fever should not have to search for the right shelf. A customer who stays a little longer because the dermocosmetics section is clear and inviting may find the product the pharmacist recommends.

Good merchandising never replaces counselling. The shelf shows the range; the pharmacist advises. Products that need professional advice stay behind the counter or in a pharmacist-controlled area, according to the current rules of the Egyptian Drug Authority / Ministry of Health and the Lotus SOP.

## 2. The zones of the pharmacy

Most Lotus branches, whether small street-front branches or larger ones, can be understood as a set of zones.

| Zone | Purpose | Typical content | Key rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entrance and window | First impression, seasonal message | Seasonal display, health-awareness message | Clean glass, no expired or faded material |
| Counter and dispensing area | Prescriptions, counselling, payment | Prescription medicines, restricted medicines, pharmacist workstation | Controlled access; customers do not reach behind the counter |
| Self-selection OTC area | Products customers may pick themselves | Vitamins, pain relief, cold and flu, digestive care | Clear category signs, price tags on every item |
| Dermocosmetics area | Skin, hair and sun care | Cleansers, moisturisers, sunscreen, hair care | Testers clean, pharmacist available to advise |
| Baby and mother care | Practical family needs | Nappies, baby care, feeding | Reachable at a height that is safe for parents |
| Health devices | Measurement and support | Thermometers, blood pressure monitors, glucose meters | Boxes sealed, demonstration unit clean |
| Storage and cold chain (back area) | Stock and refrigerated products | Reserve stock, fridge | Not visible or accessible to customers |

Walk through your own branch and name each zone. If a zone has no clear owner on the shift, it will drift into disorder.

## 3. Eye level, reach level and impulse areas

Shelves have different value depending on height.

- **Eye level**: the most visible position. Use it for products the pharmacist wants to feature, best-selling products and new arrivals, never for products that are close to expiry that you are trying to hide.
- **Reach level** (around the chest to the waist): easy to pick up. Good for fast-moving, everyday products.
- **Low shelves**: less visible. Use for bulky, heavy or low-turnover items, and for large packs.
- **High shelves**: only for light items or reserve packs. Staff should use a safe step, never climb on shelving.

**Impulse areas** are the spots where customers wait or pass: near the payment counter and along the main walking path. Suitable items are small, low-risk, low-price products such as lip balm, hand sanitiser, tissues, plasters or confectionery-free healthy items. Medicines that need counselling should not be sold as impulse items, and pushing a customer toward unnecessary products is not Lotus practice.

## 4. Category adjacency

Customers think in problems, not in product classes. Place related categories close together so that the shelf tells a story.

| Customer need | Place close together | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Cold and flu | Throat lozenges, saline nasal sprays, thermometers, vitamin C | One walk, one complete basket |
| Baby care | Nappies, wipes, nappy cream, baby shampoo | Parents can find everything together |
| Sun protection | Sunscreen, after-sun care, lip balm | Season-based need |
| Digestive care | Oral rehydration, antacids, probiotics | Natural grouping while keeping the pharmacist nearby |
| Diabetes support | Glucose meters, strips, lancets, sugar-free products | Easy to find without asking |

Never place unrelated products side by side in a way that suggests a medical link that does not exist.

## 5. OTC displays

For OTC products in self-selection:

1. Group by condition or use (for example pain, digestion, allergy), not by manufacturer alone.
2. Keep adult and children products visibly separate.
3. Face the labels forward so that the name, strength and expiry side can be seen when the box is picked up.
4. Keep a clear sign inviting the customer to ask the pharmacist.
5. Never display products in a way that encourages the customer to take several packs without advice (for example, large "take more" signs on medicines).

## 6. Dermocosmetics displays

Dermocosmetics customers often want advice about skin type and concerns. A good display is arranged by concern or routine (cleansing, moisturising, sun protection, hair) and by brand blocks only when the brand has its own fixture.

- Keep testers clean and wiped; replace damaged testers.
- Do not make skin-condition claims on shelf signs beyond what the product leaflet and the supplier materials state. Do not promise results.
- If a customer describes a persistent rash, severe acne, a changing mole or any skin problem that worries them, the pharmacist should advise and refer to a dermatologist.
- Keep sunscreen away from direct sun and heat, such as the window.

## 7. Clean and orderly shelves

A tidy shelf communicates safety. Use this simple routine:

**Shelf-care checklist (several times per shift)**
- Dust and wipe shelves; remove empty boxes.
- Face all products with labels forward, front of shelf.
- Place newer stock behind older stock, so older stock is sold first.
- Remove any damaged, opened or expired item immediately and record it according to the SOP.
- Check that each product has the correct price tag directly under it.
- Fill gaps from the reserve, but never leave a gap that hides a stock-out; note it for ordering.

## 8. Price tags

Every self-selection product needs a visible, correct, legible price in EGP. Rules:

- The tag sits directly below or beside the product it describes.
- The price on the tag matches the price at the till. A mismatch must be corrected at once, and the customer must be charged the correct displayed price according to Lotus policy.
- Promotional tags show the promotion dates and are removed on the end date.
- Handwritten tags are used only if the SOP allows them and must be clear.

## 9. Seasonal displays

Seasonal needs in Egypt are predictable: sun and heat care in summer, cold and allergy products in the seasonal change periods, school-return items, Ramadan-related digestive and hydration advice. Plan the display two to three weeks ahead.

- Place seasonal items in the entrance zone or on an end-cap, with adjacent products.
- Check storage needs: sunscreen and some products must not sit in hot window areas.
- Take the display down on the planned date and return products to their normal location.
- Use only supplier-approved or Lotus-approved messages.

**Worked example**: A branch plans a summer sun-care display. The team puts sunscreen, after-sun, lip balm and sun hats on an end-cap near the entrance. The window is hot in the afternoon, so only empty demonstration boxes are placed there; the real stock stays inside the cool shop area. A sign invites customers to ask the pharmacist about choosing a sunscreen. Prices are checked against the till before opening.

## Scenario

A busy branch in a residential area of Cairo receives a new delivery of dermocosmetics late in the afternoon. The branch has a small dermocosmetics shelf and the shift is full of customers. A technician places the new boxes on the eye-level shelf quickly, in front of the old stock, and forgets the price tags. Later, a customer picks a moisturiser, finds no price and asks the cashier; the till shows a different price from the old tag nearby.

**Model approach**
1. Fix the immediate problem: tell the customer the correct till price politely and, according to Lotus policy, honour the price displayed on the shelf if the tag was wrong.
2. Correct the shelf: place new tags directly under each product and remove the old tags.
3. Rotate: put the older units in front and the new units behind.
4. Prevent: add a "price tag check" step after each delivery to the shift checklist.

## Key takeaways

- A pharmacy shelf must serve safety first and sales second; the pharmacist counsels, the shelf only shows the range.
- Know the zones of your branch and who is responsible for each.
- Use eye level for featured products and reach level for fast movers; keep low-risk small items for impulse areas.
- Group products by customer need so that the shelf tells a story.
- Keep every product faced, clean and priced; remove damaged or expired items at once.
- Seasonal displays are planned, approved, checked for storage conditions and taken down on time.
