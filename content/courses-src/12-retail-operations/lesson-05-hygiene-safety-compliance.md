## Learning objectives

After completing this lesson, you will be able to:

1. Maintain cleanliness and safe handling standards in every area of the pharmacy
2. Keep accurate and honest records that can be shown at an inspection
3. Handle prescriptions and restricted medicines in line with current rules and the Lotus SOP
4. Carry out the daily opening and closing routines in order
5. Prepare the branch to be inspection-ready every day, not only before a visit

---

## 1. Compliance is a habit, not an event

Many pharmacies think about inspections only when a visit is expected. A professional pharmacy works the other way around: it is ready every day because the daily routines are done properly. If the branch is clean, the records are complete, the products are stored correctly, the prescriptions are handled by the rules and the staff know their roles, an inspection becomes an ordinary day.

This lesson brings together the habits from the whole course. It also covers what a pharmacy must never improvise: the rules on prescriptions and restricted medicines.

## 2. Cleanliness and hygiene

A pharmacy handles products that people put in or on their bodies. Cleanliness is therefore part of patient safety.

| Area | Daily standard | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Floor and entrance | Swept and mopped, no spills, no clutter | Wet floors are marked; spills are cleaned immediately |
| Shelves and displays | Dusted, faced, no empty boxes | Part of the shelf-care routine |
| Counter and dispensing area | Wiped, tidy, only items needed for current tasks | No food or drink on the dispensing counter |
| Counting trays and tools | Cleaned between uses | Avoid cross-contamination between products |
| Fridge and storage | Clean, ordered, temperature recorded | See Lesson 3.3 |
| Testers and demonstration devices | Clean and in good condition | Replace damaged testers |
| Staff areas | Separate from product areas | Food and drinks stay in the staff area |
| Waste | Bins emptied, expired products handled by the SOP | Do not mix expired medicines with general waste without following the SOP |

**Personal hygiene**: wash hands before handling products and after the toilet, keep the uniform clean, tie back long hair if the SOP requires, cover cuts, and do not work in a way that risks passing an infection to customers. A staff member who is unwell should inform the manager so that safe arrangements can be made.

## 3. Safe handling

- **Lifting**: bend the knees, keep boxes close to the body, ask for help for heavy cartons, and do not carry more than is safe.
- **Step ladders**: use a proper stable step; never climb shelving or stand on boxes.
- **Aisles and cartons**: keep walkways clear; do not leave cartons in the aisle.
- **Spills**: clean immediately; a leaking pack is handled with gloves and recorded as damage.
- **Sharps and used items**: follow the SOP for any needles, lancets or devices handled in the pharmacy.
- **Electrical safety**: no overloaded sockets; report broken cables or devices.
- **Fire safety**: know where the extinguisher and exits are, and keep them unobstructed.
- **First aid**: know where the first-aid kit is and who is trained according to the branch arrangements.

## 4. Record keeping

Records are the pharmacy's proof of what it did. Good records are accurate, complete, legible, dated and signed by role or code, and kept for the period the rules and the Lotus SOP require.

| Record | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Delivery notes and invoices | Proof of what was received and from whom |
| Temperature logs | Proof of correct storage |
| Expiry check records | Proof of monitoring |
| Quarantine and disposal records | Proof that expired or damaged products were removed |
| Cash sheets and end-of-day reports | Proof of financial control |
| Prescription and dispensing records | Proof of legal dispensing, kept as the current rules require |
| Complaints and incident records | Proof of learning and follow-up |
| Cleaning and opening/closing checklists | Proof of routine |

Rules for records:

1. Record at the time, not later from memory.
2. Never alter a record to hide a mistake. If you must correct, cross out with one line, write the correction, and sign and date it.
3. Never pre-fill, back-date or copy records.
4. Keep records where they can be found quickly, and protect customers' personal information.

## 5. Prescriptions and restricted medicines

This is the area where improvising is most dangerous. **Rules change over time**, so every pharmacist and technician must check the current requirements of the Egyptian Drug Authority / Ministry of Health and the Lotus SOP, and must not rely on memory, habit or what another pharmacy does.

General principles that are safe to apply:

- Medicines that require a prescription are dispensed only against a valid prescription, by the pharmacist, following the current rules.
- Some medicines are subject to **additional restrictions** (for example, tighter control, special record keeping or special storage). Which products fall in this group, and exactly what must be done, is defined by the current official rules and the Lotus SOP. Check them. Do not guess.
- Keep restricted medicines in the secured area defined by the SOP, with access limited to authorised staff.
- Record dispensing in the way the rules and the SOP require, completely and legibly.
- Do not sell a medicine that needs a prescription without one, even to a regular customer, even when asked politely, and even when the branch is busy. Explain calmly and offer to help the customer reach a doctor.
- If a prescription looks altered, unclear, incomplete or suspicious, the pharmacist does not dispense until it is clarified with the prescriber or according to the SOP.
- A technician does not take decisions on prescriptions or restricted medicines that belong to the pharmacist.
- Never give a stock of restricted medicines to another person or branch except through the approved process.
- Report discrepancies in restricted-medicine records to the manager immediately.

## 6. Daily opening routine

1. Arrive on time and check the premises: door, lights, signs of entry problems.
2. Switch on the lights, air conditioning, computers and card terminal.
3. Read and record the fridge temperature and the room temperature if required. Act on anything out of range.
4. Check the cash float with a witness according to the SOP.
5. Read the handover notes from the previous shift: orders, incidents, shortages, promotions to start or end.
6. Check that shelf tags and promotions for the day are correct.
7. Walk the floor: clean, face the shelves, check the entrance and window.
8. Make sure the pharmacist is in place and the dispensing area is ready.
9. Brief the team: priorities, deliveries expected, who covers which zone.
10. Open the doors at the official time.

## 7. Daily closing routine

1. Stop new sales at the closing time; finish serving customers who are inside.
2. Complete pending orders and delivery lists; update the stock and the system.
3. Count the cash with a witness; compare with the system; record any difference.
4. Secure the cash according to the SOP.
5. Read and record the fridge temperature; check the fridge door and plug.
6. Secure restricted medicines and the dispensing area.
7. Clean the counter, floor and shelves; empty the bins.
8. Write the handover notes: shortages, incidents, orders, tomorrow's deliveries.
9. Switch off equipment as the SOP says, lock the stockroom, set the alarm if present, and lock the doors.

## 8. Inspection readiness

An inspector or auditor checks whether the pharmacy works according to the rules. If daily routines are followed, the branch is ready. A short weekly self-check helps.

**Self-inspection checklist**
- License and required documents available and valid, kept where the SOP says.
- Premises clean and in good repair.
- Staff in uniform and aware of their roles; the pharmacist present as required.
- Temperature logs complete and signed.
- No expired products on shelves; quarantine area organised and recorded.
- Products stored correctly; no food in the fridge.
- Prices visible and accurate.
- Prescription and restricted-medicine records complete and secured.
- Complaint and incident records available.
- Cleaning, opening and closing checklists completed.

During a visit: stay calm and respectful, be honest, let the pharmacist or manager lead the conversation, show the requested documents, answer only what you know, note any recommendations and follow up. Never hide, alter or backdate a record during an inspection.

## Scenario

On a Saturday morning in a branch in Heliopolis, the manager receives a message that an inspection visit may happen this week. The team is nervous. Some staff suggest tidying everything and filling the missing temperature log entries for the last few days.

**Model approach**
1. The manager reminds the team that records are never filled in or back-dated. Missing readings stay missing and are reported honestly, with a corrective note.
2. The team uses the self-inspection checklist and fixes real problems: cleaning, shelf expiry checks, price tags, quarantine area.
3. The manager checks that documents are in their place and that restricted-medicine records are complete and the storage secure.
4. The team confirms that everyone knows the current rules and the SOP, and that the pharmacist knows how to answer questions.
5. After the exercise, the manager adds a weekly self-check so the branch is ready every day.

## Key takeaways

- Compliance is built by daily habits, so the branch is always inspection-ready.
- Cleanliness, safe handling and personal hygiene are part of patient safety.
- Records are accurate, timely and never altered, pre-filled or back-dated.
- Prescriptions and restricted medicines follow the current rules of the Egyptian Drug Authority / Ministry of Health and the Lotus SOP; check them and never rely on memory.
- Opening and closing routines protect stock, cash and security.
- A weekly self-inspection finds problems early and fixes them honestly.
