## Learning objectives

After completing this lesson, you will be able to:

1. Apply FEFO (first-expiry, first-out) when storing and displaying stock
2. Carry out a complete receiving check on a delivery and record discrepancies
3. Monitor expiry dates and handle near-expiry stock according to the Lotus SOP
4. Manage shortages and substitutions safely, with the pharmacist's approval
5. Place orders using sales history and minimum levels, and run stock counts and investigate differences

---

## 1. Why stock management is a patient-safety topic

In a pharmacy, stock is not only money on a shelf. A medicine that is out of date, damaged, stored wrongly or mixed up with another product can harm a patient. A medicine that is missing when the patient needs it can interrupt treatment. Stock management therefore protects three things: patients, the pharmacy's reputation and the pharmacy's cash.

The core principles are simple: know what you have, know when it expires, receive carefully, store correctly, sell older stock first, order sensibly and count regularly.

## 2. FEFO: first-expiry, first-out

Many shops use FIFO (first in, first out). In a pharmacy the better rule is **FEFO**: the product that **expires first** is sold first, even if it arrived later.

| Situation | FIFO result | FEFO result |
|---|---|---|
| Batch A arrived in January, expires in 14 months. Batch B arrived in March, expires in 8 months | Batch A sold first, Batch B may expire | Batch B sold first, both batches sell |

(The numbers above are only an illustration of the principle.)

How to apply FEFO:

1. At receiving, read the expiry date on every batch and write or mark it if the system requires.
2. On the shelf and in reserve storage, place the product with the earliest expiry at the front or top, and later expiry behind.
3. When refilling, move the old units forward first, then add the new units behind.
4. Never mix different batches in one open box.
5. When dispensing, pick from the front, and check the expiry date of the pack you hand over.

## 3. Receiving goods: the delivery check

Deliveries from distributors usually arrive during busy hours. Do not sign and walk away. A short, disciplined check protects the branch.

**Receiving checklist**
1. Check that the delivery is for your branch and the supplier is an approved Lotus supplier.
2. Check the number of cartons against the delivery note or invoice.
3. Check cartons for damage, wetness or tampering.
4. For refrigerated items, check that they arrived in the proper cold packaging, and check them first. Move them to the fridge immediately.
5. Open and compare each line: product name, strength, form, pack size, quantity, batch number and expiry date.
6. Check the remaining shelf life of every item against the Lotus SOP minimum.
7. Check prices against the system and report differences to the manager.
8. Record shortages, excess, damaged packs and wrong items on the delivery note before signing, and tell the supplier representative.
9. Enter the stock into the system with batch and expiry information.
10. Store the goods the same day, in the correct condition, following FEFO.

| Discrepancy | Immediate action |
|---|---|
| Quantity less than the invoice | Note it on the delivery note, request the missing items or a credit note |
| Wrong item delivered | Do not put it on the shelf; separate it and arrange return |
| Damaged or leaking pack | Do not accept it; record it |
| Very short expiry | Do not accept unless the SOP allows it and the manager approves |
| Cold item arrived warm | Do not accept it; follow the SOP and inform the supplier |

## 4. Expiry date monitoring

Expiry monitoring is a routine, not a one-time task.

- Define a regular schedule, for example a section each week so that the whole pharmacy is covered every cycle set by the SOP.
- Use the system's near-expiry report if available, and physically check the shelves as well.
- Mark near-expiry products as the SOP requires, using a clear and consistent method.
- The Lotus SOP defines the threshold for "near expiry"; follow it rather than your own judgement.

**Handling near-expiry stock**

| Option | When appropriate |
|---|---|
| Move to the front of the shelf | Product still within the safe sale window |
| Notify the manager early | To arrange return to the supplier or a promotion if the SOP allows |
| Return or exchange with the distributor | If the supplier policy allows |
| Quarantine and remove from sale | Once expired or when the SOP says so |

An **expired product** must be removed from the shelf at once, put in a clearly labelled quarantine place away from saleable stock, recorded, and disposed of according to the Lotus SOP and current official rules. Never sell it, never give it away and never "just keep it in case".

## 5. Shortages and substitutions

A shortage happens when a product is out of stock or unavailable from suppliers.

1. Record every request for an unavailable product. This is "lost demand" information and helps ordering.
2. Check other suppliers or other Lotus branches according to the SOP.
3. If the customer needs an alternative, the **pharmacist** decides whether a substitution is appropriate. A substitution must follow the prescription, the same active ingredient and strength rules, and the current regulations. Never change a prescribed medicine without the pharmacist and, where required, the prescriber.
4. Explain clearly to the customer, in simple words, what is different (brand, price, appearance).
5. Never push a more expensive alternative just for margin.

Example phrase:

> Customer: "Isn't it available today?"
> Pharmacist: "It is not available right now. I can order it for you so it arrives as soon as possible, or I can check a suitable alternative with you after I confirm it is appropriate."

## 6. Ordering basics

Ordering is about balance: not too little (stock-outs, lost customers) and not too much (expiry, locked cash).

- **Minimum level**: the quantity at which you reorder.
- **Maximum level**: the quantity you should not exceed.
- **Lead time**: how long the supplier takes to deliver.
- **Sales history**: how fast the product moves.
- **Seasonality**: for example, sun care in summer, cold products in winter.

A simple logic: order = expected sales during the lead time and review period + a small safety quantity − stock on hand.

**Worked example (illustrative)**: A product sells about 6 packs per week. The supplier delivers within 2 days, and you review orders weekly. Stock on hand is 4 packs. You expect about 6 packs sold during a week and a little more for delivery delay. With a small safety quantity of 3, order about 6 + 3 − 4 = 5 packs. Do not simply order a full carton because it is cheaper if the product would sit close to its expiry.

Be careful with: promotional bulk offers on slow movers, seasonal over-ordering, and ordering the same product from several suppliers on the same day.

## 7. Stock counts and discrepancies

A stock count compares what the system says with what is physically there.

- **Cycle counts**: small sections counted regularly, for example a shelf or category each week.
- **Full counts**: the whole branch, on the schedule set by Lotus management.

**Count procedure**
1. Prepare: finish receiving and returns, so the system is up to date.
2. Count by two people when possible, one counting and one recording.
3. Count each batch separately, with expiry date.
4. Compare with the system and list differences.
5. Recount any difference before adjusting.
6. Investigate causes: unrecorded sale, unrecorded receipt, wrong product scanned, theft, damage, expiry not recorded, wrong unit (box vs strip).
7. Adjust the system only with the manager's authorisation, and record the reason.

Frequent causes of discrepancies are simple: a technician scans the wrong strength, a strip is sold from a box without adjustment, or a delivery is entered twice. Training and discipline fix most of them.

## Scenario

On a busy Thursday evening in a branch in Giza, a distributor delivers twelve cartons. The delivery driver is in a hurry and asks the technician to sign. One carton looks crushed, and the delivery note lists a refrigerated product that is not visible in the cartons. The technician decides to follow the checklist.

**Model approach**
1. Politely tell the driver the check will take a few minutes.
2. Count cartons, and photograph or record the damaged carton in the way the SOP requires.
3. Ask where the refrigerated item is; do not sign until it is found or noted as missing on the delivery note.
4. Open the crushed carton, reject any damaged packs and record them.
5. Check batch and expiry of each line; find one line with a much shorter expiry than usual. Refer it to the pharmacy manager for a decision.
6. Sign with the notes written, enter stock with batch and expiry, store using FEFO.
7. Report the discrepancies to the supplier the same day.

## Key takeaways

- Stock management protects patients first, then the reputation and the cash of the pharmacy.
- FEFO means the product that expires first is sold first, not the product that arrived first.
- A receiving check covers quantity, condition, product details, batch, expiry and cold chain before signing.
- Expiry monitoring is a scheduled routine; expired items are removed, quarantined, recorded and disposed of per SOP.
- Substitutions are decided by the pharmacist according to the rules, never by margin.
- Ordering balances sales history, lead time and shelf life; stock counts reveal and correct discrepancies.
