## Learning objectives

After completing this lesson, you will be able to:

1. Distinguish a helpful add-on suggestion from pushing an unnecessary item
2. Apply a simple test to decide whether a complementary product genuinely helps the customer
3. Use cross-sell, upsell and bundle logic correctly and honestly
4. Discuss vitamins, dermocosmetics and home devices responsibly, without overpromising
5. Offer an add-on in one respectful sentence and accept a "no" gracefully

---

## 1. What an ethical add-on is

An add-on is a second product suggested because it **helps the customer get the result they came for, use the first product safely, or avoid a predictable problem**. A suggestion that only increases the receipt is not an add-on; it is pushing.

The **helpfulness test**. Suggest an extra product only if you can answer "yes" to all three:

1. Does it solve a real need or risk that this customer has, based on what they told me?
2. Can I explain the benefit in one honest sentence?
3. Would I be comfortable if the customer's family heard my reason?

If not, do not suggest it.

## 2. Cross-sell, upsell and bundles

| Technique | Meaning | Ethical example | Unethical example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cross-sell** | A different product that supports the first | Oral rehydration solution with a child's diarrhoea advice when a doctor has been consulted as needed; a moisturiser for skin that is dry from a treatment the doctor advised | Adding an unrelated vitamin to every receipt |
| **Upsell** | A better or larger version of the same product | A larger pack for a regular chronic medicine so the customer has fewer trips and a lower unit price | Insisting on the premium version when the basic one is enough |
| **Bundle** | Several items offered together at a combined price | A skin-care routine of cleanser, moisturiser and sunscreen for a customer who asked for a routine | A bundle that includes items the customer does not need just to reach an offer threshold |

*Bundle logic:* a bundle is fair when each item has a clear role, the combined price is genuinely lower or more convenient, and the customer can still buy items individually. Say what is in it and what each item does.

*Upsell rule:* present the upgrade as an option with a reason, never as the "proper" choice. "The larger pack costs less per tablet and saves you a monthly trip. The smaller pack is fine too."

## 3. Natural add-on situations

| Customer need | Possible helpful add-on | Condition / caution |
|---|---|---|
| Antibiotic prescribed by a doctor | Reminder about finishing the course and taking it as directed; ask whether a doctor advised anything else | Only add products the doctor or the leaflet supports; do not suggest extra medicines on your own |
| Sunscreen for summer | A lip product with sun protection, or a suitable after-sun moisturiser | Check skin type and age |
| Newborn or infant care | Items the parent is already asking about, such as safe-use thermometer, nasal saline drops | Refer to doctor for fever in very young infants |
| Chronic disease supplies | Test strips with a glucometer, a pill organiser, a blood pressure monitor for home measurement | Only when the customer needs them; explain correct use and when to see a doctor |
| Dry, sensitive skin | Gentle cleanser and moisturiser | Refer to a dermatologist for persistent rash, infection signs or severe skin disease |
| Cough with throat irritation | Simple supportive measures such as warm fluids advice and a humidifier discussion | Do not add extra cough products on top of each other |

Avoid: adding antibiotics, painkillers or sedatives as "extras"; adding duplicate products with the same active ingredient; adding anything to a prescription that the doctor did not advise unless there is a clear, safe, recognised reason.

## 4. Vitamins, dermocosmetics and devices

### Vitamins and supplements
- They are often useful for a specific need, such as a doctor-advised deficiency, pregnancy, or a particular diet. They are not a "must" for everyone.
- Never suggest that a supplement replaces a balanced diet or a doctor's treatment.
- Check the customer's other medicines, pregnancy and age. Avoid stacking several products with overlapping contents.
- Be honest about timeframes: supplements are not instant fixes.

### Dermocosmetics
- Match to skin type and concern after asking questions: oily, dry, sensitive, acne-prone, sun exposure.
- Explain a simple routine (for example cleanse, moisturise, protect from sun) and what to expect.
- Do not diagnose skin disease. Refer rashes that are painful, spreading, infected or persistent to a dermatologist.
- Brand names matter less than fit, tolerance and the customer's budget. Offer a good option at a realistic price and mention that a simple routine can be enough.
- Do not promise dramatic results, or "guaranteed" fast changes.

### Devices (thermometers, blood pressure monitors, glucometers, nebulisers)
- Recommend based on who will use it and how often.
- Explain how to use it correctly and how to read the results. A device used wrongly gives false confidence.
- Explain that a home reading does not replace a doctor's assessment.
- Be honest about running costs (batteries, strips, accessories).

## 5. How to offer an add-on

Use one respectful sentence that links to the customer's need, and accept the answer.

> Pharmacist: "Since you will be using this treatment for the next few weeks, a gentle moisturiser can help with the dryness it may cause. Would you like me to show you one?"
> Customer: "No, thank you, maybe later."
> Pharmacist: "Of course. Just remember it is available if the dryness bothers you."

Rules:

- **One** suggestion at a time, not a list.
- Offer at the right moment, **after** the main need is settled.
- Never repeat after a "no". Never make the customer feel guilty.
- Do not suggest add-ons when the customer is in distress, in a rush for an urgent need, or has said money is tight, unless the item is important for safety.
- For WhatsApp or phone orders, include a short single line, not a menu of promotions.

## 6. Basket size, price and fairness

Egyptian customers feel inflation in every receipt. A bigger basket is only good if it is good *for the customer*. Offer lower-priced alternatives when they exist. Mention what is essential and what is optional so the customer can choose within their budget. Respect the customer who wants only the main product.

## 7. Common mistakes

- Offering the same add-on to every customer.
- Suggesting an add-on before the main need is solved.
- Adding products that duplicate each other.
- Presenting an optional item as essential.
- Making a bundle that pushes the customer past what they need.
- Continuing to push after the customer says no.
- Overpromising about vitamins and skin products.

## Scenario

A mother asks for a sunscreen for herself and a product for her eight-year-old's dry skin patches. She mentions she has a limited budget this month.

**Model approach.** The pharmacist asks about skin type, sun exposure and the child's skin: is the dryness new, itchy, weeping or spreading? There are no worrying signs. The pharmacist recommends a suitable sunscreen and a gentle fragrance-free moisturiser for the child, explains each in one sentence, and says which one matters more this month. When the mother says the budget is tight, the pharmacist does not add a cleanser or a vitamin, offers a lower-priced alternative for one item, and advises that if the patches become red, painful or spread, a dermatologist should see the child. The basket is smaller than it could have been, and the mother returns every month.

## Key takeaways

- An add-on is ethical only if it helps the customer's own need or safety; use the three-question helpfulness test.
- Cross-sell supports the first product, upsell offers a better option with a reason, and a bundle must have clear roles and a fair price.
- Vitamins, dermocosmetics and devices need matching to the person, honest timeframes and no overpromising.
- Offer one add-on in one sentence, after the main need, and accept "no" without repeating.
- Never add antibiotics, painkillers or sedatives as extras, or duplicate active ingredients.
- A smaller, honest basket builds the customer who returns.

**Q9.** Which is correct about recommending a home blood pressure monitor?
