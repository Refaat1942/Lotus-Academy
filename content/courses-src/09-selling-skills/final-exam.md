# Final Exam — Pharmacy Selling Skills

## Final Exam (20 questions)

**Q1.** A branch manager announces a monthly target on a high-margin supplement. A customer asks for something simple for mild tiredness after a short illness. What is the best approach?
- a) Recommend the supplement since it supports the target
- b) Ask about the customer's situation, recommend only what genuinely fits, and be honest if a simple measure is enough ✓
- c) Recommend three supplements so the customer can choose
- d) Say the pharmacy has nothing suitable

**Q2.** A customer asks for a strong antibiotic "to be safe" for a sore throat that started this morning, without a prescription. What do you do?
- a) Sell a short course to avoid an argument
- b) Sell half a course
- c) Suggest a stronger medicine from the shelf
- d) Decline politely, explain that antibiotics are a doctor's decision, and offer suitable supportive advice and a referral trigger ✓

**Q3.** Which pair correctly describes pushing and consultative selling?
- a) Pushing starts from the product; consultative selling starts from the person ✓
- b) Pushing starts from the person; consultative selling starts from the product
- c) Both start from the target
- d) Pushing asks questions; consultative selling talks first

**Q4.** An elderly customer on several medicines asks for a pain remedy in a crowded branch. What should you do before recommending?
- a) Hand over the usual product quickly because of the queue
- b) Ask only about price
- c) Ask what the pain is, how long, what other medicines and conditions he has, and check for red flags ✓
- d) Ask him to return when the pharmacy is quiet

**Q5.** Which question is an open question?
- a) "Any fever?"
- b) "Are you pregnant?"
- c) "Is it for a child?"
- d) "What have you tried so far, and did it help?" ✓

**Q6.** A mother says her baby has fast breathing and a cough since last night. What is the right action?
- a) Sell a cough syrup and ask her to return in two days
- b) Calmly advise that the baby be seen by a doctor today, explain what to tell the doctor, and sell nothing unnecessary ✓
- c) Recommend a bundle for babies' colds
- d) Suggest a home remedy only

**Q7.** Why do you summarise and confirm before recommending?
- a) To lengthen the conversation
- b) To avoid giving instructions
- c) To check that you understood who the patient is and what the need is, and to catch mistakes ✓
- d) To prepare the invoice

**Q8.** A customer tells you he cannot swallow large tablets. Which statement is a proper FAB?
- a) "This tablet is small and coated, so it is easier to swallow, which should be more comfortable for you." ✓
- b) "This is the newest tablet we have."
- c) "This is the one on promotion."
- d) "Everyone takes this one."

**Q9.** A customer asks if the cheaper generic is really the same as the original. Which answer is honest and balanced?
- a) "Yes, they are identical in everything."
- b) "It has the same active ingredient and strength and is registered; differences are mainly brand, packaging, inactive ingredients and price, and I will tell you if any matters for you." ✓
- c) "No, only the original is reliable."
- d) "I cannot discuss this."

**Q10.** You are not sure about a claim you heard from a supplier representative. What should you do?
- a) Repeat it with confidence
- b) Add a percentage so it sounds scientific
- c) Avoid the product
- d) Check the approved leaflet and tell the customer "let me confirm this accurately" ✓

**Q11.** Which add-on passes the helpfulness test?
- a) A gentle moisturiser suggested to a customer who will use a skin treatment that may cause dryness, offered in one sentence ✓
- b) A vitamin added to every receipt
- c) A second painkiller with the same active ingredient
- d) An expensive device for a customer who did not ask about one

**Q12.** A customer says money is tight this month and wants to buy a treatment plus two optional products. What is best?
- a) Present the complete bundle once more
- b) Mention that the offer ends today
- c) Focus on the essential treatment, say which optional items can wait, and offer a lower-priced option if one exists ✓
- d) Add another product to reach a discount

**Q13.** A customer with a painful, spreading rash asks for a skin-care routine. What is best?
- a) Sell the full routine to calm the skin
- b) Recommend that a dermatologist examine the rash and avoid adding a routine until then ✓
- c) Recommend a strong cream without questions
- d) Change the product every week

**Q14.** What makes a bundle ethical?
- a) It uses up slow-moving stock
- b) It reaches an offer threshold
- c) It is cheaper only when all items are bought
- d) Each item has a clear role, the price is genuinely better or more convenient, and items can still be bought separately ✓

**Q15.** A customer says "This has become so expensive." What is the best start to your response?
- a) "Prices are fixed and I cannot help."
- b) "Other pharmacies are more expensive."
- c) Acknowledge honestly, clarify what is essential and what is optional, and offer real options ✓
- d) Offer an immediate random discount

**Q16.** A customer says "My neighbour told me to use another product." What do you do?
- a) Ask what it is for, check its suitability and safety, and support it if suitable ✓
- b) Tell the customer the neighbour is wrong
- c) Ignore the comment and repeat your recommendation
- d) Say your product is better

**Q17.** A customer says "I will think about it." Which response is appropriate?
- a) "The price will increase tomorrow."
- b) "If you wait, it will get worse."
- c) Lower the price at once
- d) "Of course. Is there anything I can clarify, such as price, use or suitability?" followed by a clear next step ✓

**Q18.** Which signal shows that a customer is ready to buy?
- a) The customer looks at the door
- b) The customer asks about daily use, pack size or monthly cost ✓
- c) The customer is silent
- d) The customer starts talking about a different problem

**Q19.** A loyalty programme gives points under certain rules. How should you present it?
- a) Explain accurately what the current programme offers under its rules, without promising more ✓
- b) Say that buying more guarantees extra rewards
- c) Skip the explanation to save time
- d) Collect the customer's details without explanation

**Q20.** A branch target requires selling products customers do not need and hiding cheaper options. Average basket is rising but complaints are rising too. What is the professional response?
- a) Continue as the figures look good
- b) Hide the complaints from the manager
- c) Raise the concern politely with the pharmacy manager, read KPIs together, and put duties to patients first ✓
- d) Apply the target only to new customers
