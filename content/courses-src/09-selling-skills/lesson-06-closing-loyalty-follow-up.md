## Learning objectives

After completing this lesson, you will be able to:

1. Close a sale respectfully by confirming the decision and summarising instructions
2. Explain loyalty programme concepts and how to present them honestly
3. Run appropriate follow-up by phone call or WhatsApp with consent
4. Read simple KPIs such as conversion and average basket value in a healthy way
5. Recognise unethical target behaviour and respond professionally

---

## 1. Closing is a service, not a trick

Closing means helping the customer move from "maybe" to a clear decision. If you have listened, recommended honestly and answered objections, closing is natural. Do not use tricks, pressure or fear. A good close does three things: **asks for the decision, confirms the details, and gives a next step**.

**Respectful ways to ask for the sale:**

- Direct and simple: "Shall I prepare this one for you?"
- Choice-based: "Would you like the single pack or the larger pack?"
- Summary-based: "So we have the treatment and the moisturiser. Is that everything for today?"
- Handover: "I will pack it and write the instructions on the box."

*Avoid:* "You have to buy this today", "Everyone takes this", or silence that leaves the customer unsure what happens next.

## 2. Signals that the customer is ready

- Asks about use, timing or how long it lasts.
- Asks about the pack size or the price per month.
- Holds the product, reads the box, or asks to see it.
- Agrees verbally ("OK", "fine", "that sounds good").

When you see these, stop explaining and close. Over-talking after the customer has decided can reopen doubts.

## 3. The closing checklist

1. Confirm the products and quantities.
2. Check the prescription (if any), the expiry date and the pack condition.
3. Summarise how to use each product in one or two lines. Write it on the box for older customers or complex schedules.
4. Give one safety reminder and one "see a doctor if" trigger.
5. State the total clearly and give a receipt, following Lotus procedures.
6. Offer follow-up or a return visit: "If you have any question, please call or message us."
7. Thank the customer and wish them well ("Get well soon" / "سلامتك").

## 4. Loyalty: the concept

Loyalty is not a card; it is the customer's feeling that this pharmacy knows them and treats them fairly. A loyalty programme supports that feeling with simple benefits. Typical concepts (the Lotus programme rules are set by the company and may change, so always explain what the current programme actually offers):

| Concept | What it means | Honest way to present it |
|---|---|---|
| **Points** | Customer earns points on purchases | "You collect points that you can use under the programme rules." State the real rules |
| **Tiers or special offers** | Extra benefits for regular customers | Explain eligibility clearly |
| **Refill reminders** | Reminder when a chronic medicine is due | Offer only with consent; useful for health |
| **Customer profile** | Basic details for service (such as chronic medicines, preferences) | Explain why you collect it, keep it confidential and follow the Lotus privacy procedures |
| **Family accounts** | One account for family purchases | Optional; explain the benefit |

Rules:

- Never promise a benefit that the programme does not provide.
- Never make the loyalty programme a reason to buy something the customer does not need ("buy more to get more points").
- Keep personal and health information confidential. Collect only what you need and only with the customer's agreement.
- Do not share a customer's information with anyone else.

## 5. Follow-up by phone or WhatsApp

Follow-up shows care and also catches problems early. Use it where it is useful and welcome.

**When it fits:**
- After a new treatment was started.
- After delivery of a phone or WhatsApp order.
- When a chronic medicine refill is due.
- After a customer was referred to a doctor ("I hope you were able to see the doctor.").
- When an item that was out of stock arrives.

**Rules:**
- Have the customer's **agreement** to be contacted, and respect their preferred time and method.
- Keep messages short, polite and about their need, not a list of promotions.
- Use a pharmacy account, never a personal one, and follow the Lotus procedures for customer data.
- Do not send health details in a way others might see (for example, a family group).
- If a customer reports a worrying symptom, advise them to see a doctor and tell the pharmacist in charge.
- Stop if the customer asks you to.

*Model WhatsApp message (English):*

> "Hello, this is the Lotus pharmacy. We hope you are feeling better after starting the treatment. If you have any question about how to use it, please reply to this message. Take care."

*رسالة نموذجية بالعربية:*

> "أهلًا بحضرتك، معك صيدلية لوتس. نتمنى أن تكون بخير بعد بدء العلاج. وإن كان لديك أي سؤال عن طريقة الاستخدام فيمكنك الرد على هذه الرسالة. سلامتك."

## 6. Simple KPIs, used in a healthy way

KPIs help a branch see how it is doing. They should guide improvement, not create pressure to push.

| KPI | What it measures | Healthy use | Unhealthy use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Conversion rate** | Share of customers who buy after a conversation | See whether discovery and recommendation help the customer decide | Pressuring each customer to buy something |
| **Average basket value** | Average spend per receipt | See whether the customer receives genuinely helpful add-ons | Chasing a higher number with unnecessary items |
| **Return rate (repeat customers)** | How many customers come back | A sign of trust and good service | Ignoring it in favour of one-off sales |
| **Customer feedback / complaints** | What customers say | Learn and improve | Hiding or blaming |
| **Refill and follow-up completion** | Whether planned contacts happened | Better care for chronic customers | Using contacts only to promote |

Read KPIs together. A higher average basket with more complaints is a warning, not a success. Rising repeat visits and positive feedback show that the approach is working.

### Unethical targets

If a target seems to require you to sell what customers do not need, to hide cheaper options, to sell prescription-only items without a prescription, or to pressure vulnerable customers, do not follow it. Raise it politely with the pharmacy manager, referring to the Lotus code of conduct. Professional and legal duties to patients come before any sales target.

## 7. Common mistakes

- Over-explaining after the customer has already decided.
- Forgetting to give usage instructions at the close.
- Collecting customer data without explaining why.
- Using WhatsApp follow-up as a promotion channel.
- Promising loyalty benefits that do not exist.
- Judging staff only by the average basket.
- Following a target at the expense of the customer.

## Scenario

A customer in his sixties has just started a new regular medicine for blood pressure after seeing his doctor. The pharmacist explained the instructions during the sale and, with his agreement, noted his preferred contact method and when his refill would be due.

**Model approach.** At the close the pharmacist summarises: the tablet and when to take it, one safety reminder, and when to contact his doctor. He is offered the larger monthly pack, with a clear reason, and chooses it. The pharmacist explains the loyalty programme as it currently stands, without exaggeration. Three days later, with his consent, the pharmacy sends a short WhatsApp message asking whether he has any questions. He replies that he has felt slightly dizzy. The pharmacist advises him to contact his doctor and informs the pharmacist in charge. At the refill date, a polite reminder brings him back. The branch reviews its KPIs weekly: repeat visits are up, complaints are down, and the average basket is steady; the manager does not set a pressure target.

## Key takeaways

- Closing is a service: ask for the decision, confirm details, give a next step.
- Stop explaining once the customer is ready; use the closing checklist every time.
- Loyalty is trust first, a card second; present only what the programme really offers and protect customer data.
- Follow up by phone or WhatsApp only with consent, briefly and about the customer's need.
- KPIs such as conversion and average basket guide improvement; read them together with repeat visits and feedback.
- Never follow a target that harms the customer; raise it with the manager.
