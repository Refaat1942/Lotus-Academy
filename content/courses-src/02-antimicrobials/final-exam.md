# Final Exam — Antimicrobials & Antivirals in Egypt

## Final Exam (20 questions)

**Q1.** A father asks for "a strong antibiotic" for his 6-year-old who has had a runny nose, mild cough and no fever for 3 days. He has no prescription. What is the best response?
- a) Sell amoxicillin for 3 days because the child is young
- b) Explain the illness is most likely viral, offer supportive care, decline the antibiotic, and give clear red flags for seeing a doctor ✓
- c) Sell azithromycin because it is only a 3-day course
- d) Sell an antibiotic if he promises to see a doctor later

**Q2.** A prescription for a Reserve antibiotic (linezolid tablets) arrives from a general practitioner for a simple skin infection. What is the most appropriate stewardship action?
- a) Dispense it without comment because it is a valid prescription
- b) Replace it yourself with cephalexin
- c) Refuse and tell the patient the doctor made a mistake
- d) Contact the prescriber politely to confirm the indication, since Reserve antibiotics are kept for resistant infections ✓

**Q3.** During a busy evening, a regular customer asks to buy ciprofloxacin for "burning urine" as he did last year, without a prescription. What should the pharmacist do?
- a) Decline to dispense without a prescription, explain local fluoroquinolone resistance, and refer him for assessment ✓
- b) Dispense because he has used it before
- c) Dispense a shorter 3-day course to reduce resistance
- d) Dispense nitrofurantoin instead without a prescription

**Q4.** A patient says she had "a rash" with amoxicillin years ago and now has a prescription for cefuroxime. What is the first step?
- a) Refuse all antibiotics permanently
- b) Dispense without asking because cephalosporins are unrelated to penicillins
- c) Ask exactly what happened (timing, type of rash, swelling or breathing problems) and confirm with the prescriber if the reaction was severe or unclear ✓
- d) Advise her to take an antihistamine with the first dose and continue

**Q5.** A patient on Augmentin for 5 days now has 8 watery stools a day, fever and severe abdominal pain. What should the pharmacist do?
- a) Recommend loperamide and continue Augmentin
- b) Advise probiotics only and review in a week
- c) Refer urgently, as these are red flags for C. difficile or severe infection ✓
- d) Advise doubling the Augmentin dose to clear the infection

**Q6.** A mother collects Augmentin suspension for her child. Which counselling is correct?
- a) Store as the leaflet directs (usually in the fridge after mixing), shake well, measure with an oral syringe, and complete the course ✓
- b) Keep it in a warm place and use a kitchen spoon
- c) Give the whole bottle over 2 days for a faster effect
- d) Stop as soon as the fever drops and keep the rest for next time

**Q7.** A 68-year-old on prednisolone is prescribed levofloxacin. Which counselling point is most important?
- a) Take with milk to improve absorption
- b) Expect orange urine
- c) No alcohol for 48 hours after the course
- d) Stop and contact the doctor if tendon pain occurs, as age and steroids increase the risk of tendon rupture ✓

**Q8.** A patient on simvastatin is prescribed clarithromycin for H. pylori eradication. What should the pharmacist do?
- a) Dispense with no comment
- b) Contact the prescriber, as clarithromycin raises simvastatin levels and the risk of myopathy; the statin is often paused during treatment ✓
- c) Advise taking the statin twice daily instead
- d) Replace clarithromycin with ciprofloxacin

**Q9.** A teenager starting doxycycline for acne asks how to take it. Which advice is correct?
- a) Take it lying down at bedtime with a small sip of water
- b) Take it with milk to protect the stomach
- c) It is safe to stop sunscreen while on doxycycline
- d) Swallow with a full glass of water, stay upright for 30 minutes, separate from antacids or iron, and use sun protection ✓

**Q10.** A patient on Flagyl for a dental abscess will attend a wedding tomorrow where alcohol will be served. Which advice is correct?
- a) One small drink is safe if taken with food
- b) Avoid alcohol completely during the course and for 48 hours after the last dose ✓
- c) Skip the Flagyl dose on the wedding day and drink normally
- d) Alcohol only matters with antifungal medicines

**Q11.** A grandmother asks for Antinal for her 18-month-old grandson who has had watery diarrhoea since morning. He is drinking well and passing urine. What is the best advice?
- a) ORS and zinc with continued feeding, and refer if blood in stool, high fever, persistent vomiting or signs of dehydration appear ✓
- b) Antinal is the first-line treatment and replaces ORS
- c) Ciprofloxacin syrup for 5 days
- d) Stop all fluids and food for 24 hours

**Q12.** A pregnant woman in her first trimester asks for albendazole because her older child has worms. What is the correct approach?
- a) Sell albendazole for the whole family including her
- b) Sell mebendazole for her instead
- c) Treat the child as prescribed, avoid anthelmintics for her, give hygiene advice and refer her to her doctor ✓
- d) Advise praziquantel instead because it is safer

**Q13.** A woman on rifampicin-containing TB treatment asks whether she can rely on her combined contraceptive pill. What is the correct advice?
- a) No — rifampicin reduces the pill's effect; use condoms now and see her doctor for a suitable method such as an IUD or injection ✓
- b) Yes — rifampicin does not interact with contraceptives
- c) Double the pill dose on her own
- d) Stop the TB treatment until she has a new method

**Q14.** A patient on isoniazid complains of tingling and burning in the feet. What is the most likely explanation and action?
- a) Rifampicin is causing orange discoloration; reassure
- b) Ethambutol is causing optic neuritis; check vision
- c) It is normal and should be ignored
- d) Isoniazid-related peripheral neuropathy; check that pyridoxine is prescribed and refer to the treating doctor ✓

**Q15.** A customer with a history of warfarin use asks for miconazole oral gel for mouth thrush. What is the safest response?
- a) Sell it; topical products never interact
- b) Sell a double amount for faster results
- c) Do not dispense without the prescriber's agreement, because miconazole oral gel can markedly raise the INR ✓
- d) Advise stopping warfarin while using the gel

**Q16.** A woman asks for her third fluconazole 150mg dose this month for vaginal itching. What is the best response?
- a) Sell a pack of four so she has supplies at home
- b) Explain that frequent recurrences need a doctor's assessment for causes such as diabetes, offer suitable short-term relief if appropriate, and refer ✓
- c) Advise doubling the dose to 300mg
- d) Recommend an oral antibiotic

**Q17.** A 35-year-old has had flu symptoms for 5 days and asks for Tamiflu without a prescription. He is otherwise healthy and breathing normally. What is the best response?
- a) Sell Tamiflu because it works at any stage
- b) Sell an antibiotic instead to prevent complications
- c) Explain that antivirals help most within 48 hours and need a prescription; offer symptomatic care and give red flags for urgent review ✓
- d) Advise dexamethasone to reduce the fever

**Q18.** A 60-year-old presents with a painful band-like rash on one side of the forehead extending to the tip of the nose, which started yesterday. What should the pharmacist do?
- a) Refer urgently because shingles near the eye or nose risks eye involvement ✓
- b) Sell acyclovir cream and review in a week
- c) Recommend an antifungal cream
- d) Advise waiting because shingles resolves alone

**Q19.** An elderly patient on warfarin, atorvastatin and bisoprolol brings a prescription for clarithromycin plus ciprofloxacin for pneumonia. His last INR was 5.2. What is the priority action?
- a) Dispense both and advise taking them with food
- b) Dispense only ciprofloxacin
- c) Advise him to stop warfarin himself
- d) Contact the prescriber before dispensing about the raised INR, QT risk, statin interaction and dual antibiotic therapy ✓

**Q20.** A patient on amoxicillin-clavulanate for a sinus infection still has a high fever and feels worse after 3 days of correct use. What is the right action?
- a) Sell a second antibiotic to add to the first
