## Course description

Evidence-based training on antibacterial, antifungal, antiparasitic and antiviral medicines in the Egyptian market. The course covers the WHO AWaRe classification, Egyptian Drug Authority (EDA) dispensing rules, local resistance patterns, antimicrobial stewardship, identification of the brands and generics that Lotus customers bring to the counter, and patient counselling in Arabic.

The course is written for pharmacists and pharmacy staff in Lotus pharmacies. In daily work it changes how you handle the most common antimicrobial situations at the counter: a mother asking for an antibiotic for a child's cold, a prescription for ciprofloxacin for simple cystitis, a customer on warfarin who has just been prescribed clarithromycin, a patient on Flagyl invited to a wedding, or a woman on tuberculosis treatment who relies on the contraceptive pill. You will learn when to dispense with good counselling, when to call the prescriber and when to refer.

The course is organised in eight lessons. Lesson 1 sets the stewardship and regulatory framework. Lessons 2 to 4 cover the main antibacterial classes, metronidazole and antiparasitics. Lessons 5 to 7 cover tuberculosis, antifungal and antiviral medicines. Lesson 8 brings everything together in resistance and stewardship practice. Each lesson ends with a 10-question quiz and a 20-question final exam covers the whole course. Pharmacists counsel and refer; they do not diagnose. Always follow the current rules of the Egyptian Drug Authority and the Ministry of Health and the Lotus SOP.

## Learning outcomes

1. Apply **antimicrobial stewardship** principles according to the WHO AWaRe classification and Egyptian Ministry of Health guidance
2. Identify **40+ antimicrobial brands** and generics from Pharco, EIPICO, Memphis, GSK, Sanofi and other suppliers in the Egyptian market
3. Select and check appropriate empiric therapy for common infections seen in Egyptian pharmacies
4. Counsel on **adherence, resistance prevention** and adverse effects in Arabic
5. Recognise **controlled antimicrobials** and OTC misuse (nifuroxazide, Antinal)
6. Screen antimicrobial prescriptions for key drug interactions, including warfarin, statins, oral contraceptives and QT-prolonging drugs
7. Decide when to dispense, when to contact the prescriber and when to refer a patient urgently
