## 1. Egyptian resistance summary

| Pathogen / setting | Key resistance patterns |
|---|---|
| Community UTI (E. coli) | ESBL reported in about 30–50% of hospital isolates; fluoroquinolone resistance above 50% in many centres |
| Healthcare-associated | MRSA, VRE and carbapenem-resistant Enterobacteriaceae (CRE) emerging |
| Respiratory (outpatient) | S. pneumoniae penicillin and macrolide resistance variable |
| GI | Growing awareness of C. difficile after antibiotic use |
| TB | MDR-TB about 3% of new cases; XDR-TB managed in specialised centres |

These figures come from published surveillance and hospital studies and vary between settings. The message for the counter is consistent: avoid unnecessary Watch antibiotics, especially fluoroquinolones for simple UTI, and never dispense antibiotics without a prescription.

## 2. Stewardship interventions checklist

| Action | Example |
|---|---|
| Validate the prescription | Are the indication, dose and duration appropriate? |
| Substitute wisely | Offer a generic Access antibiotic when it is equivalent and the prescriber agrees |
| Refuse inappropriate OTC requests | No antibiotic without a prescription |
| Counsel completion | Full course even when feeling better; no sharing, no leftovers |
| Drug interaction screening | Macrolide + statin; metronidazole + warfarin; fluoroquinolone + QT drugs; rifampicin + contraceptives |
| Refer | Infection not improving after 48–72 hours on an appropriate antibiotic |

### 2.1 Counselling that reduces antibiotic demand

Customers ask for antibiotics because they want fast relief. Effective counselling acknowledges this and offers something useful:

- Name the illness and its expected course: "This is a viral cold; it usually improves in 7 to 10 days"
- Offer symptomatic relief: saline, honey for cough in children over 1 year, paracetamol, fluids
- Give clear return conditions: high fever, breathing difficulty, symptoms beyond 10 days
- Avoid blaming the customer or the doctor; keep a respectful tone

### 2.2 Reporting pathways

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Suspected treatment failure (no improvement after 48–72 hours) | Refer to the prescriber; suggest that a culture may be needed |
| Suspected serious adverse drug reaction | Report to the Egyptian Pharmacovigilance Center (EDA) following the Lotus SOP, and inform the prescriber |
| Suspected MDR-TB, CRE or other highly resistant infection | Refer to specialist centres; do not attempt OTC management |
| Suspected falsified or substandard antimicrobial | Quarantine the stock and report according to EDA rules and the Lotus SOP |

## 3. Integrated case — Egyptian polypharmacy and infection

> A 72-year-old on Marevan (warfarin), Lipitor (atorvastatin) and Concor (bisoprolol) is prescribed Klacid + Ciprobay for community-acquired pneumonia. His latest INR is 5.2.

**Analysis:**

- Dual antibiotic therapy is questionable for outpatient CAP — a stewardship and interaction concern
- Clarithromycin + ciprofloxacin — additive QT prolongation risk in an elderly patient
- Clarithromycin raises atorvastatin levels (myopathy) and the warfarin effect; ciprofloxacin may also raise the INR
- An INR of 5.2 is already above the usual target — urgent INR management and physician contact before dispensing

Pharmacist (to the prescriber): "Your patient's INR is 5.2, and the combination of clarithromycin and ciprofloxacin may raise it further and prolong the QT interval. Would you like to review the antibiotic choice and his warfarin and statin plan?"

**Counselling script (Arabic):**

> "المقاومة معناها إن البكتيريا بتتعلم تقاوم الدوا. كل ما نستخدم المضادات غلط، المشكلة بتكبر. الصيدلي دوره يتأكد إن الروشتة مناسبة ويشرحلك تاخد الدوا صح."

In English: resistance means bacteria learn to resist the medicine. Every time antibiotics are used wrongly, the problem grows. The pharmacist's role is to make sure the prescription is suitable and to explain how to take it correctly.

## Key takeaways

- Egyptian E. coli shows high fluoroquinolone and ESBL resistance
- The pharmacy is the frontline: refuse antibiotic dispensing without a prescription
- Macrolide + warfarin + statin is a dangerous combination in the elderly
- AWaRe guides empiric antibiotic choice — Access first when appropriate
- Refer MDR-TB and CRE to specialist centres; report serious adverse reactions to EDA pharmacovigilance
- Course 2 is complete — the next course covers diabetes medicines
