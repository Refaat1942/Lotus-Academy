## 1. Mechanism & resistance

Penicillins and cephalosporins are beta-lactam antibiotics. Their action and the main resistance mechanism can be summarised in three steps:

- **Beta-lactam ring** binds penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs) → inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis → bactericidal effect
- **Beta-lactamase enzymes** produced by some bacteria hydrolyse the ring → resistance
- **Clavulanate or sulbactam** are beta-lactamase inhibitors → they protect the beta-lactam and restore activity

**Evidence:** Amoxicillin and amoxicillin-clavulanate are WHO AWaRe Access antibiotics and are first-line options for many community infections in Egyptian practice.

### 2.1 Dosing & counselling

| Drug | Adult dosing | Key counselling |
|---|---|---|
| Amoxicillin | 500mg TDS or 1g BD (as prescribed) | Take with or without food; complete the prescribed course (often 5–7 days) |
| Augmentin 625mg | 1 tab TDS | Take at the start of a meal — reduces GI upset |
| Augmentin suspension | Weight-based in children | Store as the leaflet says (usually in the fridge after mixing); shake well; measure with an oral syringe; discard after the stated days |

**Contraindications:** True penicillin allergy, especially anaphylaxis. **Interactions:** probenecid increases penicillin levels; allopurinol with amoxicillin or ampicillin increases the risk of skin rash.

**Counselling script (Arabic):**

> "أوجمنتين يتاخد مع أول الأكل عشان يقلل المغص. لو ظهر إسهال مائي شديد أو طفح حمراء، وقف الدواء وكلم الدكتور. خلّي الكورس كامل حتى لو تحسنت."

In English: take Augmentin at the start of a meal to reduce stomach cramps. If severe watery diarrhoea or a red rash appears, stop and call the doctor. Finish the course even if you feel better.

In Egypt, amoxicillin-clavulanate is sometimes co-prescribed with a cephalosporin. Two beta-lactams together rarely add benefit; query such duplication with the prescriber.

### 3.1 Penicillin allergy & cephalosporins

Always ask: "What happened when you took penicillin or amoxicillin?" The answer guides what you do.

| Allergy history | What the pharmacist does |
|---|---|
| Mild, delayed rash (non-IgE) | Cephalosporins are often tolerated, but confirm with the prescriber and counsel the patient to stop and seek help if a rash appears |
| Anaphylaxis, swelling of face or throat, breathing difficulty after penicillin | Do not dispense any beta-lactam without the prescriber's explicit confirmation; avoid 1st generation cephalosporins; cross-reactivity with 3rd/4th generation is low (about 1–2%) but still a decision for the prescriber |
| Unknown or unclear allergy history | Clarify with the patient and the physician before dispensing |
| Side effect only (for example nausea or diarrhoea) | Not an allergy — record it correctly and counsel |

## 4. Pharmacy practice scenario

### Scenario 2.2 — Augmentin diarrhoea

> A patient on Augmentin 625mg TDS, day 4 for sinusitis, reports watery diarrhoea 3 times a day, no blood, mild cramping.

**Model response:**

1. Recognise common antibiotic-associated diarrhoea (reported in up to 25% of patients on amoxicillin-clavulanate)
2. Counsel on hydration (ORS if needed); probiotics may help; take doses with meals
3. Red flags suggesting C. difficile or severe illness: blood in stool, fever, severe abdominal pain, many watery stools a day, signs of dehydration — urgent referral
4. Do not stop the antibiotic automatically — contact the prescriber if diarrhoea is moderate to severe
5. Do not give loperamide without physician advice in possible infectious or antibiotic-associated diarrhoea

Pharmacist: "Mild loose stools are common with Augmentin. Keep taking it with food and drink plenty of fluids. If you see blood, get a fever or strong pain, or the diarrhoea becomes frequent, contact the doctor straight away."

## Key takeaways

- Augmentin (GSK) and Clavimox are widely used amoxicillin-clavulanate brands in Egypt
- Ceporex (cephalexin) is a first-line 1st generation cephalosporin for uncomplicated UTI and skin infections
- Velosef (cefradine) is a common 1st generation cephalosporin in capsule and suspension forms
- Take Augmentin at the start of a meal to reduce GI effects
- True penicillin anaphylaxis — do not give a cephalosporin without the prescriber's confirmation
- Access beta-lactams should be prioritised over fluoroquinolones when appropriate
