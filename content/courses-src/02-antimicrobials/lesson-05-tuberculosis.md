## 1. First-line anti-TB drugs (RIPE)

| Drug | Mechanism | Egyptian brands | Key toxicity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rifampicin (R)** | Inhibits bacterial RNA polymerase | Rifadin, Rimactane, EIPICO generic | Hepatotoxicity, orange body fluids, strong CYP450 inducer |
| **Isoniazid (H)** | Inhibits mycolic acid synthesis | Hyzide, Isoniazid (Memphis) | Hepatotoxicity, peripheral neuropathy |
| **Pyrazinamide (Z)** | Not fully understood — active in acidic environments | Pyrazinamide generic | Hepatotoxicity, hyperuricaemia (gout) |
| **Ethambutol (E)** | Inhibits arabinosyl transferase | Myambutol, generic | Optic neuritis (blurred vision, loss of red-green colour vision) |

### 1.1 Fixed-dose combinations (Egypt)

| FDC | Contents | Phase |
|---|---|---|
| **4FDC** | RHZE — 4 drugs | Intensive phase, 2 months |
| **2FDC** | RH — rifampicin + isoniazid | Continuation phase, 4 months |

**Standard regimen:** 2HRZE/4HR — 6 months in total for drug-sensitive TB. FDCs reduce the number of tablets and prevent patients from taking only one drug, which would cause resistance.

## 2. Egypt National TB Programme

| Element | Details |
|---|---|
| **Incidence** | Roughly 11–15 per 100,000 (WHO estimate; higher in crowded urban areas) |
| **MDR-TB** | About 3% of new cases; higher in previously treated patients |
| **DOTS** | Directly Observed Treatment, Short-course — supervised dosing |
| **Pharmacy role** | Refer suspected cases; support adherence counselling; no unsupervised TB drug dispensing outside the National TB Programme (NTP) protocol |
| **BCG** | Part of the routine neonatal vaccination schedule in Egypt |

**Refer for TB assessment:** cough for more than 2 weeks, coughing blood, night sweats, fever, weight loss — especially with a TB contact at home. Do not sell repeated cough medicines or antibiotics to a patient with these symptoms.

**Rifampicin interactions (critical):**

| Drug affected | Effect |
|---|---|
| Warfarin (Marevan) | INR falls markedly — the prescriber must monitor and adjust the dose |
| Oral contraceptives | Reduced efficacy — an alternative or additional method is needed |
| Antiretrovirals | Complex — specialist management |
| Oral hypoglycaemics (for example sulfonylureas) | Lower levels — monitor blood glucose |

**INH + pyridoxine:** pyridoxine (vitamin B6), commonly 25mg once daily, is co-prescribed to prevent peripheral neuropathy.

**Hepatotoxicity warning signs:** yellow eyes or skin, dark urine, persistent nausea or vomiting, abdominal pain, severe tiredness — refer to the doctor the same day.

**Counselling script (Arabic):**

> "أدوية السل لازم تتاخد كل يوم في نفس الوقت لمدة 6 شهور كاملة — حتى لو حسيت بتحسن من أول شهر. البول والعرق ممكن يلونوا برتقالي — ده طبيعي من الريفامبيسين. لو حسيت اصفرار في العين أو تعب شديد، روح للدكتور فوراً. متوقفش الدوا من نفسك أبداً."

In English: TB medicines must be taken every day at the same time for a full 6 months, even if you feel better in the first month. Urine and sweat may turn orange — this is normal with rifampicin (soft contact lenses can be stained). If your eyes turn yellow or you feel very tired, see the doctor at once. Never stop the medicine yourself.

## 3. Second-line / MDR-TB (awareness)

| Drug | Use |
|---|---|
| Levofloxacin / moxifloxacin | MDR-TB regimens |
| Bedaquiline | MDR-TB (specialised centres) |
| Linezolid | MDR-TB |
| Cycloserine | MDR-TB |

MDR-TB drugs are dispensed through NTP-designated centres only. A community pharmacist's role is to support adherence and check interactions.

## 4. Pharmacy scenario

### Scenario 2.5 — TB patient on the combined pill

> A woman on the RHZE regimen, starting month 1, takes Microgynon (combined oral contraceptive) and asks if it is still safe.

**Model response:**

1. Rifampicin induces liver enzymes → lowers ethinylestradiol levels → risk of contraceptive failure
2. Refer promptly to her physician or family-planning clinic for an alternative: copper IUD, levonorgestrel IUD, progestogen injection, plus condoms in the meantime
3. Counsel TB adherence — 6 months minimum, every day
4. Document the interaction counselling
5. Do not recommend "doubling" the pill dose without specialist advice

Pharmacist: "Your TB medicine makes the pill much weaker, so it may not protect you from pregnancy. Please use condoms from today and speak to your doctor this week about a method that works with rifampicin. Do not stop your TB tablets."

## Key takeaways

- RIPE regimen: 2 months intensive (RHZE) + 4 months continuation (RH)
- Rifampicin: orange body fluids are normal; it is a strong enzyme inducer
- Isoniazid requires pyridoxine co-prescription to prevent neuropathy
- DOTS — directly observed therapy through Egypt's National TB Programme
- Rifampicin makes oral contraceptives unreliable and lowers the warfarin INR
- MDR-TB (about 3% of new cases) — second-line drugs only through specialist centres
