## 1. Antiplatelet agents

### 1.1 Mechanism

Platelet aggregation pathways and where drugs act:

- **Arachidonic acid → thromboxane A2:** aspirin blocks COX-1 irreversibly
- **ADP → P2Y12 receptor:** clopidogrel, ticagrelor and prasugrel block this receptor
- **Glycoprotein IIb/IIIa:** blocked by injectable agents such as abciximab (hospital use only)

**Evidence:** CAPRIE — clopidogrel modestly superior to aspirin in peripheral arterial disease. PLATO — ticagrelor superior to clopidogrel after acute coronary syndrome (ACS).

### 1.3 Aspirin counseling

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| **Dose** | 75–100 mg OD for CV prevention; 300 mg loading dose in ACS |
| **Contraindications** | Active GI bleeding, aspirin-sensitive asthma, children under 16 (Reye syndrome) |
| **Interactions** | ↑ bleeding with NSAIDs, warfarin, SSRIs; ibuprofen may block aspirin's cardioprotection if taken before aspirin |

## 2. Anticoagulants

### 2.1 Warfarin (vitamin K antagonist)

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| **Brand (Egypt)** | Marevan, warfarin generic (EIPICO) |
| **Mechanism** | Inhibits vitamin K epoxide reductase → ↓ factors II, VII, IX, X |
| **Monitoring** | INR target 2–3 (AF, VTE); 2.5–3.5 for a mechanical mitral valve |
| **Dosing** | Individualized; typical Egyptian maintenance 3–5 mg OD |
| **Approx. cost** | 30–80 EGP/month — very affordable |

**Warfarin interactions (critical in Egypt):**

| ↑ INR (bleeding risk) | ↓ INR (clot risk) |
|---|---|
| Amiodarone (Cordarone — very common) | Rifampicin (TB treatment is common) |
| Metronidazole (Flagyl) | Carbamazepine |
| Fluconazole (Diflucan) | Phenytoin |
| Paracetamol at high regular doses | Hypericum (herbal antidepressant) |
| Cranberry juice (large amounts) | Vitamin K-rich foods (variable intake) |

### 2.2 Direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs)

| Generic | Brand (Egypt) | Target | Renal dosing | Approx. EGP/month |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Rivaroxaban** | Xarelto | Factor Xa | Avoid if CrCl <15; reduce if CrCl 15–49 | 600–1200 |
| **Apixaban** | Eliquis | Factor Xa | Reduce if ≥2 of: age ≥80, weight ≤60 kg, creatinine ≥1.5 mg/dL | 700–1400 |
| **Dabigatran** | Pradaxa | Thrombin (IIa) | Avoid if CrCl <30 | 500–1000 |
| **Edoxaban** | Lixiana | Factor Xa | Limited availability in Egypt | 600–1100 |

**DOAC advantages:** no routine INR monitoring; fixed dosing; rapid onset.
**DOAC cautions:** specific reversal agents are not widely available (andexanet for apixaban/rivaroxaban is limited); renal dose adjustment is essential.

**Counseling script — warfarin (Arabic):**

> "الماريفان لازم يتاخد في نفس الساعة كل يوم. لازم تحلل INR بانتظام حسب ما الدكتور قال. الخضروات الورقية زي الجرجير والسبانخ فيها فيتامين ك — متغيرش كميتها فجأة، خليك ثابت. لو لاحظت نزيف من اللثة، أو بول لونه أحمر أو غامق، أو براز أسود، أو كدمات من غير سبب، روح للدكتور فوراً. متاخدش أي دواء جديد من الصيدلية من غير ما تقول إنك على ماريفان."

*English meaning:* take Marevan at the same hour every day and do INR tests regularly as your doctor said. Leafy vegetables such as rocket and spinach contain vitamin K — do not change the amount suddenly, keep it steady. If you notice bleeding gums, red or dark urine, black stools or unexplained bruising, see the doctor immediately. Never take a new medicine from the pharmacy without saying that you are on Marevan.

## 3. Indications summary

| Condition | Standard therapy (Egypt) |
|---|---|
| AF (non-valvular) | DOAC preferred over warfarin (ESC) if affordable; warfarin if cost-limited |
| Mechanical heart valve | Warfarin ONLY — DOACs contraindicated |
| DVT/PE | DOAC or warfarin for 3–6+ months |
| After PCI (ACS) | DAPT: aspirin + clopidogrel/ticagrelor, usually 12 months |
| Stroke prevention | Clopidogrel or aspirin per guideline |

When an anticoagulant is combined with dual antiplatelet therapy after PCI ("triple therapy"), bleeding risk rises sharply; the cardiologist keeps this period as short as possible. The pharmacy's role is to reinforce bleeding-sign counseling and to make sure no NSAID is added.

## 4. Pharmacy practice scenario

### Scenario 1.6 — Warfarin + antibiotic

> *A customer on Marevan 5 mg daily (INR usually 2.5) has been prescribed Flagyl (metronidazole) for a dental infection. INR today is 4.8.*

**Response:**

1. Recognize the metronidazole–warfarin interaction — potent inhibition of warfarin metabolism
2. Urgent physician contact — the doctor will likely hold or reduce warfarin doses
3. Counsel on bleeding signs; avoid NSAIDs
4. Document the interaction in the customer's record for future reference
5. Explain that the INR should be rechecked within 2–3 days and again after the antibiotic course

---

## Key takeaways

- Aspocid (aspirin 75–100 mg) is a cornerstone of CV prevention and very cheap in Egypt
- Plavix (clopidogrel) — Amoun/Memphis generics significantly reduce cost
- Marevan (warfarin) is still widely used; it requires INR monitoring and has many interactions
- Xarelto and Eliquis are available but costly — mainly insurance/private sector
- Mechanical valves: warfarin is mandatory — never substitute a DOAC
- Flagyl, Diflucan and Cordarone are dangerous warfarin interactions in Egyptian practice
- Every anticoagulated customer should know the bleeding warning signs and declare the medicine before buying anything new
