## 1. Integrated treatment algorithm review

### 1.1 Hypertension step therapy

1. **Step 1:** ACE-I/ARB OR CCB OR thiazide (equal first line)
2. **Step 2 (not at target):** dual therapy — common Egyptian FDCs: Co-Tareg, Exforge, Tritace Plus, Coveram, Egyptian generics
3. **Step 3 (not at target):** triple therapy — ARB/ACE-I + CCB + diuretic (e.g. Exforge HCT)
4. **Step 4 (resistant):** add spironolactone and refer to a specialist

### 1.2 ASCVD prevention bundle

| Component | Egyptian brands |
|---|---|
| Antiplatelet (when indicated) | Aspocid 100 mg |
| Statin | Ator (Amoun), Lipitor |
| BP control | Norvasc + Tareg (or local generics) |
| Diabetes | Metformin + SGLT2 inhibitor if indicated |

## 3. Comprehensive case scenarios

### Case 1 — Diabetic hypertensive (common Egyptian presentation)

> *A 58-year-old man. BP 158/92. HbA1c 8.2%. LDL 145 mg/dL. No prior CV events. Currently on metformin only.*

**Pharmacy assessment:**

- High to very high CV risk (diabetes + HTN + dyslipidemia)
- Recommend a physician visit for: ACE-I/ARB + statin + possibly an SGLT2 inhibitor
- Explain the targets the doctor is likely to aim for: BP <130/80, LDL <55–70 mg/dL
- Counsel lifestyle: salt, exercise, smoking cessation
- Cost-conscious options once prescribed: a losartan or valsartan generic + Ator (Amoun) + an amlodipine generic

### Case 2 — Post-MI polypharmacy

> *A 62-year-old woman, 3 months after a myocardial infarction. Medications: Aspocid, Plavix, Lipitor 40 mg, Concor 5 mg, ramipril 5 mg, Lasix 20 mg PRN.*

**Key checks:**

- DAPT duration — confirm with the cardiologist before Plavix is ever stopped
- Beta-blocker and ACE-I — long-term unless contraindicated
- High-intensity statin — LDL target <55 mg/dL
- Warn: ibuprofen (Brufen) should be avoided — GI bleeding risk on DAPT, fluid retention and loss of cardioprotection
- Arabic counseling on adherence to ALL drugs — stopping Concor abruptly is dangerous

### Case 3 — AF on warfarin with a new prescription

> *A 70-year-old man. Marevan 5 mg, INR 2.3. New prescription: Ciprobay (ciprofloxacin) for a urinary tract infection.*

**Action:**

- Fluoroquinolones usually increase the INR (the effect varies between patients)
- Notify the physician so that the INR is monitored more closely during and after the course
- Counsel on bleeding signs (and clot warning signs)
- No OTC NSAIDs

### Case 4 — Resistant HTN with hidden causes

> *A 45-year-old customer on amlodipine 10 mg + losartan 100 mg + HCTZ 25 mg. BP 162/98. Takes Brufen daily, drinks licorice (erk sous) daily and uses a decongestant for a "cold".*

**Pharmacy intervention:**

1. Identify the NSAID and the decongestant (pseudoephedrine) — both raise BP
2. Licorice → pseudoaldosteronism (sodium retention, low potassium, high BP)
3. Counsel stopping the OTC offenders and suggest safer alternatives
4. Refer for a secondary-HTN workup if BP is still uncontrolled

### Case 5 — Heart failure decompensation

> *A 68-year-old customer with known HFrEF. Weight up 4 kg in 5 days, ankle swelling, shortness of breath. On Tritace, Concor, Aldactone, Lasix 40 mg OD.*

**Urgent action:**

1. Recognize decompensation — refer to the physician/hospital the SAME DAY
2. Do NOT advise stopping any GDMT drug
3. The physician may increase the Lasix dose — the pharmacist does not change it
4. Reinforce daily weight monitoring for the future

## 4. Dangerous combinations — Egyptian pharmacy alert list

| Combination | Risk |
|---|---|
| ACE-I + ARB | Renal failure, hyperkalemia |
| ACE-I + K-sparing diuretic + K supplement | Severe hyperkalemia |
| Warfarin + Flagyl/Diflucan/Cordarone | Major bleeding |
| Verapamil + beta-blocker | Severe bradycardia |
| NSAID + antihypertensive | Uncontrolled BP, renal injury |
| Clopidogrel + omeprazole | Reduced antiplatelet effect (debated; pantoprazole often preferred) |
| Simvastatin >20 mg + amlodipine | Myopathy risk |

**Counseling script — polypharmacy (Arabic):**

> "عندك أكتر من دواء للقلب — ده طبيعي وكل واحد ليه دور مهم. متوقفش أي دواء من نفسك. اكتب قائمة بكل أدويتك وورّيها لأي صيدلي قبل ما تاخد دواء جديد. الإيبوبروفين وأدوية البرد والكحة ممكن تضر مع أدوية القلب — اسأل الأول."

*English meaning:* you take more than one heart medicine — this is normal and each has an important role. Do not stop any medicine on your own. Write a list of all your medicines and show it to any pharmacist before taking a new one. Ibuprofen and cold and cough medicines can be harmful with heart medicines — ask first.

## Key takeaways

- Egyptian HTN: about 26% prevalence and about 20% control — pharmacy counseling is critical
- Generic substitution from Pharco, EIPICO, Memphis, Sedico and Amoun can substantially reduce patients' costs
- NSAIDs (Brufen) are a leading hidden cause of uncontrolled HTN and worsening HF
- Warfarin interactions with commonly used Egyptian drugs (Flagyl, Cordarone, ciprofloxacin) require vigilance
- HFrEF requires four-pillar GDMT — never stop a drug without the physician
- Same-day referral for warning signs: rapid weight gain, breathlessness, bleeding, angioedema
- Always counsel in Arabic with culturally relevant dietary advice (salt, pickles, licorice drinks)
