## 1. Calcium channel blockers — mechanism

### 1.1 Pharmacology

CCBs block L-type calcium channels in vascular smooth muscle and myocardium:

- Ca²⁺ influx blocked → smooth muscle relaxation → vasodilation → ↓ BP
- **Dihydropyridines** (amlodipine, nifedipine): vascular selective → peripheral vasodilation
- **Non-dihydropyridines** (verapamil, diltiazem): cardiac + vascular → ↓ heart rate, ↓ contractility + vasodilation

**Evidence:** ALLHAT trial — amlodipine equivalent to chlorthalidone for CV outcomes. ASCOT-BPLA — amlodipine + perindopril superior to atenolol + thiazide for stroke prevention.

## 2. Beta-blockers — mechanism

### 2.1 Pharmacology

- β1 blockade → ↓ heart rate, ↓ contractility, ↓ renin release → ↓ BP
- **β1-selective** (bisoprolol, metoprolol): cardioselective — less bronchospasm
- **Non-selective** (propranolol): blocks β1 + β2 — avoid in asthma

**Evidence:** COMET trial — carvedilol superior to metoprolol tartrate for HF mortality. Post-MI beta-blockers reduce mortality (NNT about 42 over 2 years).

### 2.3 Beta-blocker — contraindications & interactions

| Contraindication | Interaction |
|---|---|
| Severe bradycardia, 2nd/3rd-degree AV block | Verapamil/diltiazem + BB → severe bradycardia |
| Decompensated HF | BB + insulin → masked hypoglycemia |
| Severe asthma (non-selective BB) | Abrupt BB withdrawal → rebound angina/tachycardia |

**Counseling script (Arabic):**

> "لو رجلك بتورم بعد النورفاسك أو الأملوديبين، ده عرض شائع من توسيع الأوعية. ارفع رجلك وقلل الملح. لو الورم زاد، قول للدكتور — ممكن يضيف دواء تاني. ومتوقفش أي دواء لضغط الدم فجأة، خصوصاً الكونكور أو اللوبريسور."

*English meaning:* if your ankles swell after Norvasc or amlodipine, this is a common effect of the vessels widening. Raise your legs and reduce salt. If the swelling increases, tell your doctor — another medicine may be added. And never stop any BP medicine suddenly, especially Concor or Lopressor.

## 3. Clinical selection guide

| Situation | Preferred class |
|---|---|
| Uncomplicated HTN | CCB (amlodipine) — equal first line |
| HTN + angina | Beta-blocker if high heart rate; CCB if vasospastic |
| HFrEF | Beta-blocker (bisoprolol, carvedilol, metoprolol succinate) — evidence-based |
| HTN + asthma | CCB preferred; cardioselective BB only if needed |
| Post-MI | Beta-blocker standard unless contraindicated |
| Peripheral edema on CCB | Add ACE-I or switch class (doctor's decision) |

## 4. Pharmacy practice scenario

### Scenario 1.3 — Ankle swelling on amlodipine

> *A 58-year-old customer has been on amlodipine 10 mg (Norvasc) for 3 months. BP 130/80, but bilateral ankle swelling. No shortness of breath.*

**Response:**

1. Confirm pitting edema of both ankles — a typical, dose-dependent amlodipine effect
2. Screen for heart-failure warning signs (rapid weight gain, breathlessness, swelling rising up the legs) — refer if present
3. Counsel leg elevation, salt reduction and compression stockings
4. Suggest a physician review — adding an ACE-I often reduces CCB edema
5. Do NOT dispense furosemide without a prescription

---

## Key takeaways

- Norvasc/Amlor (amlodipine) is the most prescribed CCB in Egypt; generics cost about 30–80 EGP/month
- CCB ankle edema is common and dose-related; ACE-I co-prescription often helps
- Concor (bisoprolol) and Lopressor (metoprolol) are key Egyptian beta-blocker brands
- Never combine verapamil or diltiazem with a beta-blocker without specialist oversight
- Beta-blockers are NOT first-line for uncomplicated HTN unless there is a specific indication
- Never stop beta-blockers abruptly — the doctor tapers them over 1–2 weeks
- Non-selective propranolol must be avoided in asthma
