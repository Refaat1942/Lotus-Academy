# Final Exam — Cardiovascular Medications in Egypt

## Final Exam (20 questions)

**Q1.** A 50-year-old customer rushes into the branch and asks for a BP check. The first reading is 165/100 mmHg. He takes no medicines. What is the best action?
- a) Let him sit and rest for 5 minutes, repeat the readings, and if they remain high advise a doctor visit and give him a BP log card ✓
- b) Sell him amlodipine 5 mg to start today
- c) Tell him he has Grade 2 hypertension and needs two medicines
- d) Tell him a single reading means nothing and he does not need to follow up

**Q2.** A customer newly told by his doctor that his BP is 145/92 asks what he can do himself alongside the doctor's plan. What is the most appropriate advice?
- a) Nothing — only medicines lower blood pressure
- b) Start a herbal "pressure" supplement instead of seeing the doctor again
- c) Reduce salt and pickles, walk 30 minutes a day, lose weight and stop smoking ✓
- d) Borrow a relative's amlodipine until his appointment

**Q3.** A doctor is choosing a first drug for a customer with uncomplicated hypertension and no other disease. Which drug would NOT normally be the first choice?
- a) Amlodipine
- b) Bisoprolol ✓
- c) Losartan
- d) Indapamide

**Q4.** A customer who started enalapril six weeks ago asks for a cough syrup for a persistent dry cough. He has no fever and no swelling of the face or lips. What is the best response?
- a) Sell a strong cough suppressant and reassure him
- b) Tell him to stop enalapril today and not tell the doctor
- c) Recommend an antibiotic for a chest infection
- d) Explain that this is probably an ACE-inhibitor cough, ask him to keep taking the drug and see his doctor (who may switch to an ARB), and to go to emergency at once if any lip or face swelling appears ✓

**Q5.** A customer on ramipril and spironolactone asks to buy effervescent potassium tablets because a friend said they help leg cramps. What should the pharmacist do?
- a) Sell the potassium tablets — cramps usually mean low potassium
- b) Sell half the usual dose to be safe
- c) Not sell them — this combination already raises potassium and could cause severe hyperkalemia; refer for a potassium test ✓
- d) Recommend a potassium-based salt substitute instead

**Q6.** A 28-year-old woman taking losartan for hypertension tells you she has just discovered she is pregnant. What is the correct advice?
- a) Contact her doctor urgently — ARBs are contraindicated in pregnancy and the doctor must change her treatment ✓
- b) Reassure her that losartan is safe in pregnancy
- c) Advise her to double the dose because BP rises in pregnancy
- d) Advise her to switch herself to ramipril, which is safer

**Q7.** A customer known to have asthma brings a GP prescription for propranolol for palpitations. What is the best action?
- a) Dispense it — there is no concern
- b) Advise him to take it together with his salbutamol inhaler
- c) Change it yourself to atenolol
- d) Contact the prescriber — a non-selective beta-blocker can trigger bronchospasm in asthma ✓

**Q8.** A customer on amlodipine 10 mg has ankle swelling, but he also reports breathlessness on lying flat and a 3 kg weight gain in a week. What should the pharmacist do?
- a) Advise leg elevation only, since ankle swelling is common with amlodipine
- b) Refer him to the doctor the same day — these signs suggest possible heart failure, not just a CCB side effect ✓
- c) Sell furosemide to remove the fluid
- d) Tell him to stop amlodipine immediately

**Q9.** A customer taking bisoprolol for angina is travelling for a week and wants to "give his heart a rest" by stopping the tablets. What is the correct advice?
- a) It is fine to stop for one week
- b) Do not stop suddenly — abrupt withdrawal can cause rebound angina and fast heart rate; take enough supply for the trip ✓
- c) Take half a tablet every other day instead
- d) Switch to verapamil during the trip

**Q10.** A customer on Co-Tareg complains of muscle cramps and weakness. He also takes Brufen every day for knee pain. What is the best management?
- a) Sell potassium chloride sachets
- b) Recommend a stronger NSAID for the knee
- c) Advise a doctor review with potassium and creatinine tests and stop the unsupervised daily ibuprofen ✓
- d) Advise him to double the Co-Tareg dose

**Q11.** A patient on lithium for bipolar disorder receives a new prescription for hydrochlorothiazide. What should the pharmacist do?
- a) Contact the prescriber — thiazides raise lithium levels and can cause lithium toxicity ✓
- b) Dispense it — there is no interaction
- c) Explain that thiazides lower lithium levels
- d) Advise taking the two drugs two hours apart to avoid any interaction

**Q12.** A customer on simvastatin 40 mg receives a new prescription for amlodipine 10 mg. What is the correct action?
- a) Dispense both — there is no issue
- b) Advise increasing simvastatin to 80 mg
- c) Advise stopping amlodipine
- d) Contact the prescriber — simvastatin should not exceed 20 mg with amlodipine, and alternatives such as atorvastatin exist ✓

**Q13.** A customer on atorvastatin reports severe pain in both thighs and dark, cola-coloured urine since yesterday. What is the correct advice?
- a) Take paracetamol and continue the statin
- b) Reduce the statin dose himself
- c) Wait until his next routine appointment
- d) Stop the statin and seek urgent medical care — this may be rhabdomyolysis ✓

**Q14.** A patient after a myocardial infarction is on atorvastatin 40 mg and his LDL is still 90 mg/dL. The doctor wants an add-on drug. Which option fits the lesson?
- a) Gemfibrozil
- b) Ezetimibe (Ezetrol), to help reach the target of below 55 mg/dL ✓
- c) No change — 90 mg/dL is already at target
- d) Stop the statin and use omega-3 instead

**Q15.** A customer on Marevan asks for something for a toothache. What is the safest recommendation?
- a) Ibuprofen 400 mg three times daily
- b) Aspirin 300 mg as needed
- c) Paracetamol at usual doses for a short time, avoiding NSAIDs, and seeing the dentist; regular high doses of paracetamol can raise the INR ✓
- d) Diclofenac tablets plus gel

**Q16.** A customer with a mechanical mitral valve on warfarin heard that "Eliquis needs no blood tests" and wants to switch. What is the correct response?
- a) Explain that DOACs are contraindicated with mechanical valves; he must stay on warfarin and discuss INR control with his doctor ✓
- b) Sell Eliquis and advise stopping warfarin tomorrow
- c) Advise taking Eliquis and warfarin together for a week
- d) Advise switching to aspirin alone instead

**Q17.** Four months after a coronary stent, a customer on aspirin and Plavix says his dentist told him to stop Plavix before an extraction. What should the pharmacist advise?
- a) Stop both aspirin and Plavix for a week
- b) Stop Plavix as the dentist said
- c) Do not stop Plavix without the cardiologist — the dentist and cardiologist must agree on a plan because early interruption risks stent thrombosis ✓
- d) Switch to warfarin for the procedure

**Q18.** An elderly customer on digoxin and furosemide complains of nausea and says lights have yellow halos. What should the pharmacist do?
- a) Sell an antiemetic
- b) Advise taking digoxin with food
- c) Advise doubling the digoxin dose
- d) Arrange urgent medical review — this suggests digoxin toxicity, possibly worsened by low potassium from the diuretic ✓

**Q19.** A patient stable on warfarin has just been started on amiodarone for atrial fibrillation. What is the key action?
- a) Contact the doctor — amiodarone markedly increases the INR, so the warfarin dose is usually reduced with close INR monitoring ✓
- b) No change is needed
- c) Increase the warfarin dose
- d) Advise the patient to stop amiodarone

**Q20.** A regular customer taking seven cardiac medicines, including amlodipine, Co-Tareg and Concor, asks for a cold-and-flu product containing pseudoephedrine and ibuprofen. What is the best approach?
- a) Sell it — it is only for a few days
- b) Check his medicine list and recommend options without a decongestant or NSAID (for example paracetamol, saline nasal spray, steam), with advice to see the doctor if symptoms worsen ✓
- c) Sell a double-strength version for faster relief
- d) Refuse to help him at all
