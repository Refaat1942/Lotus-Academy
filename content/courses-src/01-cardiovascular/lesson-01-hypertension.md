## 1. Definition and epidemiology

### 1.1 Blood pressure classification (ESC/ESH 2023)

| Category | Systolic (mmHg) | Diastolic (mmHg) |
|---|---|---|
| Optimal | <120 | <80 |
| Normal | 120–129 | 80–84 |
| High normal | 130–139 | 85–89 |
| **Grade 1 HTN** | 140–159 | 90–99 |
| **Grade 2 HTN** | 160–179 | 100–109 |
| **Grade 3 HTN** | ≥180 | ≥110 |
| Isolated systolic HTN | ≥140 | <90 |

**Egyptian prevalence data:**

- Adult hypertension: **26.3%** (WHO STEPS Egypt, various surveys 2017–2022)
- Awareness: about 50% know they have HTN
- Treatment: about 40% on medication
- Control: only **about 20%** achieve target BP — a **major pharmacy intervention opportunity**

### 1.2 Cardiovascular risk in Egypt

| Risk factor | Prevalence/significance |
|---|---|
| Obesity | Rising — 35%+ overweight/obese adults |
| Diabetes | 15.6% (IDF Diabetes Atlas Egypt) — doubles CV risk |
| Smoking | About 20% of adult males |
| Salt intake | High — Egyptian diet (pickled foods, processed foods) |
| Physical inactivity | Urban lifestyle |
| Family history | Common presentation in pharmacy |

**Risk calculation:** the doctor uses SCORE2 (or an Egyptian adaptation) to estimate 10-year cardiovascular risk. The pharmacy's role is to notice risk factors and refer, not to calculate a diagnosis.

## 2. Treatment targets

### 2.1 BP targets (ESC 2023 / Egyptian practice)

| Patient group | Target BP (mmHg) |
|---|---|
| General population <65 years | Systolic 120–129 (if tolerated) |
| General — practical Egypt target | **<140/90** |
| Age ≥65 years | Systolic 130–139 |
| Diabetes | **<130/80** (if tolerated) |
| CKD | <140/90 |
| Post-MI / heart failure | <130/80 |

### 2.2 Non-pharmacological management (FIRST LINE)

Lifestyle change comes before, or together with, medicines for every hypertensive patient.

**Counseling script (Arabic):**

> "ضغطك محتاج علاج بنظام حياة قبل أو مع الدواء:
> 1. قلل الملح — ماتزودش الملح على الأكل، قلل المخللات والسلطة المخللة
> 2. امشي 30 دقيقة يومياً
> 3. قلل الوزن — كل 1 كجم بينقص الضغط حوالي 1 ملم زئبق
> 4. بطّل السجاير لو بتدخن
> 5. قلل التوتر والقلق
> التغييرات دي ممكن تنقص الضغط 5–10 وحدات لوحدها."

*English meaning:* reduce salt (no added salt, fewer pickles), walk 30 minutes a day, lose weight (about 1 mmHg per kg lost), stop smoking, reduce stress; together these changes can lower BP by 5–10 points on their own.

**Evidence:** the DASH diet reduces SBP by **8–14 mmHg**; losing 5 kg reduces SBP by about **4–5 mmHg**.

## 3. Pharmacological algorithm — Egyptian practice

### 3.1 First-line agents

The main first-line classes are used in the following steps:

1. **Step 1 — choose ONE of:**
   - ACE inhibitor (e.g. ramipril) OR ARB (e.g. losartan, valsartan)
   - Calcium channel blocker (e.g. amlodipine)
   - Thiazide or thiazide-like diuretic (e.g. HCTZ 12.5–25 mg, indapamide)
   - Beta-blocker — NOT first line unless there is a specific indication (angina, post-MI, heart failure, rate control)
2. **Step 2 (if not at target):** combine TWO drugs from different classes — common Egyptian fixed-dose combination (FDC) products are used at this step.
3. **Step 3:** triple therapy — ACE-I/ARB + CCB + diuretic.

## 4. Pharmacy practice scenarios

### Scenario 1.1 — New diagnosis

> *A 52-year-old customer has a BP of 155/95 measured at the pharmacy. No medication. Asks what to do.*

**Response:**

1. Confirm the measurement (seated and rested for 5 minutes, repeat the reading up to 3 times)
2. Recommend a physician visit for diagnosis and workup (ECG; labs: creatinine, K+, lipids, glucose)
3. Counsel lifestyle modifications (script above)
4. Do NOT start an antihypertensive without a prescription
5. Offer a BP monitoring log card

### Scenario 1.2 — Resistant hypertension

> *A customer on amlodipine 10 mg + ramipril 10 mg + HCTZ 25 mg. BP still 150/90.*

**Response:**

1. Assess adherence with open questions: "كام مرة فاتتك الجرعة الأسبوع ده؟" (How many doses did you miss this week?)
2. Check for NSAID use (ibuprofen is very common in Egypt and raises BP)
3. Check for licorice drinks, oral contraceptives and decongestants
4. Refer to the physician for a resistant-HTN workup (secondary causes such as renal artery stenosis, primary aldosteronism, obstructive sleep apnoea)

## Key takeaways

- Egypt HTN prevalence is about 26%; only about 20% of patients are controlled
- Target <140/90 in general; <130/80 for diabetes and other high-risk patients (if tolerated)
- First-line classes: ACE-I/ARB, CCB and thiazide; beta-blocker only when there is a specific indication
- Lifestyle counseling comes FIRST — DASH diet, salt reduction, exercise, weight loss, no smoking
- The Egyptian market has extensive generic options at lower cost
- NSAID use is a common hidden cause of uncontrolled HTN in Egypt
- Pharmacy readings are a screening tool: confirm properly and refer — never start treatment without a prescription
