## Course description

This course provides comprehensive, evidence-based training on cardiovascular medications available in the Egyptian pharmaceutical market. It is written for pharmacists and pharmacy staff in Lotus pharmacies who dispense and counsel on blood-pressure, lipid, antithrombotic and heart-failure medicines every day, often for older customers on several medicines at once. Learners will master mechanisms of action, Egyptian brand identification, dosing, interactions, patient counseling in Arabic, and pharmacy practice scenarios aligned with current European (ESC/ESH) guidelines and Egyptian Society of Cardiology recommendations.

After the course you will be able to recognise the brand in a customer's hand, check it against the rest of the regimen, spot the dangerous combinations that walk into the pharmacy every day (NSAIDs with antihypertensives, warfarin with common antibiotics, potassium-raising combinations), offer affordable local generic alternatives honestly, and give clear Arabic counseling that improves adherence. The pharmacist counsels and refers; diagnosis and changes to prescribed therapy remain the decision of the doctor.

The course is organised in eight lessons of about 90 minutes each: hypertension guidelines, then the main drug classes one by one (ACE inhibitors and ARBs, calcium channel blockers and beta-blockers, diuretics and fixed-dose combinations), then statins, anticoagulants and antiplatelets, heart failure and antiarrhythmics, and a final integrated review with Egyptian case scenarios. Every lesson ends with a 10-question quiz, and a 20-question final exam covers the whole course. Prices are approximate and must be verified at dispensing; always follow the current rules of the Egyptian Drug Authority / Ministry of Health and the Lotus SOP.

## Learning outcomes

1. Identify **50+ cardiovascular drugs** by generic name and Egyptian trade name
2. Apply **ESC/ESH hypertension guidelines** (target BP <140/90 mmHg general; <130/80 high-risk)
3. Counsel patients on **antihypertensive adherence** using evidence-based techniques
4. Recognize **high-risk interactions** (warfarin, NSAIDs, potassium-sparing combinations)
5. Recommend **appropriate generic substitutes** available from Egyptian manufacturers
6. Manage **common pharmacy scenarios** (BP check, medication refill, side effect complaint)

## Egyptian market context

- **Hypertension prevalence in Egypt:** about 26–30% of adults (WHO STEPwise survey data)
- **Major local manufacturers:** Pharco, EIPICO, Memphis, Sedico, Sigma, Amoun
- **Multinational brands:** Novartis, AstraZeneca, Pfizer, Sanofi — often higher price
- **Generic penetration:** more than 70% of cardiovascular prescriptions in the public sector
