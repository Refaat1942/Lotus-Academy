## 1. Mechanism of action

### 1.1 RAAS pathway

1. The kidney releases **renin**, which converts angiotensinogen into **angiotensin I**.
2. **ACE** (angiotensin-converting enzyme, mainly in the lungs and vessels) converts angiotensin I into **angiotensin II**.
3. Angiotensin II causes **vasoconstriction** and **aldosterone release**.
4. The result is sodium and water retention, a rise in blood pressure and, over time, cardiac remodeling.

**ACE inhibitors** block the angiotensin-converting enzyme → less angiotensin II → vasodilation and less aldosterone. ACE also breaks down bradykinin, so ACE inhibitors allow **bradykinin to accumulate** — the cause of the typical dry cough and a contributor to angioedema.

**ARBs** block the AT1 receptor → the same downstream effect without affecting bradykinin (much less cough).

### 1.2 Clinical effects (evidence)

| Outcome | Evidence | NNT (5 years) |
|---|---|---|
| BP reduction | ACE-I: SBP ↓ 8–10 mmHg | — |
| Heart failure mortality | SOLVD, CONSENSUS trials | 1 in 6 lives saved |
| Post-MI remodeling | SAVE, AIRE trials | Significant LV function preservation |
| Diabetic nephropathy | RENAAL (losartan) | Slows proteinuria progression |
| Stroke prevention | PROGRESS (perindopril) | 1 in 28 for recurrent stroke |

### 2.2 ACE inhibitor side effects

| Effect | Frequency | Management |
|---|---|---|
| Dry cough | 5–20% | Switch to an ARB (doctor's decision) |
| Hyperkalemia | 3–5% | Monitor K+; avoid K+ supplements and K-sparing diuretics |
| Angioedema | 0.1–0.7% | **STOP immediately** — medical emergency |
| First-dose hypotension | Captopril > others | Take the first dose at bedtime; start with a low dose |
| Renal function change | Expected initially | Monitor creatinine — acceptable rise <30% |
| Teratogenic (pregnancy) | Category D | **Contraindicated** — counsel women of childbearing age |

**Counseling script — ACE cough (Arabic):**

> "الكحة الناشفة اللي بدأت بعد الدواء ده ممكن تكون من المادة الفعالة. مش حساسية ومش خطيرة، بس مزعجة. لو استمرت، الدكتور ممكن يغيرك لدواء من مجموعة قريبة (ARB زي اللوسارتان أو الفالسارتان) مابيعملش الكحة دي. بس لو حصل تورم في الشفايف أو الوش أو اللسان، وقف الدواء وروح الطوارئ فوراً."

*English meaning:* the dry cough that started after this medicine may come from the active ingredient; it is not an allergy and not dangerous, but it is annoying. If it continues, the doctor may switch you to a related group (an ARB such as losartan or valsartan) that does not cause this cough. But if your lips, face or tongue swell, stop the medicine and go to the emergency department immediately.

### 3.2 ACE-I vs ARB — when to choose

| Situation | Choice |
|---|---|
| New HTN, no contraindication | ACE-I (cheaper generics in Egypt) |
| ACE cough develops | Switch to ARB (same RAAS benefit) |
| Post-MI with LV dysfunction | ACE-I preferred (more evidence) |
| Diabetic nephropathy | Either — losartan/irbesartan have strong evidence |
| Pregnancy | **Neither** — contraindicated |
| Hyperkalemia risk | Use cautiously; monitor K+ with both classes |
| Cannot tolerate either | CCB or thiazide |

**NEVER combine an ACE-I with an ARB** — increased harm (hyperkalemia, renal failure) without benefit (ONTARGET trial). If both appear on one patient's medicines, contact the prescriber.

## 4. Renal dosing

| eGFR (mL/min) | ACE-I/ARB action |
|---|---|
| >60 | Full dose |
| 30–60 | Start low; monitor creatinine in 1–2 weeks |
| <30 | Use cautiously; nephrology referral; some avoid |
| Dialysis | Specialist dosing — ARBs such as losartan and telmisartan are not removed by dialysis, while several ACE-Is (e.g. captopril, enalapril, lisinopril) are dialyzable |

**Rule:** an acceptable creatinine rise is **<30%** from baseline after starting an ACE-I/ARB. If the rise is >30%, the doctor investigates (for example renal artery stenosis).

## 6. Pharmacy practice scenario

### Scenario 1.2 — Cough and swelling on an ACE inhibitor

> *A 60-year-old customer started ramipril (Tritace) 5 mg six weeks ago. She asks for a cough syrup for a dry, tickly cough that will not go away. No fever, no phlegm.*

**Response:**

1. Ask when the cough started relative to the new medicine — a dry cough weeks after starting an ACE-I is typical ACE cough
2. Ask specifically about any swelling of the lips, tongue, face or throat, or difficulty breathing — if present, this is angioedema: **stop and send to emergency immediately**
3. If there is no swelling, explain the cause using the counseling script; cough syrups will not fix it
4. Advise her not to stop ramipril on her own, and to see her doctor soon — the doctor may switch to an ARB such as losartan or valsartan
5. Check the rest of her medicines for potassium supplements, K-sparing diuretics or NSAIDs

---

## Key takeaways

- ACE inhibitors block angiotensin II formation and allow bradykinin to accumulate — the cause of dry cough
- ARBs (losartan, valsartan, telmisartan…) give the same RAAS benefit with much less cough
- Tareg and Diovan contain **valsartan**; Cozaar contains losartan; Capoten contains captopril; Tritace contains ramipril
- Angioedema (lip, face or tongue swelling) is an emergency — stop the drug immediately
- Never combine an ACE-I with an ARB (ONTARGET); both classes are contraindicated in pregnancy
- A creatinine rise of less than 30% after starting is acceptable; monitor potassium
- Affordable local captopril and losartan generics help long-term adherence
