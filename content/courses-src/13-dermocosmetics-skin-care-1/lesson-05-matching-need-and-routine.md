## Learning objectives

After completing this lesson, you will be able to:

1. Compare Bioderma, Uriage and Avène options from the Lotus sheet for dry, sensitive, oily and acne-prone skin, sun protection and healing.
2. Choose a sunscreen for different customers: oily, dry, sensitive, children, pregnancy or breastfeeding, after procedures and colour needs.
3. Conduct a model consultation from first question to final recommendation.
4. Explain a simple morning and evening routine in plain language.
5. Set realistic expectations about time, patience and what cosmetics cannot do.
6. Offer ethical add-ons, and refer to a doctor at the right moment.

---

## 1. The principle: need first, brand second

All three brands cover the same needs. The Lotus sheet gives usage notes for each product, so the pharmacist's job is to translate the customer's need into the item whose note fits, not to push one brand. If two options fit, offer a simple choice by texture, size, fragrance preference or budget, and let the customer decide. Do not say one brand is "better" than another; the sheet gives no basis for it.

## 4. Building a simple routine

Keep the routine short; people follow what is easy.

**Morning (3 steps)**

1. Cleanse gently (or rinse with water for very dry or sensitive skin).
2. Light moisturiser suited to the skin type.
3. Sunscreen on all exposed skin, re-applied during the day.

**Evening (3 steps)**

1. Remove sunscreen and make-up and cleanse.
2. Treatment if needed (for example an acne or brightening product, as the sheet describes), starting slowly.
3. Moisturiser (richer for dry skin).

**Rules**

- Start with the basics; add one product at a time.
- Patch test every new item.
- No scrubbing every day; exfoliate only as the product describes (Sebium Exfoliating twice a week).
- Do not combine several actives without advice.

**How to say it**

**Pharmacist:** "Think of three steps in the morning: wash, moisturise, protect from the sun. In the evening: wash, treat only if needed, moisturise. We start with a small test, and we add one thing at a time."

## 5. Patience and expectations

- Acne and pigmentation need weeks to months; wrinkles are softened, not erased.
- A mild reaction on day one (slight dryness) is different from burning, swelling or spreading redness: stop and seek advice.
- Photograph progress or check in after a few weeks; offer to follow up.
- Say what cosmetics cannot do: they do not cure disease and they do not replace a doctor's plan.

## 6. Ethical add-ons

An add-on is ethical when it is **relevant, useful and affordable** for this customer.

| Customer is buying | Ethical add-on |
|---|---|
| An acne wash | A light moisturiser and a fluid sunscreen |
| A brightening cream | A daily sunscreen, because the sun darkens spots |
| A wrinkle cream | Daily sunscreen |
| A rich body cream | A gentle cleanser for dry skin |
| A baby sunscreen | Shade advice and a cap, no extra products needed |

Do not add products the customer does not need, do not exceed the budget they state, and never add a product to a sensitive customer's routine just to increase the basket.

## 7. Model consultation

**Customer:** "My skin is oily and I get spots, and everything I try makes it red."
**Pharmacist:** "How does your skin feel after washing, and what are you using now?"
**Customer:** "A strong soap and a scrub every day."
**Pharmacist:** "That can irritate. Are you pregnant or breastfeeding, and do you take any prescribed treatment?"
**Customer:** "No."
**Pharmacist:** "I suggest a gentle daily wash for oily skin, a light moisturiser, and a fluid sunscreen. Stop the daily scrub. Test each new product and start one at a time. Give it a few weeks. If the spots are deep or painful, please see a dermatologist."

## 8. When to refer

- A changing mole or new growing spot.
- Eczema-like patches, oozing, crusting or severe itch.
- Moderate, advanced, painful or scarring acne.
- A baby's spreading or weeping rash.
- Large, blistering or deep burns, or wounds that do not heal.
- Eye redness, pain or swelling.
- No improvement after a reasonable trial of a suitable routine.

## Scenario

A pregnant customer, with sensitive and slightly oily skin, asks for a sunscreen and "something for the spots on my chin". The pharmacist asks about her routine and allergies, and chooses the sunscreen listed for very sensitive skin and for pregnant and breastfeeding customers, with a patch test. For the spots, the pharmacist avoids recommending any acne treatment without her doctor's agreement, suggests a gentle daily wash and light moisturiser, and advises that she ask her doctor about spot treatment during pregnancy.

## Key takeaways

- Need first, brand second; offer simple choices, never claims that one brand is better.
- Compare by need using the Lotus sheet notes only.
- Choose sunscreen by skin type, group and procedure; follow the doctor after laser or peeling.
- A simple routine: wash, moisturise, protect in the morning; wash, treat if needed, moisturise in the evening.
- Add one product at a time and patch test.
- Be honest about time: weeks for acne and spots, no promise of erasing wrinkles.
- Ethical add-ons are relevant and affordable; refer at red flags.
