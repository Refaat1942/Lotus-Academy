## Learning objectives

After completing this lesson, you will be able to:

1. List the Uriage categories in the Lotus sheet: thermal water, dry skin, anti-wrinkle and eye area, brightening, oily skin and acne, sun protection, and soothing and healing.
2. State what each Uriage product on the Lotus list is for, using only the Lotus usage notes.
3. Choose between Uriage sun products for different skin types, colours and special groups.
4. Explain wrinkle and brightening products honestly, including age notes from the sheet and realistic expectations.
5. Handle eczema-related requests safely and refer when needed.
6. Flag unclear items for verification instead of guessing.

## 1. Reading the Uriage sheet

As in the Bioderma lesson, the Lotus sheet is the only source for product facts. Items marked **(verify)** have unclear wording and have been passed to the Lotus team. Where the sheet mentions eczema, inflammation or wounds, remember that the pharmacist does not diagnose: a customer with a diagnosed or suspected medical skin condition needs a doctor's guidance, and cosmetic products are support, not a replacement.

**Eczema and very dry skin: safe handling**

- Itchy, red, flaky patches that return may be eczema, but only a doctor can say so.
- Ask whether a doctor has already seen the condition and whether treatment is prescribed.
- Moisturising often supports skin care; never advise stopping a doctor's cream.
- Refer if the skin is oozing, crusting, painful, widespread, infected-looking or keeps returning.

**Hand care for housework:** use gloves, avoid very hot water and apply cream after each wash. This is general skin-care advice and can be given alongside the product.

## 4. Anti-wrinkle and eye area

| Product | What the Lotus sheet says | Who it suits and what to say |
|---|---|---|
| **Isolift visage** | Wrinkle cream for face and neck, starting from age 30 | Customer around 30 and above who wants a first anti-wrinkle step. |
| **Isoval cream** | For wrinkles (above age 40), face and neck | Customer above 40 with visible wrinkles. |
| **Isolift eye** | For wrinkles around the eye, dark circles and puffiness; the sheet notes it as safe (wording to verify) | Customer bothered by the eye area. Check the pack for any restriction; eye redness, pain or swelling goes to a doctor. |

**Counselling tips**

- Wrinkles are linked to age, sun and dryness. Daily sunscreen is the most useful anti-ageing habit.
- Dark circles have many causes (tiredness, genetics, allergy). Do not promise they will disappear.
- Be honest: creams soften the appearance over weeks; they do not erase lines.

## 5. Brightening (dark spots and darker skin)

| Product | What the Lotus sheet says | Who it suits and what to say |
|---|---|---|
| **Depiderm cream** | Lightens dark spots and darkening of sensitive areas (underarms, between the thighs), used at night | Customer with darker patches in sensitive areas. Patch test first; stop if irritation appears. |
| **Depiderm SPF 50** | Lightening and protection cream used by day | The daytime partner of Depiderm cream. Sun makes spots darker, so protection is part of the plan. |

**Counselling tips**

- Fading takes weeks to months; set expectations.
- Never recommend skin lightening for a spot that is new, growing, bleeding or changing: refer.
- Do not recommend the product on broken or irritated skin.
- In pregnancy or breastfeeding, check with the doctor before starting a lightening product, as this sheet gives no pregnancy information for it.

- Allow several weeks before judging.
- Painful, deep, widespread or scarring acne goes to a dermatologist.
- If the customer uses a prescribed acne treatment, the moisturiser can support it; ask their doctor first.

## Scenario

A customer in her late forties asks for a cream for wrinkles on her face and neck and for her sun-darkened cheek spot. The pharmacist asks about sensitivity and medicines, then suggests a wrinkle cream listed for above 40 and a daily sunscreen suited to her skin type. She says one spot is new and growing, so the pharmacist advises a dermatologist check before any lightening product. The pharmacist explains that results take weeks.

## Key takeaways

- Uriage is grouped by need: thermal water, dry skin, anti-wrinkle and eye, brightening, oily and acne, sun, soothing and healing.
- Emollient Cream is the everyday moisturiser, Emollient Extreme the stronger one for very dry skin.
- Eczema-related requests need a doctor's guidance; cosmetic products only support.
- Isolift visage is listed from age 30, Isoval above 40.
- Depiderm cream is the night brightening product, Depiderm SPF 50 the day one.
- The Hyseac line covers wash, mild acne cream, inflamed pimples and moisturising after treatment.
- Uriage mineral is listed for very sensitive skin and for pregnant and breastfeeding women.
