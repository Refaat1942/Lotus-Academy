## Learning objectives

After completing this lesson, you will be able to:

1. Describe the typical signs of dry, normal, oily, combination and sensitive skin.
2. Ask short, open questions that reveal skin type, current routine and the customer's real concern.
3. Collect safety information: pregnancy, breastfeeding, children, allergies and earlier reactions.
4. Recognise red flags that require a doctor instead of a cosmetic product.
5. Explain the basics of daily sun protection in simple language.
6. Apply patch testing and introduce new products safely.

---

## 1. Why the consultation comes before the product

In a busy Lotus branch the customer often arrives with a product name from social media or a friend: "I want the cream that cleared my cousin's skin." The same cream can suit one person and irritate another. A dermocosmetic is chosen for **the skin in front of you**, not for the name. A two-minute consultation protects the customer, prevents returns and builds trust.

Remember the pharmacist's role: **we counsel, we do not diagnose.** We match cosmetic products to skin type and need, and we send the customer to a doctor when the picture suggests a medical problem.

## 2. The five skin types

Skin type is mainly about oil (sebum) and water content. Skin *sensitivity* is a separate dimension: any type can also be sensitive.

| Type | What the customer usually says | Typical signs | Counselling direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Normal** | "My skin is fine, nothing special." | Comfortable, even, rarely shiny or tight | Gentle cleanser, light moisturiser, daily sun protection |
| **Dry** | "My skin feels tight after washing." | Tightness, rough or flaky patches, dull look, may itch | Soap-free gentle cleanser, richer cream, avoid very hot water |
| **Oily** | "My face is shiny by noon." | Shine, enlarged pores, blackheads, spots | Gentle foaming gel or wash, light oil-free moisturiser, fluid sunscreen |
| **Combination** | "My forehead and nose are oily, my cheeks are dry." | Oily T-zone, normal or dry cheeks | Gentle cleanser, light cream, adapt by zone |
| **Sensitive** | "Everything stings or turns red." | Redness, burning, tightness, reaction to many products | Few products, fragrance-free where possible, simple routine, patch test |

*Dehydrated* skin lacks water and can still look oily; a customer with shine who also feels tightness may need a lighter moisturiser, not a stronger drying product.

## 3. Questions that reveal the skin type

Use open questions first, then confirm.

- "How does your skin feel one hour after washing with nothing on it?" (tight = dry, comfortable = normal, shiny = oily)
- "By the afternoon, where does it get shiny, if anywhere?"
- "Does it burn, sting or turn red with products, sun or weather?"
- "Is this for the face, the body, the hands, the baby?"
- "What is bothering you the most today?"

**Pharmacist:** "To choose the right thing, I'd like to ask three quick questions. Is your skin tight after washing, or shiny by midday?"
**Customer:** "Tight, and it flakes around my nose."
**Pharmacist:** "Thank you, that points to dry skin. And do you get redness or stinging with creams?"

## 4. Asking about the current routine

Many skin problems come from the routine itself: harsh soap, scrubbing, too many actives at once. Ask:

1. What do you use to wash your face and body, and how often?
2. What do you put on in the morning and in the evening?
3. Are you using any medicated cream, prescribed or bought without a prescription?
4. What have you tried before, and what happened?
5. Do you use sun protection daily?

Write the answers in your mind as a short list. If the customer is already using a prescribed treatment, do not tell them to stop it; advise that cosmetic products are *added* only if the doctor's plan allows, and encourage them to ask their doctor.

## 5. Safety questions: do not skip them

| Ask about | Why | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Pregnancy or breastfeeding** | Some products and medicines are not suitable | Choose only products whose usage line says they are suitable; otherwise refer to the doctor |
| **Children** | Children's skin is delicate; some products are not for babies | Use only products presented for children; refer for any rash that is spreading, weeping or not improving |
| **Allergies** | Known allergy to a cosmetic, fragrance, medicine | Avoid the trigger; read the product's composition on the pack |
| **Earlier reactions** | Redness, burning, swelling after a product | Do not recommend the same product; explain that reactions can repeat |
| **Medicines or procedures** | Laser, peeling or other treatments change what skin tolerates | Follow the doctor's instructions |

Never promise that a product is "completely safe for everyone." Say: "It is made for this need, and we start with a small test."

## 6. Red flags: send to a doctor

A cosmetic product is not the answer when you see or hear:

- A mole or dark spot that is changing in size, colour or shape, bleeding or growing.
- A wound that does not heal, or burns that are large, deep, blistering or on the face, hands or genitals.
- Rapid swelling of the face or lips, breathing difficulty, or widespread hives (treat as an emergency).
- Painful, red, hot, swollen skin with fever, or oozing and crusting (possible infection).
- A rash in a baby that is spreading, weeping, or comes with fever or poor feeding.
- Severe, painful or scarring acne, or acne with a sudden start in adulthood.
- Itching so severe that it disturbs sleep, or patches that keep returning despite care.
- Any condition that has not improved after a reasonable trial of a suitable routine.

**Pharmacist:** "I don't want to cover this with a cream. Please show it to a dermatologist; after that, I can help with gentle skin care that fits their plan."

## 7. Sun protection basics

Sun protection is the most useful step in any routine, whatever the skin type.

- Use a sunscreen every morning on exposed skin, not only at the beach. Egypt has strong sun for most of the year.
- Apply enough, apply early, and **re-apply** after sweating, swimming or wiping, and about every two hours outdoors.
- Use a **fluid** or light texture for oily skin, a **cream** for dry skin, a **milk or lotion** for large areas of the body.
- Tinted sunscreens give colour and can replace a foundation for some customers.
- Add hat, sunglasses and shade; sunscreen is one layer of protection, not all of it.
- Babies and children need products presented for children, and shade matters more than any cream.
- Check pregnancy or breastfeeding status and sensitivity before choosing.

## 8. Patch testing and starting new products

1. Apply a small amount to the inner forearm or behind the ear.
2. Leave it for 24 hours, without washing the spot.
3. If there is redness, itching, burning or swelling, wash it off and do not use the product.
4. Introduce **one new product at a time**, with several days between additions, so the cause of any reaction is clear.
5. Start every second day for very sensitive skin if the product allows.

## Common mistakes

- Recommending the most expensive product without asking one question.
- Treating oily skin with harsh scrubbing, which can worsen irritation.
- Forgetting the safety questions when the customer is a woman of childbearing age.
- Telling the customer to stop a doctor's treatment.
- Promising results in days.

## Scenario

A mother comes in at the evening peak with her teenage daughter who has shiny skin and spots on the forehead. The mother asks for "the strongest acne cream." The pharmacist asks the daughter how her skin feels after washing, what she uses now, whether she scrubs, and whether she uses any prescribed treatment. The daughter uses a bar of strong soap three times daily and scrubs. The pharmacist explains that over-washing irritates, suggests a gentle cleanser for oily skin, a light moisturiser and a fluid sunscreen, and explains patch testing. Because there are some painful, deep spots, the pharmacist also advises seeing a dermatologist if there is no improvement after a few weeks, and offers to help with follow-up care afterwards.

## Key takeaways

- Choose for the skin in front of you; ask before you recommend.
- Skin type (dry, normal, oily, combination) and sensitivity are different questions.
- Always ask about routine, medicines, pregnancy, breastfeeding, children, allergies and earlier reactions.
- Red flags, such as changing moles, non-healing wounds or spreading baby rashes, go to a doctor.
- Daily sunscreen with correct texture and re-application is the foundation of care.
- Patch test and add one product at a time.
- Counsel, never diagnose; never ask a customer to stop a doctor's treatment.
