## Learning objectives

After completing this lesson, you will be able to:

1. Decline a request for a prescription-only medicine politely, firmly and with an alternative path
2. Handle unavailable medicines and price shock with honesty and practical options
3. De-escalate an angry customer using a clear step-by-step approach
4. Set boundaries politely and protect your own safety and dignity
5. Decide when to involve the pharmacy manager and document what happened

---

## 1. Why these conversations are hard

Saying no, delivering bad news or facing anger triggers stress in everyone. In a busy Lotus branch, the customer may be tired, in pain, worried about a child or short of money, and the pharmacist is under time pressure. The aim is not to "win" the conversation. The aim is to protect the customer's safety, follow the rules, keep the relationship and keep your own calm.

Three principles apply to every difficult conversation:

1. **Stay calm.** Slow your breathing and your speech.
2. **Show respect.** The customer is a person with a problem, not an opponent.
3. **Be firm on safety and rules, flexible on options.** Say what you cannot do, then offer what you can do.

## 2. Saying no to a prescription-only request

Some customers ask for a prescription-only medicine without a prescription, for example an antibiotic "because it worked last time". The pharmacist must follow the current rules of the Egyptian Drug Authority / Ministry of Health and the Lotus SOP. Dispensing outside the rules is not a favour; it exposes the customer to harm and the pharmacy to risk.

### A four-part "no"

1. **Acknowledge** the need: "I understand you want to feel better quickly."
2. **Say no clearly, with the reason in simple words:** "This medicine needs a doctor's prescription, because the wrong choice or dose can harm you."
3. **Offer an alternative:** "What I can do is suggest something for the symptoms, or advise you on seeing a doctor today."
4. **Close kindly:** "I will be happy to help you once you have the prescription."

> **Customer:** Just give me the antibiotic, I know it. I don't have time for a doctor.
> **Pharmacist:** I understand you are busy and want to recover fast. This one needs a prescription, because the wrong antibiotic or dose can cause harm and may not help. What I can do now is suggest something to ease your symptoms, and tell you which signs mean you should see a doctor today.

Avoid lecturing, blaming ("people like you misuse antibiotics") or giving in after the customer insists. Repeat the same message calmly: "I understand, and I am sorry, I cannot dispense this without a prescription."

## 3. Unavailable medicines

- **Be honest quickly.** Do not keep the customer waiting while you search aimlessly.
- **Offer options:** check another Lotus branch, order it for a specific date, or discuss a suitable alternative.
- **Alternatives need care.** Substituting a different active ingredient is a decision for the prescriber. Only substitute within what the rules and the SOP allow, and explain the change clearly.
- **Never blame colleagues, suppliers or the company** in front of the customer.

> **Pharmacist:** I am sorry, this item is not available in our branch today. I can check whether another Lotus branch has it, or arrange it for you as soon as it is in stock. Which would you prefer?

## 4. Price shock

Medicine prices matter to many families. A customer may react with surprise or anger when told the total.

- **Do not be defensive** and do not imply the customer is complaining without reason.
- **Acknowledge**: "I understand, the total is higher than you expected."
- **Check for correctness**: confirm the items and quantities with the customer so nothing unneeded is included.
- **Offer legitimate options** within the SOP: a different pack size if suitable, an equivalent product only if the prescriber's intent is respected and the rules allow it, an insurance or corporate card if the customer has one, or buying only the essential items today.
- **Never advise skipping or reducing a prescribed medicine to save money**; suggest speaking with the prescriber about options.

## 5. De-escalating an angry customer

Anger usually hides worry, fatigue or a feeling of not being heard. Use the sequence below.

| Step | What to do | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Calm yourself | Breathe, lower your voice, relax shoulders | |
| 2. Listen | Let the customer finish without interrupting | "Please tell me what happened." |
| 3. Acknowledge | Name the feeling and thank them | "I can see you are upset, and I am sorry for the waiting." |
| 4. Apologise for the experience | Apologise for the situation, not for what you did not do wrong | "I am sorry this has been so frustrating." |
| 5. Clarify the facts | Ask neutral questions | "May I check the order to understand?" |
| 6. Offer solutions | One or two realistic choices | "I can do this now or call the manager." |
| 7. Confirm and follow up | Agree on the next step | "I will call you before the evening." |

Do **not**: argue, raise your voice, say "calm down", use sarcasm, blame the customer, or take it personally.

> **Customer:** Your delivery is two hours late! This is unacceptable!
> **Pharmacist:** You are right to be upset, two hours is too long, and I am sorry. Let me check exactly where your order is now. May I have your name for the order?
> **Customer:** ...it is for my mother's medicine.
> **Pharmacist:** Thank you for telling me. I will call the rider now and tell you the time within five minutes.

## 6. Setting boundaries politely

A boundary protects safety and dignity. Examples include refusing abusive language, refusing to dispense outside the rules, refusing to discuss other customers, or declining inappropriate personal questions.

- **Use calm, short sentences:** "I want to help you, and I need us to talk respectfully."
- **Give one clear warning**, then act: "If the shouting continues I will have to ask my manager to join us."
- **Do not threaten, insult or touch.**
- **Your safety first.** If a customer is threatening, violent or you feel unsafe, move away, call the manager or follow the branch security SOP, and do not try to handle it alone.

## 7. When to involve the manager

Involve the pharmacy manager when:

- the customer remains abusive after one calm warning;
- the customer demands something outside the rules after you have explained twice;
- a complaint involves a possible error, injury or safety event;
- a refund, exception or compensation decision is needed that is not yours to make;
- you feel unsafe or too upset to continue professionally.

Say: "I would like my manager to help you further, one moment please." Hand over the facts briefly. Afterwards, write a short factual note of what happened according to the SOP, with no personal opinions.

## 8. Common mistakes

| Mistake | Better approach |
|---|---|
| Saying "no" without a reason or alternative | Four-part "no" |
| Giving in to pressure to avoid a scene | Repeat the polite no, involve the manager |
| Saying "calm down" | "I can see this is frustrating, let me help" |
| Blaming another colleague | "I am sorry, let me solve it" |
| Debating prices with the customer | Acknowledge, check, offer options |
| Handling a threatening customer alone | Call the manager and follow the SOP |

## Checklist: a difficult conversation

- I stayed calm and lowered my voice.
- I acknowledged the feeling.
- I gave a clear, simple reason for the no.
- I offered at least one alternative.
- I did not argue or blame.
- I involved the manager when needed.
- I documented the event briefly and factually.

## Scenario

On a hot afternoon, a customer insists on buying a strong antibiotic without a prescription for a sore throat. The queue is growing. The pharmacist says, "I understand you want to feel better quickly. This medicine needs a prescription, because the wrong choice can cause harm. I can suggest something for the throat and explain which signs mean you should see a doctor today." The customer raises his voice: "You are just making business difficult!" The pharmacist stays calm: "I am sorry for the frustration. I cannot dispense it without a prescription, but I am glad to help in another way." When the customer continues to shout, the pharmacist gives one warning and then invites the manager. The manager repeats the same message kindly, and the customer leaves with advice and a lozenge. The pharmacist writes a short note and returns to the queue, thanking the waiting customers.

## Key takeaways

- Stay calm, show respect, and be firm on safety and rules but flexible on options.
- A four-part no: acknowledge, say no with the reason, offer an alternative, close kindly.
- Be honest about unavailable medicines and offer practical options within the SOP.
- For price shock, acknowledge, check the order and offer legitimate options; never advise skipping prescribed medicines.
- De-escalate in steps: calm yourself, listen, acknowledge, apologise for the experience, clarify, offer solutions, follow up.
- Involve the manager for abuse, repeated demands outside the rules, safety events or personal safety, and document facts.
