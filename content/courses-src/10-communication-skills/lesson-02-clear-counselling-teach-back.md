## Learning objectives

After completing this lesson, you will be able to:

1. Structure a short counselling talk covering purpose, dose, timing, duration, storage and warnings
2. Replace medical jargon with plain Arabic that every customer understands
3. Apply the teach-back method to confirm understanding without embarrassing the customer
4. Write a simple reminder note or label instruction that supports correct use
5. Avoid the most common counselling mistakes in a busy Egyptian pharmacy

---

## 1. Why counselling often fails

Many customers leave the pharmacy having heard the instructions but not understood them. Studies of health communication in many countries show that people forget a large part of what they are told, especially when they are worried, in a hurry or tired. In a Lotus branch, the typical problems are speaking too fast, using terms such as "twice daily", "on an empty stomach" or "course" without explanation, and asking "Do you understand?" to which almost everyone says "yes".

The goal of counselling is not that the pharmacist has said everything. The goal is that the customer can use the medicine correctly at home.

## 2. A simple structure: PDTSW

Keep the talk short, usually under two minutes, and cover these points in order:

| Letter | Point | Example wording |
|---|---|---|
| P | Purpose | "This is for the infection in the throat." |
| D | Dose and how to take | "One tablet each time, swallowed with water." |
| T | Timing and duration | "Morning and evening, after food, for five days." |
| S | Storage | "Keep it in a cool, dry place away from the children." |
| W | Warnings | "If you get a rash or swelling, stop and come back or see a doctor." |

Only counsel on what the prescriber or the product leaflet and the Lotus SOP support. If you are unsure, check the leaflet or ask a senior colleague. Never guess a dose.

## 3. Plain Arabic instead of jargon

Use everyday words. Egyptian colloquial phrases are acceptable in the counter conversation, as long as they are clear and respectful.

| Jargon | Plain wording |
|---|---|
| "Twice daily" | "Morning and evening" |
| "Every 8 hours" | "Three times a day, about morning, afternoon and bedtime" |
| "On an empty stomach" | "An hour before food or two hours after" |
| "Oral" | "By mouth" |
| "Topical" | "On the skin only, not to swallow" |
| "Course" | "Keep taking it until the box is finished, even if you feel better" (only when the prescriber's plan says so) |
| "Contraindicated" | "Not suitable for you" |
| "Store below 25 degrees" | "Keep it away from the sun and the heat, not in the car" |

Remember the Egyptian summer: a medicine left in a car or on a sunny balcony can be damaged. Mention storage clearly, and for items that need the fridge, say so and show the customer where to place them.

## 4. Teach-back: checking understanding the right way

Teach-back means asking the customer to explain in their own words how they will use the medicine. It checks how well *you* explained, not how well they listened. The phrasing is important.

**Avoid:** "Do you understand?" or "Is it clear?"

**Use:** "I want to be sure I explained it well. Can you tell me how you will give this to your child at home?"

Steps:

1. Explain one or two key points, not everything at once.
2. Ask for teach-back in a friendly way, taking responsibility yourself.
3. Listen. If something is wrong, do not say "no, you are wrong". Say: "Thank you, let me explain that part again in a clearer way."
4. Re-explain using different words and ask again.
5. Stop when the customer can repeat the dose, timing and one warning.

### Model dialogue

> **Pharmacist:** This antibiotic is for the infection your doctor found. Take one capsule in the morning and one in the evening with water, for seven days.
> **Customer:** Okay, okay.
> **Pharmacist:** I want to make sure I explained it clearly. How will you take it tomorrow?
> **Customer:** One capsule when I wake up, one at night, and when I feel better I stop.
> **Pharmacist:** Thank you. The morning and the night is perfect. About stopping, the doctor chose seven days, so please finish the seven days unless the doctor tells you otherwise. Shall I write it on the box?
> **Customer:** Yes please.

## 5. Written reminders

Memory fades. Support it:

- Write the dose and timing on the box in large, clear letters, using the Lotus label if available, following the SOP.
- Use simple symbols: a sun for morning, a moon for night, if the branch approves them.
- For several medicines, write a small daily table: medicine, morning, afternoon, night.
- Ask the customer to take a photo of the note on their phone, if they wish.
- Never write a dose that differs from the prescription or the leaflet.

## 6. Counselling about specific points

- **Dose:** Mention the measuring tool for liquids. A household spoon varies; use the cup or syringe that comes with the product.
- **Timing with food:** Explain only what the leaflet or prescriber says. If unsure, say "I will check this to be sure."
- **Missed dose:** Give guidance from the leaflet; if it is unclear, advise the customer to ask the prescriber.
- **Duration:** Tell the customer when to expect improvement in general terms and when to return or see a doctor if there is none.
- **Interactions and other medicines:** Always ask what else the customer takes, including herbs and supplements.

## 7. Common mistakes

| Mistake | Better approach |
|---|---|
| Reading the leaflet aloud | Pick the two or three points that matter |
| Using terms such as "bid", "prn" or "contraindicated" | Use everyday Arabic |
| "Do you understand?" | Teach-back |
| Counselling while the customer is on the phone | Wait for their attention |
| Giving all information in one breath | Chunk it and pause |
| Writing instructions that are hard to read | Print clearly, large letters |

## Checklist: a good counselling talk

- I confirmed who will take the medicine.
- I covered purpose, dose, timing, storage and one warning.
- I used plain words and measuring tools.
- I used teach-back and re-explained when needed.
- I wrote the key instructions down.
- I told the customer when to return or see a doctor.

## Scenario

A father collects antibiotic syrup for his daughter. The bottle needs shaking and is dosed with a syringe. The pharmacist shows the syringe, shakes the bottle and says, "Fill up to this line, twice a day, morning and evening." He answers "fine". The pharmacist asks, "Please show me how much you will give her tomorrow morning." The father fills to the wrong line. The pharmacist smiles: "Thank you, that helps me. Let us do it together, up to this line." Then the pharmacist writes the amount and times on the box, and explains to keep the bottle in a cool place and to come back if she vomits or gets a rash. A thirty-second demonstration prevented an under-dose for a week.

## Key takeaways

- Counselling succeeds only when the customer can use the medicine correctly at home.
- Cover purpose, dose, timing, storage and one warning in under two minutes.
- Replace jargon with plain Arabic and show measuring tools.
- Use teach-back: "Can you tell me how you will take it?", never "Do you understand?"
- If the customer is wrong, take responsibility and re-explain in different words.
- Write key instructions down and never guess a dose.
