## 1. How SSRIs and SNRIs work

Depression and anxiety disorders are common, treatable medical conditions. Many patients feel ashamed to buy their medicines, so a discreet, respectful counter manner matters as much as the clinical advice.

**SSRIs** (selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors) block the reuptake of serotonin into nerve endings, increasing its availability in the brain. **SNRIs** (serotonin and noradrenaline reuptake inhibitors) block the reuptake of both serotonin and noradrenaline. The change in receptor activity that relieves symptoms develops gradually, which is why the benefit is delayed while side effects can appear in the first days.

Antidepressants are prescription medicines started and adjusted by a doctor, usually a psychiatrist or a physician experienced in mental health. Dispense them according to the current rules of the Egyptian Drug Authority / Ministry of Health and the Lotus SOP.

## 2. SSRIs in Egypt

| Generic | Brand | Dose |
|---|---|---|
| Escitalopram | Cipralex | 10-20mg OD |
| Sertraline | Lustral | 50-200mg OD |
| Paroxetine | Seroxat | 20mg OD |
| Fluoxetine | Prozac | 20mg OD |

Escitalopram and sertraline are often chosen first because they are generally well tolerated. Fluoxetine has a very long half-life, so missed doses and stopping cause fewer withdrawal symptoms. Paroxetine has a short half-life and is the SSRI most associated with discontinuation symptoms; it is also generally **avoided in pregnancy** and when planning pregnancy because of a possible risk of heart malformations in the baby.

## 3. SNRIs

Venlafaxine (Efexor) and duloxetine (Cymbalta) are SNRIs. Besides depression and anxiety, duloxetine has **pain indications**, such as painful diabetic neuropathy, which links this lesson to the adjuvant analgesics in Lesson 4.1. Venlafaxine can raise blood pressure at higher doses, and both can cause marked discontinuation symptoms.

## 4. Counselling: onset, side effects and safety

**Counseling (Arabic):** "مضاد الاكتئاب محتاج 2-4 أسابيع. متوقفوش فجأة. لو أفكار سيئة تزيد، كلم الدكتور فوراً."

- **Onset:** some improvement usually starts after **2–4 weeks**, and full benefit may take longer. Patients often stop in week one because "it is not working" — set this expectation clearly.
- **Early side effects:** nausea, headache, upset stomach, sleep changes, sweating and sometimes **increased anxiety or restlessness in the first weeks** (early activation). These often settle; taking the dose with food may help nausea.
- **Other side effects to know:** sexual side effects, low sodium (especially in the elderly, causing confusion or falls), and an **increased bleeding risk**, particularly with NSAIDs, aspirin or anticoagulants.
- **Suicidal thoughts:** in the early weeks, and especially in young people, suicidal thoughts can increase. Any worsening mood or thoughts of self-harm mean **contacting the doctor immediately**, or emergency services if there is immediate danger.
- **Duration:** treatment usually continues for months after the patient feels well; the doctor decides when and how to stop.

## 5. Discontinuation syndrome

Stopping an SSRI or SNRI suddenly, or missing several doses, can cause **discontinuation syndrome**: dizziness, "electric shock" sensations, irritability, anxiety, flu-like symptoms, vivid dreams and nausea. It is not addiction, but it is unpleasant and can be mistaken for relapse. The solution is a **gradual taper** planned by the doctor.

## 6. Interactions: serotonin syndrome

Combining serotonergic drugs can cause **serotonin syndrome**: agitation, confusion, sweating, fever, tremor, muscle twitching, fast heart rate and diarrhoea. Severe cases are an emergency. High-risk combinations include an SSRI or SNRI with **tramadol**, triptans, other antidepressants, MAOIs, linezolid or the herbal remedy hypericum. When a patient on Cipralex brings a tramadol prescription, the pharmacist contacts the prescriber or advises the patient to check with the doctor before supplying, according to the SOP.

## Scenario

> Stopped Seroxat after 3 days — dizziness, electric shocks. Explain discontinuation; gradual taper needed.

A woman in her thirties comes in feeling dizzy, with "electric shocks" in her head. She has taken Seroxat for several months and stopped it three days ago because she felt better and "did not want to become addicted".

**Model approach:** The pharmacist explains in a calm, private voice that these symptoms are a known effect of stopping paroxetine suddenly, that it is not addiction or madness, and that it is usually managed by restarting and reducing slowly under medical guidance. The pharmacist advises her to contact her doctor today, asks gently whether her mood is low or whether she has any thoughts of harming herself, and gives emergency advice if so. The pharmacist also asks whether pregnancy is possible, because paroxetine is generally avoided in pregnancy and the doctor should know.

## Key takeaways

- Cipralex (escitalopram) is widely used in Egypt; escitalopram and sertraline are common first choices.
- A 2–4 week onset of effect is essential counselling; early anxiety can occur.
- Taper SSRIs and SNRIs gradually — never stop abruptly.
- SSRI + tramadol carries a risk of serotonin syndrome.
- Seroxat (paroxetine) is generally avoided in pregnancy; refer, do not stop abruptly.
- Cymbalta (duloxetine) treats both depression and some pain conditions.
- Worsening mood or suicidal thoughts need immediate medical contact.
