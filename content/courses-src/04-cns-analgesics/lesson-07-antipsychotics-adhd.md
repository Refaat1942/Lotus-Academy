## 1. Antipsychotics: typical and atypical

Antipsychotics treat schizophrenia, bipolar disorder and other psychotic conditions, and are sometimes used for severe agitation. They mainly **block dopamine D2 receptors**; the atypical agents also act on serotonin receptors.

- **Typical (first-generation)** antipsychotics such as haloperidol (Haldol) block D2 strongly and have a **high risk of EPS**.
- **Atypical (second-generation)** antipsychotics such as risperidone, olanzapine, quetiapine and aripiprazole have a lower EPS risk overall but a higher risk of **metabolic effects**: weight gain, raised blood sugar and diabetes, and raised cholesterol.

| Drug | Brand | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Risperidone | Risperdal | EPS, prolactin |
| Olanzapine | Zyprexa | Weight, metabolic |
| Quetiapine | Seroquel | Sedation |
| Aripiprazole | Abilify | Less metabolic |
| Haloperidol | Haldol | High EPS |

**Extrapyramidal side effects (EPS)** include acute dystonia (sudden muscle spasm of the neck, jaw or eyes, frightening but treatable), parkinsonism (stiffness, tremor, slow movement), akathisia (severe inner restlessness) and, after long use, tardive dyskinesia (involuntary movements of the mouth and tongue). Raised **prolactin** with risperidone can cause breast changes, milk secretion and menstrual problems.

**Clozapine** is reserved for treatment-resistant schizophrenia because it can cause a severe fall in white blood cells (agranulocytosis). It is only supplied with regular **white blood cell monitoring** according to the prescriber's programme. Any sore throat, fever or mouth ulcers in a clozapine patient need urgent medical review.

**Red flags for any antipsychotic:** high fever with muscle rigidity and confusion (possible neuroleptic malignant syndrome — emergency), fainting or palpitations, severe sedation, or signs of very high blood sugar (thirst, frequent urination).

## 2. Counselling patients on antipsychotics

- **Adherence:** relapse is common when doses are missed. Long-term treatment is usual.
- **Never stop abruptly**; the doctor plans any reduction as a **gradual taper** to avoid relapse and withdrawal effects.
- **Metabolic monitoring:** weight, waist, blood sugar and lipids should be checked regularly as the doctor arranges. Advise healthy meals, fewer sugary drinks and regular activity from the start.
- Sedation and dizziness: rise slowly from lying or sitting; avoid alcohol; do not drive if drowsy.
- Families are often the main carers. Respect privacy, avoid stigmatising words, and involve them when the patient agrees.

## 3. ADHD medications

ADHD (attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder) is diagnosed and treated by a specialist. Main medicines:

- **Methylphenidate — Ritalin (immediate release) and Concerta (extended release):** a **stimulant** that increases dopamine and noradrenaline. It is a **controlled medicine** because stimulants can be misused (for "studying" or weight loss) and diverted. Dispense strictly according to the current rules of the Egyptian Drug Authority / Ministry of Health and the Lotus SOP.
- **Atomoxetine — Strattera:** a **non-stimulant** (noradrenaline reuptake inhibitor), with no stimulant misuse potential; its full effect takes several weeks.

**Counselling for stimulants:** give the dose **in the morning** (a second immediate-release dose, if prescribed, is usually taken around midday — never late in the day) because evening doses cause **insomnia**. Common effects are reduced appetite, weight loss, headache, stomach ache and raised heart rate or blood pressure. Give with or after breakfast if appetite is a problem; the doctor monitors growth, weight and blood pressure. Store securely and never share with siblings or classmates.

**Counseling (Arabic):** "ريتالين صبح مش بالليل. زيبريكسا ممكن تزود الوزن — راقب السكر."

## Scenario

> Zyprexa 8kg weight gain — metabolic monitoring, psychiatrist review.

The mother of a young man with schizophrenia collects his Zyprexa and says he has gained about 8 kg in three months, is always hungry and wants to stop the medicine "because it is ruining his body".

**Model approach:** The pharmacist acknowledges the concern and explains that weight gain is a known effect of olanzapine. The key messages: **do not stop the medicine suddenly**, because relapse can be serious; book a review with the psychiatrist soon to check weight, blood sugar and cholesterol and to discuss options; and in the meantime support healthy eating, water instead of sugary drinks, and daily walking. If he has excessive thirst or frequent urination, he needs a blood sugar check promptly. The pharmacist does not suggest switching drugs; the psychiatrist decides.

## Key takeaways

- Atypical antipsychotics need metabolic monitoring: weight, blood sugar and lipids.
- Risperdal (risperidone) and Zyprexa (olanzapine) are commonly prescribed.
- Ritalin (methylphenidate) is a controlled stimulant, taken in the morning.
- Never stop an antipsychotic abruptly; the doctor tapers gradually.
- Clozapine needs regular white blood cell monitoring.
- Abilify (aripiprazole) is generally more metabolically friendly.
- Haloperidol carries a high EPS risk; high fever with rigidity is an emergency.
