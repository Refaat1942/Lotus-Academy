## Course description

This course is for pharmacists and pharmacy staff in Lotus pharmacies in Egypt who dispense and counsel on painkillers and medicines that act on the central nervous system. It covers pain management, antidepressants, anxiolytics and hypnotics, antiepileptics, antipsychotics and ADHD medicines, and substance misuse awareness, using the brands that customers ask for every day at the counter.

After the course, staff will choose safe step 1 analgesics, recognise pain that needs urgent referral, counsel on NSAID and paracetamol risks, and handle controlled medicines such as tramadol, benzodiazepines and methylphenidate according to the current rules of the Egyptian Drug Authority / Ministry of Health and the Lotus SOP. They will also support patients and families living with depression, epilepsy and psychotic illness with clear, respectful and non-judgemental counselling.

The course has eight lessons of about 90 minutes each. Every lesson includes mechanisms in simple language, Egyptian brand tables, Arabic counselling phrases, red flags and an Egyptian-pharmacy scenario, and ends with a 10-question quiz. A 20-question final exam covers the whole course. Pharmacists counsel and refer; they do not diagnose or change prescribed treatment.

## Learning outcomes

1. Apply the **WHO analgesic ladder** with Egyptian OTC and prescription brands, and recognise red-flag pain that needs referral
2. Counsel on **NSAID GI, renal and cardiovascular risks** and the paracetamol maximum dose — Brufen, Voltaren, Cetal
3. Understand **tramadol scheduling** and the misuse epidemic in Egypt, and dispense opioids according to EDA/MOH rules and the Lotus SOP
4. Identify **SSRI/SNRI** brands — Cipralex, Seroxat and Egyptian generics — and counsel on onset, discontinuation and serotonin syndrome
5. Manage **antiepileptic** counselling — Tegretol, Depakine, Keppra — including adherence, interactions and valproate in pregnancy
6. Counsel safely on **benzodiazepines, Z-drugs, antipsychotics and ADHD medicines**, including dependence, falls and metabolic monitoring
7. Recognise **prescription-medicine misuse**, refuse and document appropriately, and refer people and families to addiction treatment services
