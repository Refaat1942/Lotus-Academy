## 1. How antiepileptic drugs work

Epilepsy is a tendency to have repeated seizures caused by abnormal, excessive electrical activity in the brain. Antiepileptic drugs do not cure epilepsy; they **reduce the chance of seizures** as long as they are taken regularly. They work in a few main ways:

- **Blocking sodium channels**, which stabilises nerve membranes: carbamazepine, phenytoin, lamotrigine.
- **Enhancing GABA** (calming) activity: valproate (partly), benzodiazepines such as clonazepam.
- **Binding to the synaptic vesicle protein SV2A**, which reduces neurotransmitter release: levetiracetam.
- Valproate has **several mechanisms**, which is why it covers many seizure types.

Some AEDs are also used for other conditions, for example carbamazepine for trigeminal neuralgia, valproate and lamotrigine in bipolar disorder, and pregabalin and gabapentin for neuropathic pain. Ask about the indication before counselling.

## 2. Major AEDs in Egypt

| Drug | Brand | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| Carbamazepine | Tegretol | Enzyme inducer |
| Valproate | Depakine | Teratogenic |
| Levetiracetam | Keppra | Mood changes |
| Lamotrigine | Lamictal | Rash — slow titration |
| Phenytoin | Epilantin | Zero-order kinetics |

- **Carbamazepine (Tegretol)** is a strong **CYP enzyme inducer**. It lowers the levels of many medicines, including **hormonal contraceptives** (risk of contraceptive failure), warfarin and some other AEDs. It can also cause low sodium, dizziness and double vision, and rarely serious skin reactions; a new rash or fever needs urgent medical review.
- **Valproate (Depakine)** is effective but **highly teratogenic**: it can cause birth defects and developmental problems in the child. It should not be used in girls and women who could become pregnant unless the specialist decides there is no suitable alternative and pregnancy prevention is in place. It can also cause weight gain, tremor, hair loss and, rarely, liver or pancreas problems.
- **Levetiracetam (Keppra)** has few drug interactions and is increasingly used first-line. It can cause **mood changes**, irritability or low mood; families should report them.
- **Lamotrigine (Lamictal)** must be started with **slow dose increases** to reduce the risk of serious rash. Valproate raises lamotrigine levels, so the combination needs special dosing by the doctor.
- **Phenytoin** has **zero-order (saturable) kinetics**: above a certain level, a small dose increase can cause a large rise in blood level and toxicity (unsteadiness, slurred speech, abnormal eye movements). Doses are changed only by the doctor, often with blood level monitoring.

## 3. Counselling: adherence is everything

**Counseling (Arabic):** "مضاد الصرع بنظام — أي جرعة فائتة خطر. ديباكين ممنوع في الحمل."

- Take the medicine **at the same times every day**; use phone alarms or a pill box.
- **Missed doses raise the risk of seizures.** Follow the leaflet or doctor's advice on what to do if a dose is missed; never take a double dose without advice.
- **Never stop an AED suddenly.** Abrupt withdrawal can trigger severe repeated seizures, including **status epilepticus**, a medical emergency.
- Do not switch between brands or generics without discussing it with the doctor and pharmacist, as some patients are sensitive to small differences.
- Check every new medicine, herbal product or contraceptive with the pharmacist for interactions.
- Women of childbearing age should discuss contraception and pregnancy plans with the specialist **before** pregnancy.
- Seek urgent help for a seizure lasting more than five minutes, repeated seizures without recovery, or a seizure with injury or breathing difficulty.

## Scenario

> Pregnant on Depakine — urgent specialist referral, do not stop abruptly.

A young woman collecting her monthly Depakine tells the pharmacist, with a smile, that she has just found out she is pregnant.

**Model approach:** The pharmacist congratulates her calmly and, in private, explains that Depakine needs urgent review by her neurologist and her obstetrician because it can affect the baby. The pharmacist is very clear: **do not stop the medicine yourself**, because a sudden stop could cause serious seizures that are dangerous for both mother and baby. The pharmacist supplies her prescribed medicine, helps her arrange an urgent appointment, and advises her to ask the doctor about folic acid. The pharmacist does not suggest a replacement drug; the specialist decides, and options such as levetiracetam or lamotrigine are often considered.

## Key takeaways

- Tegretol (carbamazepine) induces CYP enzymes and can make hormonal contraceptives fail.
- Depakine (valproate) is highly teratogenic and is avoided in pregnancy unless a specialist decides otherwise.
- Keppra (levetiracetam) is increasingly first-line, with few interactions.
- Never stop an AED abruptly; it can cause status epilepticus.
- Adherence prevents seizures; missed doses are dangerous.
- Lamictal (lamotrigine) needs slow titration to reduce rash risk.
- Phenytoin has zero-order kinetics; small dose changes can cause toxicity.
