## 1. How benzodiazepines work

Benzodiazepines **enhance the effect of GABA at the GABA-A receptor**. GABA is the brain's main calming (inhibitory) messenger, so more GABA activity means reduced anxiety, sedation, muscle relaxation and an anticonvulsant effect. They work quickly, which makes them helpful for short-term crises, but the same properties cause their problems:

- **Tolerance and dependence** can develop within a few weeks of regular use. Stopping suddenly after prolonged use can cause rebound anxiety, insomnia, tremor and, in severe cases, seizures.
- **Sedation, poor concentration and memory problems**, with a risk to driving and work safety.
- **Falls and fractures** in the elderly, plus confusion that may be mistaken for dementia.
- **Dangerous breathing depression** when combined with **alcohol**, opioids (including tramadol), pregabalin or other sedatives.
- **Misuse and diversion**, which is why they are controlled medicines in Egypt (see Lesson 4.8).

## 2. Benzodiazepines in Egypt

| Drug | Brand | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Bromazepam | Lexotanil | Common Egypt |
| Alprazolam | Xanax | High dependence |
| Lorazepam | Tavor | No active metabolites |
| Diazepam | Valium | Long acting |
| Clonazepam | Rivotril | Epilepsy/panic |

Short-acting agents such as alprazolam produce faster, stronger "highs and lows" and carry a high dependence risk. Diazepam and its metabolites last a long time, so they accumulate, particularly in the elderly. Lorazepam is metabolised without active metabolites, which can be useful in liver disease under medical supervision. Clonazepam is used in some epilepsies and panic disorder.

## 3. Z-drugs

**Stilnox (zolpidem)** and **Imovane (zopiclone)** act on the same GABA-A receptor complex and are used for **short-term insomnia**. They are not "safe sleeping pills": they also cause dependence, next-morning drowsiness, falls, and rarely complex sleep behaviours such as sleep-walking or driving while not fully awake. Zopiclone often causes a bitter taste.

Before any hypnotic, good counselling explores the cause of insomnia (pain, depression, caffeine, screen use, shift work, breathing problems at night) and **sleep hygiene**: a fixed wake-up time, no caffeine after the afternoon, a dark quiet bedroom, and no screens in bed.

## 4. Controlled-medicine rules

Benzodiazepines and Z-drugs are prescription and controlled medicines. Dispensing must follow the **current rules of the Egyptian Drug Authority / Ministry of Health and the Lotus SOP**: verify the prescription, respect the prescribed quantity, record the supply as required, and refuse early refills or prescriptions that look altered. Never dispense "a few tablets to help him sleep" without a valid prescription, even to a known customer.

## 5. Counselling

**Counseling (Arabic, corrected):** "المنومات تسبب اعتماد. خد المنوم وأنت داخل تنام على طول، ولما يكون قدامك 7–8 ساعات نوم. ممنوع الكحول. أقصر مدة ممكن."

- Use for the **shortest possible time**; hypnotics are generally intended for short courses of about 2–4 weeks at most, as the prescriber directs.
- Take a Z-drug **immediately before going to bed**, not earlier in the evening, and only when a full night's sleep is possible.
- **No alcohol**, and no other sedatives, opioids or pregabalin unless the doctor knows.
- Do not drive or operate machinery if drowsy, including the next morning.
- **Do not stop suddenly** after weeks or months of use; the doctor will plan a gradual taper.
- Keep tablets locked away from children and teenagers.

## Scenario

> Elderly early Lexotanil refill third time — refer prescriber, fall risk, dependence concern.

The daughter of a woman in her late seventies asks for another box of Lexotanil. The branch record shows this is the third early request this period. The daughter mentions her mother "fell in the bathroom last week" and seems "a bit confused at times".

**Model approach:** The pharmacist does not supply early. Calmly and kindly, the pharmacist explains that the medicine can cause unsteadiness and confusion in older people, that using more than prescribed increases dependence, and that the fall is an important warning sign. The pharmacist advises the family not to stop the medicine suddenly, recommends an urgent review with the prescriber, and, with the family's agreement, contacts the prescriber according to the SOP. The pharmacist documents the request and informs the pharmacy manager.

## Key takeaways

- Benzodiazepines are controlled medicines in Egypt; a valid prescription is required.
- Lexotanil (bromazepam) is commonly prescribed; Xanax (alprazolam) has a high dependence risk.
- Stilnox (zolpidem) is for short-term insomnia only, taken immediately before bed.
- Alcohol plus a benzodiazepine is a dangerous combination.
- Elderly patients are at high risk of falls and confusion.
- Hypnotic courses should be short, usually no more than 2–4 weeks.
- Taper gradually after prolonged use; never stop suddenly.
