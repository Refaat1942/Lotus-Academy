## 1. How these medicines work

**NSAIDs** (non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs) block the cyclo-oxygenase enzymes COX-1 and COX-2, which make prostaglandins. Less prostaglandin means less pain, less fever and less inflammation. The problem is that prostaglandins also protect the stomach lining, keep blood flowing to the kidneys and help balance blood pressure. This explains the three main NSAID risks: **stomach** (indigestion, ulcers, bleeding), **kidney** (reduced kidney function, fluid retention) and **cardiovascular** (raised blood pressure, and with some agents at higher doses, an increased risk of heart attack and stroke).

**Paracetamol** relieves pain and lowers fever, mainly through effects in the central nervous system. It has little anti-inflammatory action, so it does not reduce swelling, but it also does not irritate the stomach or raise blood pressure in the way NSAIDs do. Its main danger is the **liver in overdose**.

## 2. Paracetamol in the Egyptian market

| Brand | Manufacturer | Strength | Max dose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cetal | EIPICO | 500mg | 4g/day |
| Panadol | GSK (now Haleon) | 500mg | 4g/day |
| Paramol | Pharco | 500mg | 4g/day |

For a healthy adult, the usual maximum is **4 g in 24 hours** (eight 500 mg tablets), with at least four hours between doses. Key safety points:

- **Hepatotoxicity in overdose.** Liver damage may not cause symptoms for the first day, so a person who has taken too much must be sent to emergency **even if they feel well**.
- **Hidden paracetamol:** cold and flu sachets, combination analgesics and some prescription products also contain paracetamol. Always ask what else the patient is taking.
- **A lower limit may be needed** in people with liver disease, regular heavy alcohol use, low body weight or poor nutrition, and in frail elderly people. Refer them to the doctor or pharmacist-in-charge for a suitable dose.
- **Children:** dose by weight, using the paediatric product and its measuring device, as directed in the leaflet.
- **Pregnancy:** at therapeutic doses, paracetamol is generally considered the preferred analgesic in pregnancy. Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest time, and advise her to inform her doctor.

## 3. NSAIDs

| Generic | Brand | Form | Key risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ibuprofen | Brufen | Tabs, gel | GI, renal, ↑BP |
| Diclofenac | Voltaren, Cataflam | Tabs, gel | CV risk oral |
| Naproxen | Naproxen | Tabs | Longer acting |
| Ketoprofen | Ketofan | Gel | Topical safer |

**Contraindications:** active peptic ulcer or previous GI bleeding, severe kidney disease (CKD), severe heart failure, the **third trimester of pregnancy**, and asthma or allergy triggered by aspirin or other NSAIDs ("aspirin asthma").

**Use with extra caution and usually refer:** elderly patients, people with hypertension, heart disease, kidney disease or diabetes, and anyone taking anticoagulants (such as warfarin), antiplatelets (such as aspirin or clopidogrel), corticosteroids, SSRIs, lithium, diuretics, ACE inhibitors or ARBs.

**Comparing risks.** Oral diclofenac carries a **higher cardiovascular risk** than naproxen or low-dose ibuprofen, so it is a poor choice for patients with heart disease. Naproxen lasts longer, so it is taken less often. **Topical NSAIDs** (ibuprofen gel, diclofenac gel, ketoprofen gel) reach much lower blood levels, so they are a lower-risk option for localised muscle and joint pain, although they are still avoided in late pregnancy and on broken skin.

**The blood pressure interaction.** Hypertension is very common among Lotus customers. NSAIDs **increase blood pressure** and weaken the effect of antihypertensives. Combined with an ACE inhibitor or ARB (such as valsartan) and especially with a diuretic as well, they can cause acute kidney injury, particularly in dehydrated or elderly patients.

## 4. Counselling at the counter

**Counseling (Arabic):** "الباراسيتامول متعديش 8 أقراص في اليوم. البروفين والفولتارين مع الأكل. لو عندك ضغط أو كلى، اسأل الدكتور."

- Take oral NSAIDs **with or after food** to reduce stomach upset, at the lowest effective dose for the shortest time (a few days for OTC use).
- Never take two NSAIDs together (for example Brufen plus Cataflam), and do not add an NSAID to a prescribed one.
- Stop and seek medical help for black stools, vomiting blood, severe stomach pain, swelling of the legs, reduced urine, or wheezing.
- Ask before every NSAID sale: "Do you have blood pressure, kidney, heart or stomach problems? Are you pregnant? Do you take a blood thinner?"

## Scenario

> Patient on Tareg requests Voltaren for back pain. Counsel NSAID worsens BP/renal function; suggest paracetamol, topical gel, physician review.

A man in his sixties, taking Tareg (valsartan) and a water pill for blood pressure, asks for Voltaren tablets for back pain after moving furniture.

**Model approach:**

Pharmacist: "With your blood pressure tablets, Voltaren tablets can raise your pressure and put strain on your kidneys. For this kind of muscle pain, I suggest paracetamol at the correct dose and a diclofenac gel on the painful area. If the pain is not better in a few days, or if you get pain down the leg or any weakness, please see your doctor."

The pharmacist checks he has no other paracetamol at home, explains the maximum daily dose, and reminds him to keep taking his blood pressure medicines.

## Key takeaways

- Cetal (paracetamol) maximum 4 g/day in healthy adults; overdose causes hepatotoxicity, and a lower limit applies in some patients.
- Take Brufen and Voltaren with food, at the lowest dose for the shortest time.
- NSAIDs raise blood pressure — an important interaction in Egypt, where hypertension is common.
- Topical diclofenac and other gels have lower systemic risk.
- Avoid NSAIDs in the third trimester of pregnancy.
- Oral diclofenac carries a higher cardiovascular risk than naproxen.
